## 1. What FATF is, how it is governed, what the 40 Recommendations and 11 Immediate Outcomes cover, and what changed recently

### Takeaway
FATF is an intergovernmental standard-setter: about 38 member jurisdictions plus the European Commission and the GCC (Russia suspended since February 2023). It runs a one-year rotating presidency, and its Plenary decides by consensus three times a year. Its 40 Recommendations, assessed through 11 Immediate Outcomes, were heavily revised between 2022 and 2026: beneficial ownership (R.24 in March 2022, R.25 in February 2023), asset recovery (R.4/R.38 in October 2023), financial inclusion and proportionality (R.1 in February 2025), payment transparency (R.16 in June 2025, in force by end-2030), and humanitarian safeguards in R.6 (June 2026).

### Cited Findings
**Membership and governance**
- On 22–24 February 2023 the FATF Plenary suspended Russia's membership because of the war against Ukraine, the first such step in FATF's 34-year history. Russia must still implement the standards and pay its dues, and it remains a member of the Eurasian Group (EAG) — [FATF Plenary outcomes Feb 2023](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-february-2023.html); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/pc-fatf-suspends-russia-membership-adds-nigeria-south-africa-greylist)
- Indonesia, an observer since June 2018, became a full member at the October 2023 Plenary — [FATF outcomes Oct 2023](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-october-2023.html) (via search summary)
- In March 2023, counting Russia, FATF had 39 members: 37 jurisdictions plus the European Commission and the Gulf Cooperation Council. Adding Indonesia gives 38 jurisdictions + 2 regional organisations (this is arithmetic, not an official count) — [Vixio / IELR summaries](https://ielr.com/node/8804)
- "Global Network" = FATF + 9 FSRBs, covering "more than 200 jurisdictions". An earlier FATF speech gave 198 jurisdictions, all committed to the standards and to peer assessment. One trade outlet says 206 — [FATF, fight illicit finance](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/fight-illicit-finance.html); [FATF speech on Global Network](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/fatf-gafi/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/Moneyval-speech-importance-of-fatf-global-network.html); [CFATF Global Network](https://cfatf-gafic.org/global-network/)
- Presidency: Elisa de Anda Madrazo (Mexico) held the presidency until June 2026. The 17–19 June 2026 Plenary was the "sixth and final" under Mexico. It approved the priorities of the incoming **UK presidency** (Giles Thompson, per secondary reports) and appointed **Vivek Aggarwal (India)** as Vice-President for July 2026–June 2027 — [FATF outcomes June 2026](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html); [ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/); [US Treasury readout](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0547)
- Plenary rhythm: three plenaries a year (February, June, October). Plenaries announced for 2027: 22–26 Feb, 21–25 June, and 18–22 Oct, all in Paris — [FATF event calendar](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/calendars/events.html)
- In August 2026 FATF added Barbados, Namibia and Thailand to its "Guest Initiative", which lets non-members attend — [AML Intelligence, Aug 2026](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2026/08/news-fatf-adds-barbados-namibia-and-thailand-to-its-guest-initiative/)

**Recent standard revisions (dated)**
- **R.24, legal persons (adopted 4 March 2022).** Countries must hold adequate, accurate and up-to-date beneficial ownership (BO) information. They must use a "multi-pronged" combination of collection mechanisms, in practice a registry or alternative mechanism. New bearer shares and bearer share warrants are prohibited, and controls on nominees are stronger. Guidance followed on 10 March 2023 — [FATF R.24 public statement](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/R24-statement-march-2022.html); [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2022/03/fatf-public-statement-on-revisions-to-recommendation-24/)
- **R.25, legal arrangements and trusts (agreed at the 22–24 Feb 2023 Plenary)**, after an October 2022 consultation — [FATF outcomes Feb 2023](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-february-2023.html)
- **R.4/R.38, asset recovery (October 2023 Plenary).** Countries must prioritise asset recovery, establish **non-conviction-based (NCB) confiscation** regimes and have a power to suspend transactions. R.38 now contains concrete duties to cooperate internationally, including enforcing foreign NCB orders. The pre-2023 text only required NCB cooperation at minimum when the perpetrator was dead, had fled, was absent or was unknown. The Basel Institute notes the standards were further restructured in October 2025 — [FATF outcomes Oct 2023](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-october-2023.html); [Basel Institute blog](https://baselgovernance.org/blog/fatf-seeks-change-landscape-international-asset-recovery-what-means-latin-america); [Basel Policy Brief 16 (2026)](https://baselgovernance.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/Policy-Brief-16_Enforcing-foreign-NCBF-orders.pdf)
- **Financial inclusion / proportionality (standards updated 25 Feb 2025).** The changes revised R.1 and INR.1, with consequential edits to INR.10, INR.15 and the Glossary. "Commensurate" is replaced throughout by "**proportionate**". Non-face-to-face relationships are clarified as a possible lower-risk example. The aim is more use of simplified measures, framed around about **1.4 billion unbanked adults**. A guidance consultation closed 4 April 2025 — [FATF, Feb 2025 update](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-standards-promote-financial-conclusion-feb-2025.html); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/revised-fatf-aml-and-ctf-standards-to-better-prom)
- **R.16, "Payment transparency" (published 18 June 2025).** The scope widens from AML/CFT to predicate offences such as fraud. The payment chain starts at the FI that receives the customer instruction. Card purchases of goods and services stay exempt, with clarified scope. Account numbers may not be used to disguise the country of the account-holding FI (this targets virtual IBANs). The aim is to support the G20 cross-border payments roadmap. **Implementation is due by end-2030.** After the October 2025 Plenary, FATF published **Annex IV to the Methodology** on how revised R.16 will be assessed. A secondary summary says cross-border transfers above USD/EUR 1,000 must carry the originator's and beneficiary's names and account numbers (or a unique transaction reference), plus address or date of birth in some cases. This was not verified against the FATF text — [FATF R.16 update June 2025](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-Recommendation-16-payment-transparency-june-2025.html); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/fatf-updates-standards-on-payment-transparency); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2025/06/fatf-releases-revisions-to-international-standard-for-payments-transparency/)
- **June 2026:** R.6 (targeted financial sanctions for TF) was strengthened to protect humanitarian assistance from the unintended effects of sanctions. A public consultation opened on guidance for the revised R.16 — [FATF outcomes June 2026](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html); [ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/)
- 11 Immediate Outcomes (IOs) are the "key goals an effective AML/CFT system should achieve". Effectiveness is rated High / Substantial / Moderate / Low — [FATF consolidated ratings page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Assessment-ratings.html); [FATF methodology](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Fatf-methodology.html)

### Inferences
- The 2022–2025 revisions together move FATF from "paper rules" toward (a) actually knowing who owns entities (R.24/25), (b) getting money back (R.4/38), (c) fraud-relevant payment data (R.16), and (d) not de-risking the poor (R.1). For a training twin, R.16 data fields and BO registry checks are the most concrete rule hooks.
- From general knowledge, not fetched this session: the 40 Recommendations are grouped into 7 blocks: A policies and coordination (R.1–2); B ML and confiscation (R.3–4); C TF and PF (R.5–8); D preventive measures (R.9–23); E transparency and BO (R.24–25); F powers of authorities (R.26–35); G international cooperation (R.36–40). The 11 IOs are: IO.1 risk/policy, IO.2 international cooperation, IO.3 supervision, IO.4 preventive measures, IO.5 legal persons/arrangements, IO.6 financial intelligence, IO.7 ML investigation/prosecution, IO.8 confiscation, IO.9 TF investigation, IO.10 TF preventive measures/sanctions, IO.11 PF sanctions. The writer should verify against the current Recommendations PDF, because the 2025 restructuring may have renumbered text.

## 2. Mutual evaluations: technical compliance vs effectiveness, follow-up, the 5th round, typical scores

### Takeaway
Each country gets two parallel ratings: technical compliance (TC) on each of the 40 Recommendations (Compliant / Largely / Partially / Non-compliant) and effectiveness on the 11 IOs (High / Substantial / Moderate / Low). The 5th round uses the 2022 Methodology (amended June 2026), launched in 2024 on a roughly six-year cycle to about 2030. It is more risk-focused and results-focused, and early reports suggest it is harder to score "High".

### Cited Findings
- The **2022 Methodology** (marked as amended June 2026) assesses TC and effectiveness of AML/CFT/**CPF** systems. The 2013 Methodology and 4th Round Procedures still apply to countries still in 4th-round follow-up — [FATF methodology page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Fatf-methodology.html); [5th Round Methodology](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/fatf-gafi/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/5th-Round-Methodology.html)
- Separate **5th-round Procedures** cover mutual evaluations, follow-up and the ICRG (the review group behind the grey list) against the 2022 Methodology. They were updated **October 2025** — [FATF methodology page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Fatf-methodology.html)
- The 5th round launched in 2024. Commentary describes risk-based scheduling, a roughly six-year cycle, and more structured, results-oriented follow-up — [RUSI commentary on UK 2027 evaluation](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/opening-door-uks-2027-financial-action-task-force-evaluation); [Silent Eight blog](https://www.silenteight.com/blog/when-the-assessors-come-fatf-mutual-evaluations-the-effectiveness-era-and-what-they-mean-f)
- Example schedule: UAE evaluated in 2026. **UK on-site June 2027, adoption at a 2028 Plenary.** Russia scheduled for 2028 — [RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/opening-door-uks-2027-financial-action-task-force-evaluation); [Themis](https://www.wearethemis.com/uk/resources/publications/preparing-for-fatf-mutual-evaluation-uae-2026)
- Early 5th-round results: **Singapore** got no IO rated High. **Latvia** (MONEYVAL) got five Highs. RUSI concludes the round "appears to be significantly increasing the onus" on countries to prove risk-based effectiveness — [RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/opening-door-uks-2027-financial-action-task-force-evaluation)
- **June 2026 Plenary adopted the MERs of Canada and Türkiye** — [FATF outcomes June 2026](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html)
- MONEYVAL calls the same cycle its "sixth round". Same standards, different label — [5th Round Methodology handout](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/methodology/Handout-5th-Round-Methodology.pdf) (via search summary)
- FATF publishes a **consolidated 4th-round ratings table** (XLSX/PDF), last updated 23 April 2025 — [FATF Assessment ratings](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Assessment-ratings.html)
- Typical scores (secondary source): only **19% of 120 assessed jurisdictions reached High or Substantial on IO.8 (confiscation)**, citing FATF's 2022 effectiveness report — [FluxForce statistics page](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/fatf-mutual-evaluation-results) (vendor aggregator; it contradicts FATF on update dates, so treat as unconfirmed)

### Inferences
- The ratings matrix (40 TC × 4 levels + 11 IO × 4 levels) is a clean, finite data structure. It could be mirrored in a training twin as "country profile" state.
- From general knowledge (4th-round practice, not re-verified): follow-up is either "regular" or "enhanced", and countries can request TC re-ratings in follow-up reports. Effectiveness ratings generally change only at the next full evaluation or a follow-up assessment.

### Gaps
- No verified global distribution of effectiveness ratings. FATF's 2022 "Report on the State of Effectiveness and Compliance" is the right source but was not retrieved. The commonly cited headline from memory, that most countries score Low or Moderate on most IOs, needs that report.
- The exact 5th-round ICRG entry thresholds were not retrieved. From memory, the 4th-round ICRG prioritisation criteria were, for example: 20 or more NC/PC TC ratings; or NC/PC on 3 or more of R.3, 5, 10, 11 and 20; or Low/Moderate on 9 or more IOs with at least 2 Low; or Low on 6 or more IOs. Whether the October 2025 Procedures changed these is unverified.

## 3. Grey list and black list: current composition, recent changes, economic impact

### Takeaway
After the **19 June 2026** Plenary, **22 jurisdictions** are under increased monitoring (grey list). Three are subject to a call for action (black list): **DPRK, Iran, Myanmar**. In 2025–26 the grey list churned heavily: several big African economies exited (South Africa, Nigeria in October 2025), while Kuwait, Papua New Guinea, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Iraq entered in 2026. IMF research finds grey-listing cuts capital inflows substantially.

### Inferences

- Grey-listing is not a sanction in law. The economic effect comes through correspondent banks applying EDD and de-risking. That is why the IMF looks at capital flows rather than legal penalties.

## 4. FSRBs, IMF/World Bank, UN, Basel Committee, Wolfsberg Group

### Takeaway
FATF writes the rules. **9 FATF-Style Regional Bodies** run peer evaluations for most of the world's roughly 200 jurisdictions. The IMF and World Bank also assess countries against the same methodology. The UN supplies the legal base (Security Council sanctions regimes and conventions). The Basel Committee translates AML into bank-supervisory expectations, and the private Wolfsberg Group sets bank-to-bank due diligence norms (CBDDQ).

### Cited Findings
- The **9 FSRBs** include APG (Asia/Pacific), CFATF (Caribbean), EAG (Eurasia), ESAAMLG (Eastern and Southern Africa), GABAC (Central Africa), GAFILAT (Latin America), plus MENAFATF, MONEYVAL and GIABA (West Africa) to make 9. FSRBs "conduct mutual evaluations to assess the level of compliance and effectiveness" of members' AML/CFT/CPF frameworks — [CFATF Global Network](https://cfatf-gafic.org/global-network/)
- Grey-list exits hand countries back to their FSRB for ongoing monitoring (e.g., Algeria to MENAFATF, Namibia to ESAAMLG, June 2026) — [ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/)
- **UN / laundering volume:** the widely quoted "2–5% of global GDP (USD 800 bn–2 trn)" is attributed to the UN/UNODC but originates in the **IMF's 1998 "consensus range"**. UNODC's own 2011 study estimated all criminal proceeds at about **3.6% of GDP (range 2.3–5.5%), about USD 2.1 trn in 2009**, and said the estimates must be "treated with caution" — [UNODC, Illicit Financial Flows 2011](https://unodc.org/lpomex/es/publicaciones/documentos/crimen/Illicit_financial_flows_2011_web.html); [UiT summary of UN figure](https://site.uit.no/arcsecc/?p=221)
- **Basel Committee:** *Sound management of risks related to money laundering and financing of terrorism* (January 2014; revised 2017 for correspondent banking and de-risking; updated July 2020). It is now chapter **AFS10** of the Basel consolidated framework. Core elements are risk assessment, governance, three lines of defence, transaction monitoring, and customer acceptance — [BIS d505 (2020)](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d505.pdf); [BIS consolidated AFS10](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-consolidated-guidelines/module/afs/10)
- **Wolfsberg Group:** its **Correspondent Banking Due Diligence Questionnaire (CBDDQ)** is now at v1.4. It is answered at legal-entity level and covers ownership, the AML/CTF and sanctions programme, ABC, and SAR processes. Banks publish completed copies, e.g. Vontobel (Feb 2026) and PNC — [Wolfsberg CBDDQ sample (2026)](https://s203.q4cdn.com/215756951/files/doc_downloads/corporate-responsability/2026/01/Wolfsberg-Correspondent-Banking-Due-Diligence-Questionnaire.pdf); [Vontobel Feb 2026](https://www.vontobel.com/globalassets/content/legal-notice/patriot-act-certification/wolfsberg-anti-money-laundering-questionnaire_february-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- From general knowledge, not fetched: the IMF and World Bank are FATF observers and conduct some mutual evaluations under the same methodology, which FATF or the FSRB then adopts. The IMF also runs AML/CFT assessments within Financial Sector Assessment Programs (FSAPs). UN Security Council resolutions (1267/1989/2253 ISIL/Al-Qaida, 1373 TF, 1718 DPRK, 2231 Iran) are what FATF R.6 and R.7 implement through targeted financial sanctions. The writer should source these if quoted.
- Wolfsberg has 12 member banks (from memory; the 12 were not listed in any fetched source).

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: FSRB-specific numbers (e.g., APG has 42 members, MONEYVAL covers 30+ states — memory only), the IMF/World Bank share of evaluations, and the current Wolfsberg member list.
- No newer UNODC global laundering estimate (after 2011) was found. The 2–5% figure is decades old.

## 5. Egmont Group of FIUs, Egmont Secure Web, FIU models

### Takeaway
The Egmont Group links **182 FIUs** (as of early 2026). They exchange intelligence through the FinCEN-managed **Egmont Secure Web (ESW)**, under binding Principles for Information Exchange (revised July 2025). FIUs come in four models (administrative, law-enforcement, judicial, hybrid), and the administrative model dominates.

### Cited Findings
- **182 member FIUs**, per Egmont's FAQ and FIU press releases from January–February 2026 — [Egmont FAQ](https://egmontgroup.org/faqs/); [Gibraltar FIU, Feb 2026](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/press-releases/gibraltar-fiu-participates-in-egmont-group-meetings-in-tanzania-and-secures-key-appointment-852026-11679)
- Growth: there were 181 members after the 2025 Plenary, which admitted Mozambique, The Gambia, Equatorial Guinea and Sierra Leone. Tanzania's FIU hosted the 26th Working and Regional Group meetings in January 2026 with 400+ attendees — [EAG Secretariat](https://eurasiangroup.org/en/the-eag-secretariat-attended-the-30th-egmont-group-plenary-meeting); [Egmont news, Tanzania](https://egmontgroup.org/news/tanzanias-financial-intelligence-unit-fiu-hosts-over-four-hundred-egmont-group-representatives-from-around-the-globe/)
- **ESW:** the primary secure channel for member information exchange. **FinCEN** manages it and reports that FIUs use it for "thousands of law enforcement cases per year". Egmont operational guidance (2017) says FIUs should ideally check ESW **daily** — [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2016-08/20110531.pdf); [Egmont Operational Guidance 2017](https://www.mpf.gob.ar/procelac-lavado/files/2020/04/EGMONT.2017.Operational_Guidance.pdf)
- **Principles for Information Exchange (revised July 2025):** binding on members, with significant non-compliance going to the Egmont **Support and Compliance Process**. Exchange rests on reciprocity, and an FIU may decline where reciprocity is lacking. Cooperation should not be unreasonably restricted because of a counterpart's institutional status (its model) — [Egmont Principles, July 2025](https://egmontgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/EG-Principles-for-Information-Exchange-Revised-July-2025.pdf); [Egmont informal cooperation brochure](https://egmontgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Informal-Int-Cooperation-Brochure-For-FIUs.pdf)
- FATF **R.29** expects FIUs to apply for Egmont membership — [Egmont Principles](https://egmontgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/EG-Principles-for-Information-Exchange-Revised-July-2025.pdf) (via search summary)
- **The four FIU models:**
  - **Administrative:** attached to a regulator, central bank or finance ministry, or an independent authority. Acts as a buffer between reporting entities and police.
  - **Law-enforcement:** attached to a police agency.
  - **Judicial:** attached to a prosecutor or the judiciary. It can trigger freezing or searches.
  - **Hybrid:** combines the others.
  - Source: [Egmont, Financial Intelligence Unit](https://egmontgroup.org/about/financial-intelligence-unit)
- Model counts: in 2007–08, 69 administrative, 27 law-enforcement, 4 judicial and 8 mixed — [Egmont Annual Report 2007–08](https://egmontgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Egmont-Group-Annual-Report-2007-2008.pdf). A 2024–25 survey of 139 FIUs reportedly found about 102 administrative, 18 law-enforcement and about 19 hybrid — [Zigram summary of Egmont AR 2024–25](https://www.zigram.tech/?p=39301) (secondary; not verified)

### Inferences
- The flow of intelligence: obliged entity, then STR/SAR to the national FIU, then FIU analysis, then dissemination to domestic law enforcement or a request/spontaneous disclosure to a foreign FIU over ESW, then (if needed) formal MLA under R.37/38 for evidence and confiscation. Egmont is intelligence-to-intelligence. It does not replace MLA.

### Gaps
- The current count of FIUs by model was not verified from a primary source. The Egmont annual report 2024–25 itself was not opened.
- No figures were found on ESW message volumes.

## 6. Global indices: Basel AML Index 2025, Financial Secrecy Index 2025

### Takeaway
The **Basel AML Index 2025** (released 8 Dec 2025) ranks Myanmar as highest risk (8.18) and Finland as lowest. The global average barely moved (5.30 to 5.28). The **FSI 2025** (released 3 June 2025) ranks the **United States first**, followed by Switzerland, Singapore, Hong Kong and Luxembourg. This shows that "risk of being a laundering venue" and "supplying secrecy to non-residents" are different measures.

### Cited Findings
- **Basel AML Index 2025** (14th Public Edition, 8 Dec 2025). Scores run 0–10 (higher is riskier) and measure vulnerability and capacity, not actual laundering volume. Highest risk: **Myanmar 8.18, Haiti 8.12, DR Congo, Chad, Equatorial Guinea**. Lowest: **Finland, Iceland, San Marino, Denmark, Estonia**. The global average went from 5.30 to 5.28 (not significant); more than half of jurisdictions improved and 43% worsened. Bands: low <4.70, medium 4.70–6.08, high >6.08 — [Basel Institute, Basel AML Index 2025](https://baselgovernance.org/publications/basel-aml-index-2025); [Basel key findings](https://index.baselgovernance.org/news/key-findings); [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/127547-morocco-improves-ranking-in-2025-basel-anti-money-laundering-index.html); [ScandAsia](https://scandasia.com/finland-tops-global-anti-money-laundering-index-as-myanmar-ranks-highest-risk/)
- Regional: of 48 African countries, 25 are high risk, 20 medium and 3 low (Botswana, Seychelles, Mauritius). In MENA, Djibouti scores 6.93 and Algeria 6.82 — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/1112-51304-basel-aml-index-maps-africa-s-highest-and-lowest-money-laundering-risks-in-2025); [Hespress](https://en.hespress.com/127547-morocco-improves-ranking-in-2025-basel-anti-money-laundering-index.html)
- **Financial Secrecy Index 2025** (TJN, released 3 June 2025; previous edition 2022; 141 jurisdictions). Top 5: **USA, Switzerland, Singapore, Hong Kong, Luxembourg**; Japan is 10th. Method: 20 indicators (100+ questions), combined with the volume of financial services to non-residents. TJN says a high rank means a bigger role in enabling hiding and laundering, not necessarily more secrecy. The FSI is now updated on a rolling basis instead of every two years — [Tax Justice Network, indexes](https://taxjustice.net/indexes-tools/); [St Andrews research portal](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-tax-justice-networks-june-2025-update-to-the-financial-secrec/); [Corruption Watch](https://www.corruptionwatch.org.za/tax-justice-network-financial-secrecy-a-growing-driver-of-autocracy/)

### Inferences
- Index contrasts are useful in teaching. Myanmar is on both the FATF black list and top of Basel. The US and Switzerland are not on any FATF list yet top the FSI. Monaco and the BVI are FATF grey-listed financial centres. Each index measures something different: compliance process (FATF), vulnerability (Basel), and secrecy × market size (FSI).

### Gaps
- The full Basel 2025 table (164-ish jurisdictions; exact count not retrieved) and its scores beyond the top two were not opened directly.
- A French-language outlet reported 133 jurisdictions for FSI 2025 versus TJN's 141. 141 is preferred.
