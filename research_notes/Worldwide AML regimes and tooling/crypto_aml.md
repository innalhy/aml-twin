# AML for Virtual Assets and Crypto: Worldwide (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-13. Note on sources: the FATF site returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so FATF figures below come from FATF page titles plus law-firm / vendor summaries of the reports. Vendor figures (Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic, Arkham) are **industry estimates**, not official statistics. Items marked "[background, not re-verified]" come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed by a source fetched in this session.

## 1. FATF Recommendation 15 / INR.15 and the 2025-2026 Targeted Updates

### Takeaway
R.15 extends the full FATF standard to virtual assets and VASPs (licensing/registration, risk-based supervision, CDD, and the "travel rule" via R.16). The seventh Targeted Update (16 July 2026) shows only one of ~149 assessed jurisdictions fully compliant with R.15 and 34% largely compliant; travel-rule legislation is now in 83% of responding jurisdictions, but enforcement and supervision lag.

### Cited Findings
- **What R.15/INR.15 require** [background, not re-verified]: countries must assess VA/VASP ML/TF risk; license or register VASPs (at minimum where created); apply risk-based AML/CFT supervision by a competent authority (not an SRO alone); VASPs must apply preventive measures (CDD, record-keeping, STRs, sanctions screening); and R.16 "travel rule" applies to VA transfers with a de minimis threshold of USD/EUR 1,000 permitted. FATF guidance referenced by vendors confirms the US$1,000 VASP-to-VASP benchmark — [Elliptic: What is the Travel Rule?](https://www.elliptic.co/blockchain-basics/what-is-the-travel-rule)
- FATF's seventh Targeted Update on Implementation of the FATF Standards on VAs/VASPs was published July 2026 — [FATF publication page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/targeted-updated-virtualassets-vasps-2026.html); [FATF news release](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html); [Regulation Tomorrow, July 2026](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/07/fatf-publishes-targeted-update-on-implementation-of-the-fatf-standards-on-virtual-assets-and-virtual-asset-service-providers/)
- 2026 figures (as of April 2026): 34% of assessed jurisdictions largely compliant with R.15 (up from 29% in 2025); 43% partially compliant; only **one** of 149 assessed jurisdictions fully compliant (same as 2025; FATF does not name it) — [21 Analytics 2026 summary](https://www.21analytics.co/blog/2026-fatf-targeted-update-part1/); [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/fr/resources/blog/fatf-updates-on-recommendation-15-implementation-in-jurisdictions-with-materially-important-virtual-asset-sectors). A "22% non-compliant" figure appears in one secondary summary only and was not verified — [KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/fatf-releases-2026-virtual-asset-regulatory-report-global-compliance-progress-and-emerging-risks)
- Travel rule: 83% of responding jurisdictions have passed travel-rule legislation in 2026 vs 73% in 2025; implementation "remains uneven" — [21 Analytics 2026](https://www.21analytics.co/blog/2026-fatf-targeted-update-part1/); [FATF news](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html)
- 2026 gaps flagged by FATF: turning risk assessments into mitigation; operationalising licensing/registration; identifying unlicensed persons conducting VASP activity; effective supervision and enforcement. Risks flagged: industrialised VA-enabled fraud by organised crime, stablecoin misuse, P2P transfers via unhosted wallets, offshore VASPs, DeFi — [FATF news release](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html) (via search summary); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/fatf-virtual-assets-standards)
- Sixth Targeted Update (26 June 2025): of 138 jurisdictions assessed, 29% largely compliant (25% in 2024), non-compliant fell from 25% to 21%, 49% partially compliant; 99 jurisdictions had adopted or were drafting travel-rule legislation; the 73% figure = 85 of 117 respondents — [21 Analytics 2025 summary](https://www.21analytics.ch/blog/2025-fatf-targeted-update-summarised); [Notabene](https://notabene.id/post/2025-fatf-targeted-update); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/FATF-publishes-targeted-update-and-guidance-on-vi)

### Inferences
- Seven years after INR.15 (2019), full compliance is essentially nil and roughly two-thirds of jurisdictions are still only partially or non-compliant, so cross-border VASP flows routinely touch weakly supervised counterparties.
- The gap between "law passed" (83%) and "law enforced" is the main residual risk, and it is what the "sunrise problem" describes in practice.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the FATF PDF directly (403); exact denominators differ across summaries (147 respondents vs 149 assessed). Count of jurisdictions actively enforcing the travel rule in 2026 not found.
- FATF did not name the single fully compliant jurisdiction.

## 2. How the travel rule works technically; thresholds by jurisdiction

### Takeaway
The travel rule requires originator and beneficiary data to move with a VA transfer between VASPs. IVMS101 is the shared data model; competing transport protocols and networks (TRUST, TRP, Notabene, Sumsub, VerifyVASP, CODE, Sygna, etc.) carry it, and interoperability plus uneven adoption ("sunrise problem") remain the main frictions. Thresholds diverge sharply: EU zero, US US$3,000, Singapore S$1,500 (tiered), Hong Kong HK$8,000 (tiered), Dubai AED 3,500, Japan no threshold since June 2023.

### Cited Findings
- IVMS101 is the industry data standard aimed at fixing message fragmentation across solutions — [Spark travel-rule guide](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide). [background, not re-verified]: IVMS101 (InterVASP Messaging Standard, published 2020 by the Joint Working Group on interVASP messaging standards) defines field structures for natural/legal person names, geographic address, national ID, customer ID, date/place of birth, and account (wallet) numbers.
- TRUST (Travel Rule Universal Solution Technology) is a closed network operated by Coinbase as a not-for-profit coalition of VASPs; members identify counterparties and send data securely. BitMEX joined TRUST alongside existing VerifyVASP and Notabene integrations — [21 Analytics glossary: TRUST](https://www.21analytics.ch/glossary/trust); [BitMEX blog](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/bitmex-joins-trust-solution-to-expand-travel-rule-capabilities)
- TRP (Travel Rule Protocol) is an open protocol that lets VASPs connect across different solutions if they implement it — [21 Analytics: TRUST and TRP compared](https://www.21analytics.ch/blog/trust-and-trp-compared)
- "Sunrise problem": the FATF 2025 update framed travel-rule progress as narrowing the sunrise issue, i.e., VASPs in jurisdictions with live rules must transact with counterparties in jurisdictions that have not yet implemented it — [21 Analytics 2025](https://www.21analytics.ch/blog/2025-fatf-targeted-update-summarised)
- **EU**: Transfer of Funds Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 applies to CASPs from 30 December 2024 with **no minimum threshold** and no transitional grace period — [Joinble MiCA travel rule](https://joinble.io/en/blog/mica-travel-rule-july-2026-casp-compliance); [Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide). One secondary guide lists €1,000 for the EU — likely conflating with the TFR's extra verification duty for transfers over €1,000 involving self-hosted wallets [background, not re-verified] — [Spark lookup](https://www.spark.money/tools/travel-rule-lookup)
- **US**: FinCEN's funds travel rule applies at US$3,000; transfers under US$3,000 are exempt — [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blockchain-basics/what-is-the-travel-rule)
- **Singapore**: MAS Notice PSN02 (from 28 Jan 2020) — originator and beneficiary info required regardless of amount; broader originator data above S$1,500 — [Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide)
- **Hong Kong**: from 1 June 2023 under AMLO/SFC AML Guideline — below HK$8,000 names and account numbers; above HK$8,000 additionally originator address or ID — [private.law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/travel-rule); [Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide)
- **Japan**: FSA asked JVCEA (Mar 2021) to adopt self-regulatory travel rules by April 2022; statutory amendment promulgated Dec 2022; from 1 June 2023 all conditions including the threshold were removed — [FSA notice](https://www.fsa.go.jp/en/news/2021/20210331/20210331.html); [Sygna Japan guide](https://www.sygna.io/?p=7815)
- **UAE (Dubai VARA)**: rulebook requires originator/beneficiary data before transfers "exceeding AED 3,500" (≈US$1,000); VARA Rulebook 2.0 issued May 2025, effective 19 June 2025; DFSA (DIFC) applies the rule with no threshold — [VARA rulebook travel rule](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/node/146); [Nominis](https://www.nominis.io/insights/dubais-vara-changes-coming-into-effect-june-2025); [21 Analytics Dubai](https://www.21analytics.co/blog/dubais-travel-rule-what-vasps-need-to-know/)
- **UK**: conflicting secondary claims (no minimum vs £800) — [Spark lookup](https://www.spark.money/tools/travel-rule-lookup); unresolved.
- Unhosted wallets: FATF 2026 flags P2P transfers via unhosted wallets as a key risk — [FATF news](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html). [background, not re-verified]: typical controls are collecting the counterparty name, proof of wallet ownership (e.g., Satoshi test or message signing) above thresholds, and blockchain screening of the address.

### Inferences
- A global VASP must run the strictest-common-denominator rule (EU zero threshold) or maintain per-corridor logic; vendors' multi-protocol gateways exist largely because no single protocol has won.
- Travel-rule data and on-chain analytics are complementary: the rule supplies identity off-chain; analytics supplies on-chain counterparty risk.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm current technical details/market share of Sumsub, Notabene, VerifyVASP, CODE (Korea) protocol networks from a primary source this session.
- UK threshold and the EU self-hosted wallet €1,000 trigger not verified against primary text.

## 3. Blockchain analytics: tracing methods and limits

### Takeaway
Firms such as Chainalysis, Elliptic and TRM Labs combine clustering heuristics (common-input ownership, change detection) with off-chain attribution (tags from exchanges, seizures, OSINT) to map addresses to entities, then score risk by exposure. Clusters are probabilistic; CoinJoin/PayJoin, privacy coins and chain-hopping through bridges degrade tracing, pushing compliance to "edge" monitoring at fiat ramps.

### Cited Findings
- Common-input-ownership heuristic: addresses spent together as inputs in one transaction are presumed controlled by one entity (all keys needed to sign) — [Spark glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/common-input-heuristic); [Bitfury clustering whitepaper](https://bitfury.com/content/5-white-papers-research/clustering_whitepaper.pdf)
- Change-address detection keeps the change output in the sender's cluster; other sub-heuristics include consolidation transactions, address reuse and round-number detection — [arXiv 2104.09979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09979); [open-source clustering project](https://github.com/VincenzoImp/bitcoin-address-clustering)
- Clusters carry confidence levels; off-chain tags are used to verify; naive clustering can wrongly merge unrelated actors — [Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/wallet-clustering); [arXiv 2104.09979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09979)
- CoinJoin breaks common-input assumption; PayJoin deliberately violates it — [Spark glossary](https://www.spark.money/glossary/common-input-heuristic)
- Privacy coins: academic work (2018-2019) found Monero cross-chain traceability limited to a small share of inputs after protocol changes; Zcash anonymity sets can be reduced with usage heuristics; compliance shifts to measuring risk "at the edges" (fiat ramps, exchanges, bridges) — [Monero cross-chain traceability study](https://deepai.org/publication/an-empirical-analysis-of-monero-cross-chain-traceability); [UCL / Bentham's Gaze](https://www.benthamsgaze.org/2019/08/15/tracing-transactions-across-cryptocurrency-ledgers/); [Elliptic privacy coin risk](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-3864/blockchain-analysis/privacy-coin-risk-scoring-and-traceability-strategies-for-aml-and-sanctions-compliance.html)
- Cross-chain: ShapeShift data showed users can be tracked across ledgers; "u-turn" round-trip heuristics found a scam address in 34 round trips — [Bentham's Gaze](https://www.benthamsgaze.org/2019/08/15/tracing-transactions-across-cryptocurrency-ledgers/)
- Real-world chain-hop example (Bybit, Feb 2025): Arkham estimated ≥US$240m laundered via THORChain to native BTC; one analyst counted 161,490 ETH across 3,934 bridge transactions in ~115 hours; TRM said ~US$400m laundered within a day; funds dispersed across thousands of addresses on several chains — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/343855/bybit-hackers-half-the-stolen-eth-bitcoin-fbi-confirms-lazarus); [The Record](https://therecord.media/fbi-bybit-laundering-crypto-warning)
- Successful large-scale attribution example: TRM describes "Operation Prince" analytics supporting the 127,271 BTC forfeiture held in 25 unhosted wallets — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/operation-prince-inside-the-global-effort-that-led-to-the-largest-forfeiture-in-us-history)
- Chainalysis is deploying AI agents for investigations (vendor claim) — [VaaSBlock](https://www.vaasblock.com/ai/chainalysis-deploys-ai-agents-against-crypto-crime-as-illicit-volume-hits-154-billion//)

### Inferences
- Account-based chains (Ethereum, Tron) lack UTXO input clustering, so attribution there leans more on deposit-address reuse, contract interactions and off-chain tags [background].
- Vendor risk scores are probabilistic opinions; for a rules-first system they belong as inputs to deterministic rules, not as the decision itself (fits AGENTS.md rule 8).

### Gaps
- No primary methodology documents from Chainalysis/Elliptic/TRM fetched; vendor accuracy claims are unverified. Academic privacy-coin results are dated (2018-2019).

## 4. Major enforcement actions and the 2025 US political shift

### Takeaway
2023-2025 saw record exchange resolutions (Binance US$4.3bn, OKX ~US$504m, KuCoin ~US$297m, BitMEX), sanctions-driven takedowns (Bitzlato, Garantex/Grinex/A7A5), and contested mixer cases (Tornado Cash). From April 2025 the US pivoted: the DOJ narrowed crypto enforcement to fraud and serious crime, OFAC delisted Tornado Cash, and Trump pardoned CZ (23 Oct 2025), while the GENIUS Act imposed BSA duties on stablecoin issuers.

### Cited Findings
- **Binance (Nov 2023)**: pleaded guilty to conspiracy to violate the BSA, failure to register as a money transmitter, and IEEPA violations; ~US$4.3bn total; FinCEN civil penalty US$3.4bn with five-year monitorship (first FinCEN monitor); OFAC US$968m for >1.6m apparent violations; DOJ credited ~US$1.8bn toward CFTC/FinCEN/OFAC; CZ pleaded guilty to willful BSA violation, US$50m fine, stepped down — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ita1/binance-and-founder-changpeng-zhao-plead-guilty-and-pay-more-than-4-billion-in-p/); [DOJ letter to Senate Banking](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/doj_binanceresponse.pdf); [GetNickLaw on OFAC](https://getnicklaw.com/news-media/2023/11/binance-pleads-guilty-and-pays-4-3b-fine-including-nearly-1b-to-settle-ofac-sanctions-violations/). Forensic Risk Alliance appointed DOJ monitor May 2024; Binance later reported in talks to drop the monitor — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/370929/binance-in-talks-with-doj-over-dropping-independent-compliance-monitor-bloomberg)
- **CZ pardon**: Trump granted a full pardon on 23 October 2025; White House called the prosecution part of the Biden "war on cryptocurrency"; AP noted Zhao/Binance support for Trump-family venture World Liberty Financial — [Citizen Digital (Reuters)](https://www.citizen.digital/news/trump-pardons-convicted-binance-founder-zhao-white-house-says-n371838); [Salina Post (AP)](https://salinapost.com/posts/421f4786-a185-47aa-977f-8a2b761c9ebd)
- **BitMEX**: pleaded guilty (July 2024) to BSA violation; 15 Jan 2025 Judge Koeltl imposed US$100m fine (DOJ had sought US$417m), on top of the earlier US$110m — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/banking-law/bitmex-fined-100-million-for-violating-us-bank-secrecy-act); [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2025/01/16/bitmex-fined-additional-100-million-for-flouting-u-s-anti-money-laundering-laws). [background, not re-verified]: BitMEX's founders were pardoned by Trump in March 2025.
- **KuCoin (Jan 27, 2025)**: Peken Global pleaded guilty to unlicensed money transmitting; US$112.9m fine + US$184.5m forfeiture (~US$297m); exit US market ≥2 years; no KYC until Aug 2023; founders took DPAs and forfeited US$2.7m — [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/kucoin-to-pay-nearly-300-million-in-penalties-after-guilty-plea); [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/kucoin-pleads-guilty-to-unlicensed-money-transmission-agrees-to-nearly-300m-in-penalties)
- **OKX (Feb 24, 2025)**: Aux Cayes Fintech pleaded guilty to operating an unlicensed money transmitting business; US$420.3m forfeiture + ~US$84.4m fine (>US$504m); served US customers 2018-early 2024 with >US$1tn in transactions and >US$5bn suspicious transactions/criminal proceeds — [FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulations-compliance/okx-resolves-us-doj-investigation-agrees-to-pay-around-504m-in-penalties); [DOJ release (copy)](https://www.lit-wc.aoshearman.com/siteFiles/50509/[Enforcement%202]%20DOJ%20Press%20Release%20-%20OKX.pdf)
- **Bitzlato**: founder Anatoly Legkodymov sentenced 18 July 2024 to time served (18 months) after admitting the exchange was a conduit for Hydra darknet funds (~US$700m case); forfeited claim to US$23m seized by France; later sought a Trump pardon (status unverified) — [BNN Bloomberg](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/international/2024/07/18/crypto-exchange-head-gets-no-more-jail-in-700-million-scam); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bitzlato-founder-anatoly-legkodymov-pardon-trump)
- **Garantex / Grinex / A7A5**: disrupted 6 March 2025 (US with German and Finnish police; domain seized, ~US$26m frozen); staff launched successor Grinex; OFAC on 14 Aug 2025 designated Garantex co-owners, Grinex and entities behind ruble stablecoin A7A5 (issuer Old Vector, Kyrgyzstan; linked to Ilan Shor and Promsvyazbank) — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/garantex-grinex-and-the-a7a5-token-a-deep-dive-into-sanctions-evasion-networks); [Fintelegram](https://fintelegram.com/garantex-2-0-how-grinex-a7a5-built-moscows-crypto-escape-hatch-and-why-regulators-just-slammed-it-shut/). Volume figures for A7A5 conflict (US$51bn per Chainalysis via Fintelegram vs US$9.3bn over four months per UK)
- **Tornado Cash**: Fifth Circuit (Van Loon v. Treasury, 26 Nov 2024, 122 F.4th 549) held immutable smart contracts are not "property" under IEEPA; OFAC delisted Tornado Cash on 21 March 2025 "exercising discretion," keeping developer Roman Semenov on the SDN list — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/treasury-department-delists-tornado-cash-following-the-fifth-circuits-decision.html); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/04/05/why-ofac-delisted-tornado-cash); [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/crypto-policy-tracker/a-whirlwind-of-change-the-delisting-of-tornado-cash)
- **Roman Storm**: August 2025 jury convicted on conspiracy to operate an unlicensed money transmitting business; hung on money-laundering and sanctions-evasion conspiracy counts; acquittal motion argued April 2026 still pending; retrial set for 26 April 2027 — [Decrypt verdict](https://decrypt.co/333790/jury-tornado-cash-developer-storm-guilty-money-transmitting); [Decrypt retrial](https://decrypt.co/376577/roman-storms-tornado-cash-retrial-pushed-to-april-2027); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/roman-storm-retrial-delayed-april-2027). DOJ official Matthew Galeotti said after the verdict DOJ would not bring §1960 charges where software is truly decentralized and non-custodial — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/roman-storm-legal-fund-influx-defense-retrial)
- **DOJ Blanche memo (7 April 2025)**, "Ending Regulation by Prosecution": disbanded the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team; DOJ to stop prosecuting platforms for users' acts; focus on fraud on investors, scams, hacks, terrorism, drugs; discourage licensing/registration/BSA charges absent knowing, willful violations; close inconsistent ongoing investigations — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/doj-announces-shift-in-crypto-enforcement-and-end-of-regulation-by-prosecution); [CoinDesk via Coinspectator](https://coinspectator.com/coindesk/2025/04/08/doj-axes-crypto-unit-as-trumps-regulatory-pullback-continues/); ethics complaint over Blanche's undivested crypto — [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/todd-blanche-complaint-conflict-of-interest)
- **GENIUS Act (signed July 2025 [date background, not re-verified])**: payment stablecoin issuers treated as BSA financial institutions with AML/sanctions programs (risk assessment, sanctions screening, compliance officer, CIP, SAR monitoring, recordkeeping); must have technical ability to freeze/burn/block to comply with "lawful orders," including foreign issuers whose tokens trade in US secondary markets — [Senate Banking fact sheet](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/majority/fact-sheet-the-genius-act-bolsters-national-security); [Davis Polk slides](https://projects.law.harvard.edu/financialregulation/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2025/10/Davis-Polk_GENIUS-Act-AMLCFT-and-Sanctions-Compliance.pdf). FinCEN and OFAC proposed implementing AML/CFT and sanctions rules on 10 April 2026 (comments due 9 June 2026); Treasury cited ~55,000 SARs referencing stablecoins Jan 2015-Nov 2025 — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260420-treasury-announces-proposed-rule-to-implement-the-genius-acts-requirements-to-counter-illicit-finance); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-au/insights/publications/2026/04/treasury-proposes-aml-cft-and-sanctions-compliance-rules-for-stablecoin-issuers-under-the-genius-act)

### Inferences
- The US moved from "platform liability" (Binance/OKX/KuCoin via §1960 and BSA) to "bad-actor" enforcement, while simultaneously hard-wiring AML and freeze capability into regulated stablecoin issuers. Enforcement against foreign illicit networks (Garantex, Huione, Prince Group) continued and arguably intensified.
- Clemency for convicted exchange executives (CZ, BitMEX founders) weakens the individual-liability deterrent that the 2023 Binance resolution created.

### Gaps
- Final GENIUS Act implementing rule status after the June 2026 comment period not found. Van Loon mootness and Coin Center (11th Cir.) outcomes not found. Whether Binance's DOJ/FinCEN monitorships were ended in 2026 not confirmed.

## 5. Regional regimes: EU, Hong Kong, Singapore, UAE, Japan

### Takeaway
The EU runs MiCA authorisation (transition ended 1 July 2026) plus the TFR travel rule (Dec 2024), with AMLA taking direct supervision of selected high-risk obliged entities (possibly including CASPs) from 1 Jan 2028. Hong Kong pairs SFC VATP licensing with an HKMA stablecoin licensing regime (1 Aug 2025). Singapore closed the offshore-only loophole with DTSP licensing from 30 June 2025. Dubai's VARA runs a rulebook with an AED 3,500 travel-rule threshold. Japan has applied the travel rule without threshold since June 2023.

### Cited Findings
- **EU MiCA**: maximum transitional period under Art. 143(3) ended 1 July 2026; unauthorised firms must stop serving EU clients; some member states shortened the transition — [AGP Law](https://www.agplaw.com/?p=32785); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/micas-transition-ends-on-1-july-2026-is-your-crypto-business-authorised-to-continue/1810268)
- **EU AMLA/AMLR**: first selection of directly supervised entities from 1 July 2027 (six months), direct supervision from 1 Jan 2028; CASPs named among likely candidates; ~40 institutions expected in first wave; AMLR applies from 10 July 2027 and brings MiCA CASPs fully into the single rulebook; AMLA currently does indirect supervision of high-risk sectors including CASPs — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/direct-supervisory-powers-of-the-new-european-antimoney-laundering-authority); [FinTech Global, Jul 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/07/31/eus-amla-puts-high-risk-banks-on-notice-for-2028/); [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/from-vision-to-action-amla-publishes-its-first-annual-work-programme); AMLA warned of crypto-sector ML risk (Jul 2025) — [Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/blog/news/amla-watchdog-warns-of-money-laundering-risk-in-crypto-sector)
- **Hong Kong**: Stablecoins Ordinance (Cap. 656) passed 21 May 2025, effective 1 Aug 2025; HKMA licenses issuers of fiat-referenced stablecoins; unlicensed activity punishable by HK$5m fine and 7 years; only HKMA-licensed FRS may be offered to retail; SFC-licensed VATPs among permitted offerors; HKMA consulted on AML/CFT requirements 26 May 2025 — [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/blogs/fintechlinks/2025/august/hong-kong-launches-new-stablecoin-regime); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/en/perspectives/2025/07/hong-kongs-stablecoin-regulations-unveiled-bill-passed-licensee-guidelines); [Regulation Tomorrow](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2025/06/hong-kong-stablecoins-sprint-historic-bill-passes-draft-licensee-guidelines-released-and-aml-cft-consultation-begins/). First licences reported 10 April 2026 (single source, unverified) — [private.law wiki](https://wiki.private.law/en/hk-stablecoin-ordinance.md). VATP licensing under AMLO (since June 2023) and HK$8,000 travel-rule tier — see §2.
- **Singapore**: from 30 June 2025, Singapore-based persons providing digital token services solely to overseas customers must hold a DTSP licence under FSMA 2022 or cease; MAS "will generally not issue a licence"; rationale: higher ML risk and inability to supervise offshore activity; WazirX moved to Panama — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/singapore-s-june-30-hard-deadline-for-crypto-firms-serving-overseas-customers); [Singapore Law Watch](https://www.singaporelawwatch.sg/Headlines/MAS-clarifies-position-on-regulation-of-digital-token-service-providers); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/singapore-confirms-near-ban-on-foreign-only-digital-token-services?rand=6792)

## 6. Illicit crypto volumes, stablecoins, DPRK hacks, and pig-butchering laundering

### Takeaway
Vendor estimates for 2025 converge on a record US$154-158bn in illicit inflows (Chainalysis / TRM), driven mainly by sanctions-linked flows (A7A5, Garantex/Grinex), with stablecoins carrying ~84% of illicit volume. DPRK's US$1.5bn Bybit hack (Feb 2025) was the largest crypto theft ever. Southeast Asian scam-compound laundering prompted the FinCEN 311 cutoff of Huione Group and the record US$15bn Prince Group bitcoin forfeiture (Oct 2025).

### Cited Findings
- **Chainalysis 2026 Crypto Crime Report (industry estimate)**: illicit addresses received at least US$154bn in 2025, +162% vs revised US$57.2bn for 2024; still <1% of attributed on-chain volume; sanctions-related activity +694%; stablecoins 84% of illicit transaction volume; ≥US$14bn scam revenue (may exceed US$17bn); theft >US$3.4bn, a record — [GN Crypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/chainalysis-crypto-crime-2026-record-illicit-volume/); [Bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/crypto-crime-hits-154b-in-2025-but-its-below-1-of-onchain-activity/?rand=52384); [OneBullEx](https://www.onebullex.com/news/articles/chainalysis-2026-report-stablecoins-carry-84-of-154-billion-illicit-crypto-flows)
- **TRM Labs 2026 Crypto Crime Report (industry estimate, released 28 Jan 2026)**: ~US$158bn illicit volume in 2025 (+145% from US$64.5bn); 1.2% of total crypto activity (1.3% in 2024); ~US$141bn stablecoin-linked transfers to illicit entities; sanctions-related flows = 86% of illicit; nearly half of sanctions-related volume via A7A5; methodology changed (prior years "undercounted") — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/258852-cryptocurrency-ecosystem-saw-major-spike-in-illicit-activities-totaling-158b-report-reveals/); [TRM reports page](https://trmlabs.com/resources/reports-and-whitepapers)
- **USDT on Tron**: TRM's 2024 report (2023 data) said Tron carried 58% of illicit volume vs 24% Ethereum, 12% Bitcoin — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/335268/illicit-use-of-blockchains-represents-just-0-4-of-total-crypto-volume-trm-labs-finds); 2026 report describes ISIS affiliates in Syria using USDT on Tron — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/258852-cryptocurrency-ecosystem-saw-major-spike-in-illicit-activities-totaling-158b-report-reveals/)
- **Bybit (21 Feb 2025)**: FBI attributed ~US$1.5bn theft to North Korea ("TraderTraitor," incl. Lazarus); attack vector was a compromised Safe{Wallet} developer machine; Bybit offered up to US$140m in bounties — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/fbi-says-north-korea-was-responsible-15-billion-bybit-hack-rcna193944); [The Record](https://therecord.media/fbi-bybit-laundering-crypto-warning); laundering via THORChain (see §3) — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/343855/bybit-hackers-half-the-stolen-eth-bitcoin-fbi-confirms-lazarus). - **Huione Group (FinCEN §311)**: NPRM 5 May 2025; final rule published 16 Oct 2025 (90 FR 48295), effective 17 Nov 2025; bars US financial institutions from correspondent accounts for or on behalf of Huione Group (Cambodia) and requires special due diligence; basis: laundering for Lazarus Group heists and Southeast Asian CVC investment scams; covers Haowang Guarantee, Huione Pay, Huione Crypto — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/16/2025-19571/imposition-of-special-measure-regarding-huione-group-as-a-foreign-financial-institution-of-primary); [FinCEN final rule PDF](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-10/Huione-Group-Final-Rule_0.pdf); [Money Laundering News](https://www.moneylaunderingnews.com/2025/10/fincen-bars-huione-group-from-financial-system/)
- **Prince Group / Chen Zhi (14 Oct 2025)**: DOJ civil forfeiture complaint against 127,271 BTC (~US$14-15bn), largest in DOJ history, held in 25 unhosted wallets; Chen indicted for wire-fraud conspiracy and money laundering tied to forced-labour scam compounds in Cambodia; OFAC and UK sanctions in parallel (UK also sanctioned Byex) — [TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/operation-prince-inside-the-global-effort-that-led-to-the-largest-forfeiture-in-us-history); [Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/prince-group-targeted-with-crypto-sanctions-for-global-pig-butchering-operations/); [Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2025/10/17/department-of-justice-seizes-about-15-billion-in-bitcoin-relating-to-pig-butchering-scheme/)

### Inferences
- The 2025 jump is mostly sanctions-evasion volume (A7A5/Garantex), not a proportional rise in fraud or theft; headline totals are heavily sensitive to which entities vendors label as sanctioned and to methodology revisions.
- Stablecoins dominate illicit flows, which is why issuer-level freeze powers (GENIUS Act, Tether freezes) and issuer AML rules have become central levers.
- Pig-butchering laundering runs through guarantee marketplaces (Haowang), payment firms and OTC desks; network-level tools (311 cutoffs, mass forfeiture) are now the primary US response.
