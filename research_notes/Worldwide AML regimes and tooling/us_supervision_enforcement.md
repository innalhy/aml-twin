## 1. Who supervises whom, and how a BSA/AML exam runs (including 2025-2026 changes)

### Takeaway
FinCEN administers the BSA and holds overall enforcement authority, but it delegates examination to the functional regulators:
- OCC, Fed, FDIC and NCUA for banks and credit unions
- SEC and FINRA for broker-dealers
- CFTC and NFA for futures firms
- IRS SB/SE for MSBs, casinos and other non-bank financial institutions
- State regulators (for example NYDFS, and multistate money-transmitter regulators coordinated through CSBS) as a parallel layer

Bank exams follow the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual. The exam is risk-focused: scoping, review of the bank's risk assessment, then testing of the program pillars and transactions. In 2025-2026 the exam approach changed in three ways:
- "Reputation risk" was removed from supervision, codified by OCC/FDIC final rule in April 2026.
- The FFIEC manual and 15 interagency guidance documents were scrubbed of reputation-risk references.
- The OCC introduced tailored minimum BSA exam procedures for community banks, effective February 2026.

The BSA/AML requirements themselves were not relaxed.

### Cited Findings
**Agency map**
- FinCEN has delegated BSA examination authority as follows. — [31 CFR 1010.810 (eCFR), not fetched this session](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.810)
  - Banks: federal functional regulators (OCC, Fed, FDIC, NCUA).
  - Broker-dealers and investment companies: SEC.
  - Futures commission merchants and introducing brokers: CFTC.
  - Most other financial institutions, including MSBs, casinos and card clubs: IRS.
- FinCEN keeps "overall authority for enforcement of the BSA". It investigates financial institutions, non-financial trades and businesses, and individuals, and it receives whistleblower tips. In FY24 it evaluated **127** new whistleblower tips, complaints and referrals. — [FinCEN Year in Review FY2024 (PDF)](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-08/FinCEN-Infographic-Public-2025-508.pdf)
- FINRA brings most broker-dealer AML cases in practice. One year-end 2025 commentary contrasts **four** enforcement actions from the traditional federal banking agencies in 2025 with FINRA's **20+** AML penalties. A separate source says FINRA AML enforcement rose about 50% in 2025. Both are secondary. — [AML RightSource](https://www.amlrightsource.com/resources/aml-conversations-navigating-the-aml-crossroads-2025-in-review-and-whats-next); [Compliance Corylated](https://www.compliancecorylated.com/news/finra-aml-enforcement-action-up-50-in-2025/)
- State money-transmitter regulators coordinate multistate BSA/AML exams and enforcement through CSBS. Example: Block/Cash App, $80m in January 2025 by 48 state regulators (see Section 5). — [CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/newsroom/state-regulators-issue-80-million-penalty-block-inc-cash-app-bsaaml-violations)
- **Federal Fed/OCC/FDIC overlap with BSA enforcement.** The Fed and FDIC both say they are running debanking reviews alongside the OCC. — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/industries/financial-services/library/our-take/debanking-fsoc-systemic-risk-regulatory-update-dec-2025.html)

**How a BSA/AML exam runs (FFIEC manual)**
- The bank program rule requires a written program, approved by the board, with these elements. — [31 CFR 1020.210 (eCFR), not fetched this session](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1020/subpart-B/section-1020.210)
  - A system of internal controls.
  - Independent testing.
  - A designated BSA compliance officer.
  - Training.
  - Risk-based customer due diligence. This "fifth pillar" was added by the 2016 CDD Rule.
- The OCC calls its Community Bank Procedures "supplementary guidance" for applying the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual. The full FFIEC manual should still be completed "as appropriate for the bank's unique risk profile" to address higher-risk areas. — [OCC Bulletin 2025-37](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-37.html); [Community Bank Procedures (PDF)](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-37a.pdf)
- **[IN FORCE]** OCC Bulletin 2025-37 (November 2025) set minimum BSA/AML exam procedures for community banks, for exams beginning **1 February 2026**. — [OCC Bulletin 2025-37](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-37.html); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/occ-updates-bsa-aml-examination-procedures-community-banks)
  - Examiners may rely on satisfactory independent testing to reach procedure conclusions.
  - Examiners may carry forward prior-cycle conclusions on the **Training** and **BSA Compliance Officer** pillars for one cycle if the risk profile has not changed significantly.
  - Other federal agencies had not issued comparable procedures.
- The FFIEC manual was revised in line with EO 14331 to remove references to reputation risk. Affected sections reportedly include the Introduction, Suspicious Activity Reporting, Payable Through Accounts, Electronic Banking, and Nondeposit Investment Products. **[UNVERIFIED]**: the date (reportedly February 2026) and section list come from a secondary summary. — [Moody's summary](https://www.moodys.com/web/en/us/kyc/resources/insights/occ-updates-bsa-aml-examination-procedures-for-us-community-banks.html)
- Earlier manual update: OCC Bulletin 2023-26 revised FFIEC sections on topics including foreign correspondent banking and private banking. — [OCC Bulletin 2023-26](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2023/bulletin-2023-26.html)

**Reputation risk removed from supervision**
- The Fed announced in June 2025 that it would end the use of reputational risk in its examination programs. — [Consumer Finance Monitor (Ballard Spahr)](https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2025/06/26/fed-to-end-use-of-reputational-risk-in-examination-programs/)
- **[FINAL]** The OCC and FDIC issued a final rule on 7 April 2026, published at 91 Fed. Reg. on 10 April 2026 and effective **9 June 2026**. It is codified at 12 CFR 4.91 (OCC) and 12 CFR 302.100 (FDIC). — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06947/prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk-by-regulators); [OCC final rule (PDF)](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-ia-2026-26a.pdf); [Winthrop](https://winthrop.com/bold-perspectives/occ-and-fdic-codify-the-prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk-in-supervision/); [Troutman CFS Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/04/occ-and-fdic-issue-reputational-risk-final-rule/)
  - Supervisors may not criticize or take adverse action "on the basis of reputation risk".
  - An anti-evasion clause bars using credit, operational or compliance risk as a pretext for reputation risk.
  - The rule leaves BSA/AML and OFAC obligations intact.
- **[PROPOSED]** The Fed proposed its own codifying rule on 23 February 2026, with comments due 27 April 2026. I found no confirmation of a final Fed rule. — [Global FinReg Blog](https://www.globalfinregblog.com/2026/04/us-banking-regulators-finalize-rule-eliminating-use-of-reputation-risk/)
- In **June 2026**, the three agencies reissued **15 interagency guidance documents** with reputation-risk references removed, stating that this "does not ... relax" safety-and-soundness expectations. — [Cooley](https://finsights.cooley.com/agencies-strip-reputation-risk-from-interagency-supervisory-guidance/); [Troutman](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/06/federal-banking-agencies-take-next-step-in-dismantling-reputation-risk-in-supervision-by-updating-interagency-guidance/); [OCC Bulletin 2026-23](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-23.html)
- **[PROPOSED]** The April 2026 FinCEN program NPRM would require banking regulators to give FinCEN at least **30 days' notice** before a "significant supervisory action". It would also limit maintenance-based actions to "significant or systemic" failures. This is the main structural change proposed for how BSA exam findings become enforcement. — [Gibson Dunn mid-year 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- Exam *scope* is narrowing for low-risk community banks (reliance on independent testing, carry-forward), and the *grounds* for criticism are narrowing (no reputation risk). The pillar framework and the obligations themselves are unchanged.
- For a training twin, a realistic exam flow is:
  1. Scoping, using the prior exam, the bank's risk assessment and the independent test.
  2. Risk assessment review.
  3. Pillar-by-pillar testing.
  4. Transaction testing in higher-risk areas.
  5. Findings, ranging from MRA, to formal agreement, to consent order or C&D, to a CMP.

## 2. NYDFS Part 504 (transaction monitoring and filtering program certification)

### Takeaway
3 NYCRR Part 504 (effective 2017, first certification in April 2018) requires NYDFS-regulated banks, BitLicensees and money transmitters to keep a risk-based transaction monitoring program and an OFAC filtering program. A Board Resolution or Senior Officer Compliance Finding must be filed each **15 April** for the prior calendar year. NYDFS enforces Part 504 together with its virtual currency and money transmitter rules. Recent orders target alert backlogs, delayed SARs and weak blockchain-analytics coverage.

### Cited Findings
- **[IN FORCE]** §504.4 requires each Regulated Institution to adopt and submit to the Superintendent a **Board Resolution or Senior Officer(s) Compliance Finding** (Attachment A) by **15 April each year**. Supporting records, schedules and data must be kept for **5 years**. — [3 NYCRR 504.4 (Justia, current through 24 Dec 2024)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-3/chapter-iii/subchapter-c/part-504/section-504-4)
- The first certification was due 16 April 2018, the next business day after 15 April. Documented weaknesses must be identified and made available to the Superintendent. — [BDO](https://www.bdo.com/insights/advisory/are-you-ready-for-april-15,-2018)
- Part 504 (AML transaction monitoring) is distinct from Part 500 (cybersecurity). The two are often confused because both certify on 15 April. — [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/are-you-ready-for-it-nydfs-annual-cybersecurity-certification-deadline-april-15)
- **Block, Inc. consent order (10 April 2025)** — [NYDFS consent order (PDF text)](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/enforcement-discipline/ea20250410-block)
  - Penalty and monitor: a **$40m** civil money penalty plus an **Independent Monitor** to assess compliance with the Virtual Currency Regulation, the Money Transmitter Regulation and **Part 504**, and to oversee remediation.
  - Alert backlog: about **18,000** in 2018, growing to **over 169,000** by 2020.
  - SAR delays: from February 2021 to September 2022, some SARs were filed more than a year after the alert. The average was **129 days** from alert to SAR and **70 days** from alert to case opening, against an internal procedure of a decision within 60 days.
  - Bitcoin monitoring: one vendor's system did not alert on Bitcoin transactions for a period, and thresholds were set above 0% without risk-based support.
  - Sanctions screening: customers were not rescreened against updated OFAC lists.
  - Other failures: KYC gaps let bad actors re-enter the platform. Block self-identified more than 8,000 problematic accounts in 2022.
  - Scale: about **54m** monthly transacting actives; total assets grew from $15.02bn (2021) to $34.06bn (2023).
- **Paxos Trust (7 August 2025):** $48.5m in total, made up of a **$26.5m** penalty and **$22m** in mandated AML compliance investment. The case involved about $1.6bn of Binance-related transactions linked to illicit actors, darknet markets and sanctioned entities. Secondary sources. — [Willkie Compliance Concourse](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/nydfs-reaches-48-5-million-settlement-with-paxos-trust-company-to-resolve-aml-deficiencies/); [Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2025/paxos-settlement-sets-precedent-for-state-oversight-of-crypto)
- **Coinbase (earlier, January 2023):** NYDFS found a failure to maintain an effective transaction monitoring program in violation of Part 504. The settlement was reported at $100m. Secondary source; the date is from background knowledge. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-york-state-department-financial-services-and-coinbase-reach-100-million)
- NYDFS guidance of 28 April 2022 expects virtual currency entities to use blockchain analytics and to show tailored monitoring coverage, for example for mixers. The Block order cited this guidance. — [NYDFS blockchain analytics guidance](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il20220428_guidance_use_blockchain_analytics)

### Inferences
- Part 504 is the one US regime that makes a named senior officer or the board personally *certify* the monitoring and filtering program each year. That makes it a natural "attestation" event for a training twin, for example a mock certification gate with evidence.
- Typical Part 504 enforcement facts are an **alert backlog plus a slow alert-to-SAR cycle plus model and threshold choices with no documented rationale**. All three are measurable twin-state variables.

## 3. OFAC sanctions compliance and how it differs from AML monitoring

### Takeaway
OFAC administers IEEPA/TWEA-based sanctions programs, including the SDN list and the 50% rule. Civil liability is strict: no knowledge or intent is required. Penalties follow the Enforcement Guidelines, under which the base penalty depends on whether a case is egregious and whether it was voluntarily self-disclosed. The limitations period is now 10 years. 2025 penalties totalled about $262-266m, dominated by GVA Capital ($216m). Sanctions screening is a real-time, list-matching control that must block or reject a transaction before it is processed. AML monitoring is retrospective, behavioural, and ends in a SAR.

### Cited Findings
- **Enforcement Guidelines (31 CFR Part 501 App. A):** — [Cleary Gottlieb (2009 guidelines summary)](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/-/media/organize-archive/cgsh/files/publication-pdfs/ofac-publishes-new-economic-sanctions-enforcement-guidelines.pdf); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/a/web/6085/aogQv/091109_ofac-publishes-final-rule-on-economic-sanctions.pdf)
  - Egregious and not voluntarily self-disclosed: base penalty equals the statutory maximum.
  - Egregious and voluntarily self-disclosed: base penalty is 50% of the statutory maximum.
  - Substantial cooperation without self-disclosure generally reduces the base by 25-40%.
- The **50% rule**: entities owned 50% or more, in aggregate, by SDNs are themselves blocked even if not listed. The search returned only a law-firm explainer, and strict-liability treatment was not separately sourced. — [Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/files/34423_OFACs_50_Percent_Rule_Penalty.pdf)
- **Statute of limitations:** extended from 5 to **10 years** by the April 2024 21st Century Peace through Strength Act. **[UNVERIFIED]** against statutory text this session. — [Hogan Lovells](https://biopreviewuat.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/ofac-issues-statute-of-limitations-guidance-for-us-sanctions-violations-and-recordkeeping)
- **2025 penalties:** — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/five-key-takeaways-from-2025-us-sanctions-enforcement); [Polixis](https://polixis.com/news-and-press-releases/sanctions-2025-in-review-ofacs-enforcement-year-at-a-glance/); [Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2025/july/lessons-from-the-first-four-ofac-sanctions-enforcement-actions-under-the-second-trump-administration/)
  - **GVA Capital** (June 2025): **$215,988,868** penalty, not a settlement, for managing an investment for a sanctioned Russian oligarch. OFAC found the conduct egregious and not self-disclosed and imposed the statutory maximum. It was the largest OFAC penalty since Binance in 2023 and about 81% of the 2025 total.
  - 2025 total: 13-14 actions, about $262m-$265.7m depending on counting.
- **Interactive Brokers** (15 July 2025): about **$11.8m** for **12,367** apparent violations from July 2016 to January 2024, covering Iran, Cuba, Syria, Crimea, Russia and Chinese military companies. The case was treated as non-egregious and voluntarily self-disclosed. Control failures included gaps in screening customer addresses and IP addresses. — [IBKR FY2025 10-Q (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1381197/000138119725000158/ibkr-20250930x10q.htm); [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/interactive-brokers-to-pay-11-8-million-for-sanctions-violations-across-multiple-jurisdictions-programs); [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102kv34/beyond-the-fine-what-financial-gatekeepers-must-learn-from-interactive-brokers)
- **Other actions:** — [OFAC Recent Actions](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/enforcement-actions)
  - Unicat Catalysts: $3.88m (June 2025).
  - IPI Partners: $11.49m (December 2025), secondary.
  - Gracetown: $7.14m (December 2025), secondary.
  - An individual's Syria-related settlement: $3.777m (February 2026).
  - IMG Academy: $1.72m (February 2026).
- **2026:** OFAC's page listed three actions through 17 March 2026 totalling about $6.6m. A tracker reports **Adani Enterprises at $275m** and 7 cases totalling $284m in 2026. **[UNVERIFIED]**: no OFAC primary source was found for Adani. — [SanctionsLookup tracker](https://sanctionslookup.com/ofac/enforcement/2026)
- AML monitoring and sanctions screening collide in practice. The Block order shows both failures in one firm: an AML alert backlog and no OFAC rescreening. That was a violation of OFAC reporting rules (31 CFR 501.604). — [NYDFS Block order](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/enforcement-discipline/ea20250410-block)

### Inferences
- **Screening vs monitoring** (my synthesis from the above):
  - Screening is pre-transaction. It does name, entity and ownership matching against SDN and other lists, including derived 50% ownership, plus geography and IP. The outcome is block or reject, with a report to OFAC within 10 business days. Liability is strict, so a single miss is a violation.
  - AML monitoring is post-transaction. It looks for behavioural patterns, and its outcome is a SAR within 30 days. It is judged on whether the program is reasonable.
  - A twin should model the two as separate queues with different SLAs and different failure semantics.
- Cartel FTO designations (Section 4) moved cartel-linked parties onto the SDN list. That merges counter-narcotics AML risk into strict-liability sanctions screening.

## 4. Enforcement mechanics and the 2025-2026 policy shift

### Takeaway
US AML enforcement is layered:
- **Supervisory**: MRAs, formal agreements, consent orders and C&Ds, CMPs, asset caps and growth restrictions.
- **FinCEN civil**: CMPs, including against individuals.
- **DOJ criminal**: plea, DPA or NPA, monitorships and forfeiture.
- **State**: NYDFS and multistate CSBS actions.

In 2025-2026, DOJ's May 2025 Galeotti memo did three things:
- made declination near-automatic for self-reporting companies
- capped resolution terms at 3 years and tightened monitor use
- reprioritised to cartels, TCOs, sanctions and fraud

The administration designated cartels as FTOs in February 2025, which creates material-support exposure. FinCEN first used FEND Off Fentanyl Act §2313a orders against three Mexican FIs and lowered CTR-like thresholds for southwest-border MSBs through GTOs. The first GTO was partly enjoined.

### Cited Findings
**Tools**
- **Formal agreement with growth restriction.** The OCC's September 2024 Wells Fargo agreement carried no fine. The bank must obtain a **prior written determination of no supervisory objection** before offering new products or entering new markets with medium or high BSA/AML or OFAC inherent risk. — [OCC NR 2024-99](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2024/nr-occ-2024-99.html); [Compliance Week](https://www.complianceweek.com/regulatory-enforcement/wells-fargo-agrees-with-occ-to-shore-up-bsa/aml-sanctions-program-deficiencies/35371.article)
- **Asset caps.** The Fed's 2018 Wells Fargo cap of $1.95tn (a governance and sales-practices matter, not AML) was lifted in 2025, which shows how long caps last. The TD Bank US asset cap is $434bn, covered elsewhere. — [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fed-lifts-wells-fargo-asset-cap/749736/)
- **Independent consultants and monitors** are imposed by state and federal orders:
  - NYDFS Block monitor. — [NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/enforcement-discipline/ea20250410-block)
  - Multistate Block independent consultant: report within 9 months, remediation within 12 months. — [CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/newsroom/state-regulators-issue-80-million-penalty-block-inc-cash-app-bsaaml-violations)
  - OCC Bank of America independent consultant and SAR lookback. — [OCC NR 2024-140](https://occ-gov.cms.awsdev.treasury.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2024/nr-occ-2024-140/)
- **NPAs.** Brink's (DOJ NPA signed 31 January 2025, alongside a FinCEN consent order). EagleBank (DOJ NPA, 30 June 2026). — [Brink's 10-Q (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78890/000007889025000154/bco-20250331.htm); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

**Individual liability**
- **Thomas Haider**, former MoneyGram CCO: FinCEN assessed **$1m** in December 2014. The court held that the BSA's civil penalty "may be imposed on corporate officers and employees". The case settled in May 2017 for **$250,000** and a **3-year bar** from compliance roles at money transmitters. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/fincen-assesses-penalty-former-moneygram-compliance-officer); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/former-chief-compliance-officer-can-be-held-personally-liable-for-aml-violations)
- **Michael LaFontaine**, former U.S. Bank Chief Operational Risk Officer: **$450,000** on 4 March 2020 for failing to staff AML compliance adequately and for capping SAR alerts despite regulator warnings. The bank had been penalised $185m in 2018. — [Post & Schell](https://postschell.com/insights/fincen-imposes-civil-money-penalty-former-bank-officer-failing-adequately); [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/2020/03/05/fincen-imposes-civil-money-penalty-on-former-bank-officer-for-failing-to-adequately-staff-banks-aml-compliance-function-and-for-capping-banks-suspicious-activity-reports/)
- **Willfulness standard** for individuals: reckless disregard or willful blindness is enough. — [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/financial-services-law/raising-the-stakes-for-aml-compliance-officers-co)
- **EagleBank:** the former CEO and Chairman was fined $521,000 and permanently barred (Fed and SEC, 2022). Senior executives overrode compliance's attempts to close accounts. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

**DOJ policy (12 May 2025 Galeotti memo)** — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/05/us-doj-unveils-white-collar-enforcement-priorities); [Paul Weiss 2025 review](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/department-of-justice-corporate-enforcement-2025-year-in-review); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/doj-announces-new-policies-and-priorities-in-prosecution)
- **Corporate Enforcement Policy:** a company that self-reports, cooperates and remediates, with no aggravating circumstances, *will* receive a declination. The earlier policy only gave a presumption. Self-reporting within 120 days of an internal whistleblower report keeps declination eligibility.
- **Corporate Whistleblower Awards Pilot:** expanded to cartel and TCO money laundering, material support to terrorism, sanctions, trade, tariff and customs fraud, and procurement fraud.
- **Priorities:** these include sanctions evasion and money laundering enabling cartels, TCOs, FTOs and hostile states. Financial institutions are named as "gatekeepers".
- **Resolution terms:** no longer than 3 years except in rare cases. Monitor selection was narrowed.
- **April 2026 reorganisation:** DOJ created a National Fraud Enforcement Division, which absorbs the Money Laundering and Asset Recovery Section (MNF). — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

**Cartels as FTOs and material support**

- Providing money or financial services to these groups may be material support under **18 USC 2339B**. Institutions should screen for agents acting directly or indirectly for designees. — [Bracewell](https://bracewell.com/resources/trump-administration-efforts-to-eliminate-cartels-pose-heightened-risk-for-financial-institutions); [Hughes Hubbard](https://hugheshubbard.com/news/designation-of-criminal-cartels-as-foreign-terrorist-organizations-increases-compliance-risks-for-companies-us-or-not-operating-in-mexico-and-greater-latin-america)
- **[IN FORCE, first use] FEND Off Fentanyl Act §2313a orders (25 June 2025)** — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/30/2025-11993/imposition-of-special-measure-prohibiting-certain-transmittals-of-funds-involving-cibanco-sa); [Bracewell](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/fincens-cartel-crackdown-on-three-mexican-financial-institutions-implications-for-us-financial-transactions); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/ongoing-developments-related-to-fincens-cibanco-order)
  - Targets: CIBanco, Intercam and Vector, named as of "primary money laundering concern".
  - Effect: covered US institutions must stop all fund transmittals to or from them, including CVC and indirect flows.
  - Process: §2313a lets FinCEN act by order, without §311-style notice and comment.
  - Stated basis includes a CIBanco employee helping open an account to launder $10m for a Gulf Cartel member.
  - Effective dates were later extended.

**Southwest border GTOs**
- **[ENJOINED in part, expired]** The March 2025 GTO covered **30 ZIP codes** in Texas and California and lowered MSB cash reporting to **$200**, from about 14 April 2025. — [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-07-17/ninth-circuit-affirms-preliminary-injunction-of-fincens-reporting-order-for-businesses-along-the-southern-border/); [Bradley](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2026/08/9th-circ-rule-block-unlikely-to-defer-fincen-cartel-focus); [Money Laundering News](https://www.moneylaunderingnews.com/2025/04/fincens-southwest-border-geographic-targeting-order-faces-challenges-in-texas-and-southern-california/)
  - Challenges were brought in W.D. Tex. (Texas Association for MSBs; *Valuta Corp. v. FinCEN*) and in California.
  - The Ninth Circuit affirmed a preliminary injunction in *Novedades y Servicios v. FinCEN* on 13-14 July 2026 (split panel; Koh for the majority, Lee dissenting).
- **[IN FORCE, successor orders]** The September 2025 GTO raised the floor to **$1,000** (for $1,000-$10,000 transactions) and added Arizona. A March 2026 GTO extended coverage to New Mexico. — [FinCEN Sept 2025 GTO (PDF)](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-09/September-2025-SWB-GTO-Order-FINAL-508C.pdf); [Bradley](https://www.bradley.com/insights/publications/2026/08/9th-circ-rule-block-unlikely-to-defer-fincen-cartel-focus)
- **January 2026:** a Minnesota GTO for banks and money transmitters, plus four MSB notices of investigation, tied to the federal child-nutrition fraud cases. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- The 2025-2026 pattern is **lighter on program-technicality exams and heavier on targeted national-security tools**: FTOs, §2313a, GTOs and fraud. For banks, the cartel FTO designations turn some former SAR-only typologies into potential sanctions and material-support exposure.
- The 3-year cap and the declination promise raise the value of self-reporting. Under the proposed FinCEN whistleblower program, internal escalation timing (the 120-day window) becomes a modelled decision point.

### Gaps
- I found no reported bank criminal case under §2339B tied to cartel FTOs, and no Anti-Terrorism Act civil suits against banks over cartels.
- I did not confirm whether the government sought en banc or Supreme Court review of the Ninth Circuit GTO ruling. The district injunction's scope (named plaintiffs or broader) is unconfirmed.
- I found no FinCEN individual-penalty actions after 2020. Absence was not exhaustively checked.
- I did not retrieve current §2313a order status for CIBanco, Intercam or Vector (for example license transfers or revocations).

## 7. The 2025-2026 policy debate: debanking EO, threshold reform, FinCEN priorities

### Takeaway
The deregulatory agenda has three strands:
- **Debanking**: EO 14331 (7 August 2025) led to removal of reputation risk, OCC findings against the nine largest banks (December 2025), SBA orders to its lenders, and a 2026 DOJ (D.C. USAO) subpoena probe.
- **Threshold modernization**: bills only. The STREAMLINE Act and H.R. 1799 are in the companion file.
- **FinCEN priorities**: no replacement for the June 2021 National AML/CFT Priorities was found. The April 2026 program NPRM would hard-wire the existing priorities into risk assessments. Treasury budget documents frame cartels, Iran, terrorist financing and cyber as the operative focus.

### Cited Findings
- **[IN FORCE] EO 14331, "Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans" (7 August 2025):** defines "politicized or unlawful debanking", directs regulators to remove reputation risk and identify offending institutions, and contemplates fines and referrals to the Attorney General. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/); [Cooley](https://finsights.cooley.com/agencies-strip-reputation-risk-from-interagency-supervisory-guidance/)
- **OCC preliminary findings (10 December 2025)** on the nine largest national banks: — [OCC NR 2025-123](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-123.html); [Troutman](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2025/12/occ-issues-preliminary-findings-on-its-supervisory-review-of-large-banks-debanking-activities/); [Spencer Fane](https://www.spencerfane.com/insight/the-debanking-minefield-navigating-fair-access-in-2026/)
  - From 2020 to 2023 they treated customers unequally based on lawful business activities, with sector restrictions or escalations for oil and gas, coal, firearms, private prisons, tobacco and e-cigarettes, and adult entertainment.
  - The OCC is reviewing about 100,000 complaints and possible coordination with law enforcement on political views.
  - It plans referrals to the AG for religion-based ECOA violations.
  - Findings will feed into licensing, merger and CRA decisions.
- **SBA (August 2025):** ordered its lending-network banks to stop debanking and report compliance. In October 2025 it released a streamlined form for community banks. — [ICBA](https://www.icba.org/w/occ-releases-findings-on-large-bank-debanking-activities%C2%A0)
- **D.C. U.S. Attorney's Office:** reportedly subpoenaed several large banks over debanking (reported 10 June 2026), coordinating with the OCC and evaluating FIRREA among other laws. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)
- **AML tension:** the OCC/FDIC reputation-risk rule keeps BSA/AML and OFAC obligations intact. — [Winthrop](https://winthrop.com/bold-perspectives/occ-and-fdic-codify-the-prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk-in-supervision/)
- **FinCEN priorities:** the June 2021 list remains operative. It covers corruption, cybercrime (including virtual currency), terrorist financing, fraud, TCO activity, drug trafficking organization activity, human trafficking and smuggling, and proliferation financing. FinCEN committed to update it at least every four years, which would be due around mid-2025. No update was found. — [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2021/06/fincen-issues-national-anti-money-laundering-priorities); [FinCEN NBFI statement](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-statement-non-bank-financial-institutions-issuance-amlcft-national)
- **FinCEN FY2026 budget justification** frames the mission around the "total elimination of international cartels", border security, "maximum pressure" on Iran, terrorist financing and cyber-enabled illicit activity. — [Treasury FinCEN FY2026 CJ (PDF)](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/266/11.-FinCEN-FY-2026-CJ.pdf)
- **Fraud pivot (2026):**
  - EO 14395, Task Force to Eliminate Fraud (16 March 2026).
  - A cyber-fraud EO (6 March 2026).
  - DOJ Scam Center Strike Force.
  - FinCEN 314(b) guidance (June 2026) treating fraud as a specified unlawful activity.

  — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Debanking pulls against de-risking.** Banks face pressure *not* to exit customers for reputational reasons, while still being liable for BSA, OFAC and now FTO material-support failures. Exit decisions therefore need individualised, documented, risk-based rationales tied to financial crime risk, not perception.
- **Priorities in practice.** The 2021 list is formally still in place, but enforcement priorities in practice are cartels/FTOs, Iran, fraud (scam centres, benefits fraud) and CMLNs. That is narrower and more national-security-led than the 2021 list.

### Gaps
- I found no final OCC, Fed or FDIC debanking reports after December 2025, and no enforcement action yet taken under EO 14331.
- I did not confirm whether FinCEN published updated AML/CFT priorities in 2025-2026.
- I did not confirm the status of the threshold bills in October 2026 beyond the companion file. No enactment was found.
