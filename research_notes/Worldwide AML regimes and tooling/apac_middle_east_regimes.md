# AML regimes of major Asia-Pacific and Middle East jurisdictions (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-13. These notes come from web searches run on that date. Many results were law-firm, vendor, or news summaries rather than primary texts, and each finding names its source. Items labelled "background, not verified this session" come from the researcher's prior knowledge. Do not cite them without checking.

## Hong Kong: AMLO, supervisors, JFIU/STREAMS 2, FINEST, SVF/VASP/stablecoin regimes, FATF, anti-fraud

### Takeaway
Hong Kong was the first Asia-Pacific jurisdiction rated "compliant and effective overall" in the 4th FATF round (2019). Since 2023 it has built bank-to-bank sharing in stages. FINEST launched in June 2023 for corporate accounts. The Banking (Amendment) Ordinance 2025 took effect on 3 Nov 2025 and extended sharing to individual (mule) accounts. Police Scameter data now feeds bank network analytics. STREAMS 2 has been the only STR channel since 2 Feb 2026. The Stablecoins Ordinance took effect on 1 Aug 2025, and the first licences were issued on 10 Apr 2026.

### Cited Findings
**FATF 2019 evaluation and follow-up**
- The FATF and the Asia/Pacific Group (APG) assessed Hong Kong jointly, and the MER was published on 4 Sep 2019. The government said the system was "compliant and effective overall", the first such result in Asia-Pacific — [FATF Hong Kong page](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/countries/detail/Hong-Kong-China.html); [HSF summary](https://www.hsf.com/insights/2019-09/fatf-and-apg-publish-long-awaited-mutual-evaluation-report-on-hong-kong)
- The 2019 MER rated 36 of the 40 Recommendations Compliant or Largely Compliant. The evaluation took 18 months and included a three-week onsite visit in late 2018 — [Sovereign Group](https://www.sovereigngroup.com/news/fatf-finds-hong-kong-aml-ctf-regime-to-be-compliant/); [LegCo paper FA 2019](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr18-19/english/panels/fa/papers/facb1-1347-1-e.pdf)
- Hong Kong is in **regular follow-up**. The First Regular Follow-up Report (Feb 2023) shows C on 11 Recommendations, LC on 25 and PC on 4 — [FATF FUR 2023 PDF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/fur/Follow-Up-Report-Hong-Kong-China-2023.pdf.coredownload.pdf); [HK Gov press release 17 Feb 2023](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202302/17/P2023021700306.htm)
- 5th round: the FATF says the new round focuses on effectiveness relative to risk. FSRBs will adopt their first 5th-round MERs by end-2026 or early 2027 — [FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/fight-illicit-finance.html). No official date for Hong Kong's 5th-round onsite visit was found (see Gaps).

**JFIU and STREAMS 2**
- The legacy STREAMS system shut down at midnight on 28 Jan 2026. During a blackout until 2 Feb 2026, no online STR filing was possible. From **2 Feb 2026, STREAMS 2 is the only STR submission channel**. STREAMS 2 adds automation and analytics for STR analysis — [IA/JFIU AML seminar, Oct 2025](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/supervision/antimoney_laundering/files/20251027_AML_Seminar_Eng_JFIU_MLEC.pdf) (as summarised by search; also [Companies Registry speech Nov 2025](https://www.cr.gov.hk/en/publications/docs/Speech_20251120_PP_e.pdf))
- A JFIU presentation reported 137,736 STRs received in 2025, as of September 2025 — [IA/JFIU seminar deck](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/supervision/antimoney_laundering/files/20251027_AML_Seminar_Eng_JFIU_MLEC.pdf) (the period this figure covers should be checked against the deck)

**FINEST and bank-to-bank sharing**
- FINEST (Financial Intelligence Evaluation Sharing Tool) launched on **20 June 2023**. The HKMA, HKAB and Hong Kong Police announced it, and HKAB developed it under HKMA guidance. It was one of five joint anti-deception initiatives in 2023 — [HKMA press release 20 Jun 2023](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2023/06/20230620-5/)
- The pilot covered five D-SIBs: BOCHK, Standard Chartered HK, HSBC, Hang Seng and ICBC (Asia). It shared information only on **corporate** accounts suspected of fraud-related ML. The plan was to add banks, personal accounts and other typologies such as TBML — [Conventus Law](https://conventuslaw.com/report/hong-kong-launch-of-bank-to-bank-information-sharing-platform-finest/)
- By April 2025, ten banks shared through FINEST. An upgraded platform was expected by end-2025 — [HKMA press release 10 Apr 2025](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/04/20250410-7/); [fintechnews.hk](https://fintechnews.hk/33193/security/financial-crime-hong-kong-measures/)
- **Banking (Amendment) Ordinance 2025** (Ord. No. 23 of 2025) passed LegCo on 4 Jun 2025 and was gazetted on 13 Jun 2025. It creates a voluntary gateway for banks to share information on corporate *and individual* accounts through HKMA-designated secure platforms when ML/TF/PF is suspected, with safe-harbour protection. Officials said about 90% of fraud mule accounts are individual accounts, which FINEST did not cover — [news.gov.hk 4 Jun 2025](https://news.gov.hk/eng/2025/06/20250604/20250604_162550_541.html); [Ordinance text](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr2025/english/ord/2025ord023-e.pdf)
- The commencement notice was gazetted on 5 Sep 2025 and appointed **3 Nov 2025** as the commencement date — [HK Gov press release 5 Sep 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/05/P2025090500211.htm)

**Anti-fraud and mule accounts: Scameter and ADCC**
- April 2025 HKMA/HKPF/HKAB measures: Police share account numbers, phone numbers and emails from the **Scameter** database with banks. Banks combine this with **network analytics** to find and share further mule networks — [HKMA 10 Apr 2025](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/04/20250410-7/)
- Scameter is a police anti-fraud search engine launched in 2022. Since 2023, banks have shown pre-transaction alerts when an FPS proxy ID is high-risk on Scameter — [fintechnews.hk](https://fintechnews.hk/33193/security/financial-crime-hong-kong-measures/)
- In 2024 there were 44,480 deception cases and 10,496 arrests for deception or ML, including about 7,700 people who sold or lent their accounts. RTHK gives about 8,600 for account lenders, a figure that conflicts — [fintechnews.hk](https://fintechnews.hk/33193/security/financial-crime-hong-kong-measures/); [RTHK 10 Apr 2025](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1799754-20250410.htm)

**SVF, VASP and stablecoin regimes**
- The HKMA regulates stored value facilities under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/de/perspectives/2022/01/hong-kong-monetary-authority-on-cryptoassets-and-stablecoins)
- The SFC opened a voluntary virtual-asset trading platform licensing regime in Nov 2019. A 2022 AMLO amendment bill created a mandatory VASP licensing regime — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/hong-kong-introduces-licensing-regime-for-virtual-asset-services-providers/)
- The FSTB and SFC consulted on new VA dealing and VA custody licensing regimes, with the consultation closing in Aug 2025. Under the proposal, the SFC sets standards and the HKMA is the frontline regulator for banks and SFC licensees offering these services. Further consultations followed in Dec 2025 — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/consultation-closes-new-licensing-regimes-virtual-asset-dealing/); [Withers](https://www.withersworldwide.com/en-gb/insight/read/aspire-to-deliver-sfc’s-crypto-roadmap-review-and-next-regulatory-milestones)
- The **Stablecoins Ordinance** took effect on **1 Aug 2025** and licenses issuers of fiat-referenced stablecoins. The HKMA received 36 first-round applications. The **first two licences** were granted on **10 Apr 2026** to HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial, a JV of Standard Chartered HK, HKT and Animoca Brands. Licensed issuers need full reserve backing in HQLA and redemption within one business day — [Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hkma-issues-first-stablecoin-issuer-licences/); [RTHK 29 Jul 2025](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1815690-20250729.htm); [AsiaAsset](https://www.asiaasset.com/analysis/hong-kong-sets-high-bar-for-stablecoin-issuance-with-first-licences/). The sources disagree on the Cap. number (656 vs 566). Cap. 656 is more likely, because Cap. 566 is an unrelated existing ordinance (background, not verified this session).

### Inferences
- Hong Kong's data-sharing model has moved in stages. It went from police-to-bank sharing (Scameter feeds), to bank-to-bank sharing for corporates (FINEST 2023), to a statutory gateway that includes individuals (Nov 2025). This makes it a useful "threading" example for a digital twin, because external events (police intel, peer-bank alerts) update a case's state.
- Background, not verified this session: AMLO is Cap. 615. The supervisors under it are the HKMA (AIs, SVF licensees), SFC (licensed corporations, VATPs), IA (insurers), Customs & Excise (MSOs, DPMS registration since 2023), and CR (TCSPs). The JFIU is jointly run by the Police and Customs. The VATP licensing regime under AMLO Part 5B took effect on 1 Jun 2023. The ADCC was set up in 2017 and runs the 18222 hotline.

### Gaps
- No official date for Hong Kong's 5th-round APG/FATF onsite visit or plenary. Check the APG assessment calendar and FSTB announcements.
- Not confirmed: which designated platform replaced or upgraded FINEST after 3 Nov 2025, and how many banks now participate.
- No primary source checked for the AMLO Cap. 615 supervisor list, the ADCC founding date, or the VATP 1 Jun 2023 commencement. These are background only.
- Not confirmed whether the VA dealing and custody bills were introduced or passed in 2026.

## Singapore: MAS Notices, STRO, COSMIC, S$3bn case and IMC reforms, 2024–2026 law changes

### Takeaway
Singapore's August 2023 Fujian-gang case led to more than S$3bn in seizures and ten convictions. An Inter-Ministerial Committee was formed in Nov 2023 and reported on 4 Oct 2024. Laws passed in 2024 tightened controls on gatekeepers (CSPs) and casinos. COSMIC went live on 1 Apr 2024 with six banks.

### Cited Findings
- **COSMIC** (Collaborative Sharing of ML/TF Information & Cases) launched on **1 Apr 2024**. It was co-developed with, and initially limited to, **DBS, OCBC, UOB, Citibank, HSBC and Standard Chartered**. It started with three risks: misuse of legal persons, trade-finance abuse and proliferation financing. Sharing is allowed only when a customer profile shows potential criminal behaviour. The legal basis is amendments to the Financial Services and Markets Act, which took effect the same day. The first phase was expected to run about two years — [OpenGov Asia](https://opengovasia.com/singapore-expands-financial-crime-information-sharing-network/); [Rajah & Tann](https://www.rajahtannasia.com/?p=133999); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/mas-introduces-cosmic-to-tackle-money-laundering-and-terrorism-financing). Sources disagree on whether the FSMA is the 2022 or 2023 Act.
- MAS has reported that COSMIC sharing led participating banks to file more STRs. This is MAS's own claim as relayed by the press — [Fintech News SG](https://fintechnews.sg/135520/security/mas-cosmic/)
- **The S$3bn case**: In Aug 2023 police seized, or obtained prohibition-of-disposal orders over, more than S$3bn in assets. Ten people were arrested and convicted. The **IMC**, chaired by Minister Indranee Rajah, was set up in Nov 2023 and published its report on **4 Oct 2024** — [MOF IMC report PDF](https://www.mof.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/news-and-publications/press-releases/2024/imc-aml-report.pdf); [Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/sg/perspectives/articles/29168/sgkh-inter-ministerial-committee-publishes-recommendations-to-strengthen-s-anti-money-laundering-framework)
- The IMC reviewed five areas: misuse of corporate structures, FI controls and collaboration, gatekeepers (CSPs, real estate agents, precious stones and metals dealers), centralised cross-agency monitoring ("sensemaking"), and enforcement tools. Its recommendations fall under three pillars: prevention, detection and enforcement — [PwC SG](https://www.pwc.com/sg/en/services/risk/guidance-on-the-singapore-inter-ministerial-aml-paper.html); [Kapronasia](https://kapronasia.com/insight/blogs/banking-research/asia-banking-research/singapore-rolls-out-new-anti-money-laundering-recommendations)
- The **Corporate Service Providers Bill** passed on 2 Jul 2024, with criminal liability and fines up to S$100k per AML breach. Nominee directors acting by way of business must now be arranged through registered CSPs — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/singapore-passes-corporate-service-providers-bill/)
- The **Anti-Money Laundering and Other Matters Bill** passed on 6 Aug 2024. It strengthens law-enforcement powers and the handling of seized property. It also amends the Casino Control Act to lower the casino CDD threshold from S$10,000 cash (or S$5,000 deposits) to **S$4,000** — [Rahmat Lim / Allen & Gledhill](https://www.rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/28899/anti-money-laundering-and-other-matters-bill-enhancing-amlcft-regime-passed-key-takeaways-from-second-reading)

### Inferences
- COSMIC differs from Hong Kong's FINEST and the Banking (Amendment) Ordinance. It is a MAS-operated, statute-backed platform focused on corporate and trade risks, while Hong Kong's effort is industry-built (HKAB) and focused on fraud and mule accounts.
- Background, not verified this session: MAS Notice 626 sets AML/CFT requirements for banks. STRO is the FIU within the Commercial Affairs Department, and STRs go through its SONAR system, which is bespoke and not goAML. Singapore's last FATF MER (2016) is due to be followed by a 5th-round evaluation.

### Gaps
- The search did not confirm which 2025–2026 amendments followed the IMC report, their commencement dates, or the AMLOM Act's commencement.
- COSMIC expansion beyond the six founding banks by 2026 is not confirmed.
- No primary source checked for MAS Notice 626, STRO/SONAR details, or Singapore's FATF timetable.

## Mainland China: revised AML Law (2025), PBOC/CAMLMAC, reporting thresholds, beneficial ownership, FATF

### Takeaway
China's revised AML Law was passed on 8 Nov 2024 and took effect on 1 Jan 2025. It adds extraterritorial reach and explicit beneficial-ownership checks. A separate PBOC/SAMR beneficial-ownership filing regime has applied since 1 Nov 2024. The large-cash reporting threshold remains RMB 50,000 (or US$10,000 equivalent), as set in the 2016 rules. China's 2019 FATF MER was critical of DNFBP coverage, BO, and PEPs.

### Cited Findings
- The NPC passed the revised **Anti-Money Laundering Law** on **8 Nov 2024**, effective **1 Jan 2025**. It largely codifies existing PBOC rules, adds **extraterritorial jurisdiction** over foreign ML/TF that threatens China's sovereignty, security or financial order, and requires FIs to verify **beneficial ownership** and report discrepancies — [Rödl & Partner](https://roedl.com/insights/china-anti-money-laundering-law-revised); [Han Kun](https://hankunlaw.com/en/portal/article/index/cid/8/id/14762); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/chn/legal-updates/china-amends-its-anti-money-laundering-law)
- The **BOI Measures** (beneficial ownership information), issued jointly by the PBOC and SAMR on 29 Apr 2024, took effect on 1 Nov 2024. Pre-existing entities had to file by 1 Nov 2025 — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/0bcfa5a3/pboc-releases-consultation-draft-on-beneficial)
- On 11 Oct 2025 the PBOC released a consultation draft of measures on how financial institutions identify beneficial owners. Its finalisation was not confirmed — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/0bcfa5a3/pboc-releases-consultation-draft-on-beneficial)
- **Large-value thresholds**: The 2016 PBOC measures, effective 1 Jul 2017, require FIs to report large-value transactions to **CAMLMAC**. This includes single or same-day cumulative cash transactions of **RMB 50,000** or more, lowered from RMB 200,000 (or about US$10,000 in foreign currency) — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/278d4460/china-financial-services-regulation-increased-supervision-of-large-sum-and-suspicious-transactions); [KPMG China Tax Alert 2017](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/cn/pdf/en/2017/01/china-tax-alert-02.pdf)
- **PBOC Order No. 10 of 2025** was issued on 30 Sep 2025 and took effect on 1 Dec 2025. It amended the 2016 reporting measures and the 2021 AML supervision measures to align them with the new law. The excerpts reviewed show no threshold change — [gov.cn gazette](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12426/202511/content_7049744.html)
- **FATF 2019 MER**: It said China should extend preventive measures and STR filing to DNFBPs and online lenders, and introduce domestic PEP requirements. BO gaps limited both FI preventive measures and the FIU. R.7 was rated NC, and R.3, 6, 8, 12, 15, 16 and 18 were rated PC. A 2022 FUR reviewed technical-compliance progress — [FATF MER China 2019](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Mer-china-2019.html); [HSF summary](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/fsrandcorpcrime/2019-05/fatf-releases-fourth-round-mutual-evaluation-report-of-china)

### Inferences
- The 2025 law and the BOI filing regime appear aimed at the 2019 MER's BO findings, ahead of China's 5th-round evaluation.
- Background, not verified this session: CAMLMAC (China Anti-Money Laundering Monitoring and Analysis Center) is the PBOC's FIU. China's reporting runs on a bespoke PBOC system, not goAML. China was placed in enhanced follow-up after 2019.

### Gaps
- China's follow-up category and its R.24/R.25 ratings were not confirmed from a primary source.
- Not confirmed whether the Oct 2025 FI BO identification measures were finalised in 2026.

## UAE: grey-listing, goAML, Decree-Law 20/2018 → 10/2025, real estate/DPMS, Executive Office

### Takeaway
The FATF grey-listed the UAE in March 2022 and removed it on 23 Feb 2024, a stay of under two years. Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 replaced Decree-Law 20/2018 from 14 Oct 2025. The UAE FIU receives STRs through UNODC's goAML.

### Cited Findings
- The UAE was grey-listed in Mar 2022 and removed at the **21–23 Feb 2024** plenary, alongside Gibraltar, Barbados and Uganda. The FATF credited an **Executive Office for AML/CTF**, a specialised financial-crimes court, new AML/CFT guidelines for FIs and DNFBPs, and a new penal code — [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/pc-fatf-takes-uae-greylist); [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2024/finance/uae-exits-fatfs-grey-list); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/cross-border-white-collar-crime-and-investigations-review-2025/uaes-enhanced-aml-and-ctf-regime-a-journey-from-grey-list-to-compliance)
- Naming conflict: one aggregator (amluae.com) credits the EOCN (Executive Office for Control & Non-Proliferation) instead. The EOCN is a separate sanctions body, so the attribution looks wrong — [Waystone](https://compliance.waystone.com/uaes-success-exiting-the-fatf-grey-list/) vs [amluae.com](https://amluae.com/uae-removed-from-fatf-grey-list/)
- FIs and DNFBPs file STRs/SARs on the **goAML** platform — [Waystone](https://compliance.waystone.com/uaes-success-exiting-the-fatf-grey-list/)
- The UAE remained on the EU high-risk list after the FATF delisting. Its 5th-round evaluation was expected to start in 2026 — [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2024/finance/uae-exits-fatfs-grey-list)
- **Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025** took effect on 14 Oct 2025 and repealed and replaced Decree-Law 20/2018. The title now covers countering proliferation financing. Tax evasion is an explicit predicate offence. ML/TF via digital systems, virtual assets or encryption is expressly covered. Corporate penalties reach up to AED 100m — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/uae-issues-new-aml-law); [Hadef & Partners](https://hadefpartners.com/news-insights/insights/a-new-era-for-amlctfpf-in-the-uae-analysing-federal-decree-law-no-10-of-2025-on-combating-money-laundering-countering-terrorism-and-proliferation-financing/); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/news/shifting-burdens-and-accountability-the-new-uae-aml-law)
- **Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025** is reported as the new executive regulation, effective 14 Dec 2025 and repealing Cabinet Resolution 10/2019. This comes from a single non-primary source — [amluae.com](https://amluae.com/the-new-uae-aml-cft-law-federal-decree-law-no-10-of-2025-explained/)

### Inferences
- The UAE moved from grey list to exit in about 23 months, a useful case study for how quickly FATF action plans can be completed when reforms are prioritised.
- Background, not verified this session: real estate agents and DPMS (gold) are DNFBPs supervised by the Ministry of Economy. DPMS must file cash transactions of AED 55,000 or more through goAML as DPMSRs, and real-estate cash or virtual-asset deals go through REAR reports. These details were not confirmed this session.

### Gaps
- No primary source checked for the real-estate and DPMS goAML report types or thresholds.
- Not confirmed: the date the UAE adopted goAML, and the official name and creation year (2021?) of the Executive Office.

## Australia: AUSTRAC, Tranche 2, enforcement, Fintel Alliance

### Takeaway
Australia's AML/CTF Amendment Act 2024 brought "Tranche 2" professions into the regime from **1 Jul 2026**, adding an estimated 80,000–90,000 new reporting entities. AUSTRAC's bank and casino enforcement includes Westpac A$1.3bn, CBA A$700m, Crown A$450m and SkyCity A$67m. The Star case was filed in 2022, and its outcome is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **Tranche 2** obligations began on **1 Jul 2026**. They cover real estate professionals and conveyancers, DPMS, lawyers, accountants and TCSPs when they provide designated services. AUSTRAC estimates **80,000–90,000** new reporting entities. Enrolment opened on 31 Mar 2026 with a deadline of 29 Jul 2026. Transitional CDD relief runs to 31 Mar 2029 in some cases. Maximum corporate civil penalties are about A$31.3m per contravention — [LexisNexis AU](https://www.lexisnexis.com/blogs/en-au/insights/austraia-aml-ctf-regime-whats-changed-and-whats-coming-in-2026); [Legl](https://legl.com/articles/tranche-2-commences-1-july-what-australian-law-firms-must-have-in-place); [Persona](https://withpersona.com/blog/tranche-2-australia) (secondary sources; sector lists vary slightly)
- **Westpac**: The Federal Court ordered a **A$1.3bn** penalty, agreed in 2020, for more than 23 million contraventions. AUSTRAC called it the largest civil penalty in Australian history — [AUSTRAC: Westpac penalty ordered](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/our-recent-work/westpac-penalty-ordered)
- **CBA**: A **A$700m** penalty in 2018 covered more than 53,000 contraventions involving intelligent deposit machines — [Thomson Reuters AU](https://insight.thomsonreuters.com.au/business/posts/westpac-agrees-to-1-3bn-penalty-for-aml-ctf-breaches); [Minister for Home Affairs release](https://minister.homeaffairs.gov.au/peterdutton/Pages/record-penalty-for-breaches-anti-money-laundering-laws.aspx)
- **Crown**: **A$450m** agreed penalty, reported for 2023 — [Compliance Week](https://complianceweek.com/austrac/6820.tag); [InDaily](https://www.indailyqld.com.au/news/archive/2022/11/30/star-in-more-strife-as-serious-systemic-money-laundering-to-be-probed)
- **SkyCity**: The Federal Court ordered a **A$67m** penalty on 7 Jun 2024 — [AUSTRAC enforcement list](https://www.austrac.gov.au/lists-enforcement-actions-taken)
- **Star**: AUSTRAC filed against The Star Pty Ltd and The Star Entertainment QLD in Nov 2022, alleging 1,189 (Sydney) and 325 (Queensland) contraventions since Nov 2016. Reports said AUSTRAC sought at least A$400m, while Star argued A$100m would threaten its viability — [AUSTRAC media release](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-commences-proceedings-federal-court-against-star-entertainment-group-entities); [Newcastle Herald](https://www.newcastleherald.com.au/story/8001653/watchdog-takes-casino-giant-star-to-court/)
- **Fintel Alliance** is AUSTRAC's public-private partnership with law enforcement and major banks. Westpac is a confirmed member — [AUSTRAC](https://austrac.gov.au/node/548)

### Inferences
- Australia's Tranche 2 change closes the DNFBP gap that Singapore's IMC and the UAE also targeted. Gatekeeper coverage is a common theme across the region in 2024–2026.
- Background, not verified this session: Fintel Alliance was launched in 2017. AUSTRAC reporting (SMRs, TTRs of A$10,000 or more, IFTIs) uses its own AUSTRAC Online system, not goAML.

### Gaps
- No final Star penalty was found. The AUSTRAC enforcement page timed out on fetch.
- The Fintel Alliance member list and launch year were not confirmed from a primary source.

## India: PMLA, FIU-IND/FINnet 2.0, FATF 2024

### Takeaway
The FATF adopted India's MER at the June 2024 Singapore plenary and placed India in **regular follow-up**, a status shared with only four other G20 members. The FATF flagged slow PMLA prosecutions from 2014 to 2022. FIU-IND is moving reporting entities to FINnet 2.0 (the FINGate 2.0 portal).

### Cited Findings
- The MER was adopted at the FATF plenary in Singapore on **26–28 Jun 2024**, with India placed in **regular follow-up**. The Finance Ministry called it an "outstanding outcome". The FATF recognised India's shift from cash to digital as an ML/TF mitigant. It noted weak progress on PMLA prosecutions in 2014–2022 — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!international/india-now-in-regular-follow-up-category-of-countries-under-fatf-what-it-means-enn24062806665); [Civils Daily](https://civilsdaily.com/news/india-achieves-an-outstanding-outcome-in-fatf-mutual-evaluation-2023-24) (secondary; relaying the MoF statement)
- **FINnet 2.0**: Reporting entities started re-registering from 19 Jan 2022. Stock brokers had a deadline of 15 Nov 2022. Once the new platform went live, filing STRs on FINnet 1.0 was no longer possible. The portal is also called FINGate 2.0 — [NSE circular](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/COMP54354.pdf); [CA Rajput FAQ](https://carajput.com/blog/fingate-2-0-portal/)

### Inferences
- India's regular-follow-up result puts it with Hong Kong among the stronger 4th-round performers in this group. China's 2019 result was weaker, and the UAE's was weak enough to lead to grey-listing.

### Gaps
- The full IO and Recommendation ratings were not retrieved from the FATF MER itself.

## Japan (brief): JAFIC and FATF follow-up

### Takeaway
After its 2021 MER, Japan was in enhanced follow-up. By the third FUR (Oct 2024) it had upgraded all remaining PC ratings to LC, giving 4 C and 35 LC. The next assessment is the 5th round.

### Cited Findings
- The third enhanced FUR was adopted on 10 Oct 2024 and re-rated R.7, 8, 12, 22, 23 and 25 from PC to LC. Japan now has 4 C, 35 LC and no PC ratings. Remaining deficiencies roll into the 5th round. The FUR covers technical compliance, not effectiveness — [FATF Japan FUR 2024](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/japan-fur-2024.html); [MOF Japan 3rd FUR PDF](https://www.mof.go.jp/policy/international_policy/amlcftcpf/3rdFUR-en.pdf)
- JAFIC is Japan's FIU, located in the National Police Agency since Apr 2007 after moving from the FSA — [JAFIC](https://www.npa.go.jp/sosikihanzai/jafic/kokusai/kokutop.htm)

### Inferences
- Japan's FIU sits in law enforcement (NPA), like Hong Kong's JFIU (Police and Customs). Singapore's STRO is also police-based (CAD). By contrast, China's and Australia's FIUs are administrative bodies inside the central bank or a regulator.

## Which jurisdictions use UNODC goAML vs bespoke STR systems?

### Takeaway
Of the seven jurisdictions, only the **UAE** is confirmed this session as a goAML user. Hong Kong (STREAMS 2), India (FINnet 2.0) and, by background knowledge, Singapore (SONAR), Australia (AUSTRAC Online), China (PBOC/CAMLMAC) and Japan use bespoke systems. goAML is run by UNODC and is used by about 75 FIUs worldwide.

### Cited Findings
- UNODC offers goAML to Member States' FIUs — [UNODC goAML](https://unitego.un.org/goaml/)
- The goAML International User Group has 75 FIUs. FIU-Netherlands chaired it from late 2025. Other estimates range from 50 to more than 70 — [FIU-Nederland](https://www.fiu-nederland.nl/en/?p=4829); [Mongol Bank FIU](https://fiu.mongolbank.mn/en/p/1600)
- Asia-Pacific users named in the sources include Mongolia (since 2019), Bangladesh, Brunei, Nepal, Pakistan and New Zealand. The list is a vendor's and is incomplete — [Mongol Bank FIU](https://fiu.mongolbank.mn/en/p/1600); [Alessa](https://alessa.com/blog/goaml-reporting/)
- UAE uses goAML — [Waystone](https://compliance.waystone.com/uaes-success-exiting-the-fatf-grey-list/)
- Hong Kong uses the bespoke STREAMS, replaced by STREAMS 2 from 2 Feb 2026 — [IA/JFIU seminar](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/supervision/antimoney_laundering/files/20251027_AML_Seminar_Eng_JFIU_MLEC.pdf)
- India uses the bespoke FINnet 2.0/FINGate 2.0 — [NSE circular](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/COMP54354.pdf)

### Inferences
- Large, high-volume FIUs (HK, SG, AU, CN, IN, JP) have built their own systems, while goAML is more common among smaller FIUs and those rebuilding quickly, such as the UAE. For a TwinAML demo, goAML's XML report schema is a realistic, publicly documented template for an STR "event" format.

### Gaps
- No primary sources confirmed Singapore's, Australia's, China's or Japan's systems this session.
- No official UNODC country list was found.
