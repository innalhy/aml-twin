# Hong Kong Professional Firms, Property and Dealer Enforcement

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update adds professional-firm, TCSP, estate-agent, MSO and precious-goods evidence to the [baseline cases](2026-10-13-baseline.md#market-trends). Regulatory findings, inspection counts and alleged registration offences are kept distinct from laundering convictions.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** AFRC announced its first AML disciplinary actions on 5 March 2026: three cases involving four parties and HK$290,000 in combined penalties. [Release][S01].
- **Fact:** Four Companies Registry TCSP decisions in January–June 2026 imposed penalties from HK$4,000 to HK$30,000 for control failures. Company names containing “accounting” do not change the regulator or legal basis of those decisions. [Statements][S05], [S06][S06], [S07][S07], [S08][S08].
- **Fact:** EAA reported 618 AML spot checks and 15 AML non-compliance cases in H1 2026. Its 189 licensee actions covered all categories, not only AML. [Release][S09].
- **Fact:** September/October 2026 Customs cases concern alleged dealer-registration breaches and remain under investigation in their announcements. [Pearls][S16], [Watches][S17].
- **Analysis:** A practical technology/training problem is proving that the correct checks occurred at the required time and that changes were followed up. These sources do not establish software causation or market-wide purchasing demand.

### MSOs and dealers

**Facts:** Customs decisions dated 2 June 2026 publicly reprimanded Hongkong Runding Industry Development Group Limited and Joyful Billion Limited for missing instruction-receipt times, and a money changer and remittance business published only under its Chinese name (romanised: Hangtian Renminbi Money Changer and Remittance), for ineffective PEP procedures. No fine appears in these statements. [S13][S13]. A separate 11 September 2026 decision concerned late ultimate-owner/director change notification and ordered remedial action. [S14][S14]. Earlier 26 February 2026 statements concern unnotified account changes with instance counts of 11, 10, 5 and 1; they are not a deduplicated count of unique bank accounts. [S15][S15]. These affect licensed MSOs' records, governance and notification workflows.

**Facts:** Customs' 22 September 2026 announcement describes a 21 September action concerning pearl/jewellery transactions above HK$120,000 without required registration. Its 9 October announcement describes an 8 October action involving cash transactions of watches containing precious metals/stones above HK$120,000 without Category B registration. Both investigations were ongoing. [S16][S16], [S17][S17]. Category A covers qualifying non-cash activity; Category B covers qualifying cash activity and is subject to AML supervision. [Current regime explanation][S18]. Do not treat a registration allegation as proof of laundering.

## Market Trends

**Facts:** EAA's H1 2026 figures show 618 AML checks and 15 AML cases, compared with 580 and 13 in H1 2025. Across all inspection subjects there were 72 cases; the 189 licensee actions, five suspensions and 18 revocations were all-category figures. The release notes multiple allegations and carried-over cases. [S09][S09]. Its January 2026 release separately records 1,120 AML checks in full-year 2025; the 319 actions and HK$4.7 million fines again cover all subjects. [S10][S10].

**Analysis:** Checks, cases and actions use different units and time boundaries. Their ratios do not establish sector prevalence, algorithm accuracy or the probability of detecting laundering. Targeted inspection also differs from a representative sample.

**Facts:** CR reported 7,412 TCSP licensees at end-June 2026 and 333 new licences in the first half. [S12][S12]. A published EAA illustration describes purchaser CDD omissions found in a sole proprietor's random inspection before a preliminary sale agreement, with a reprimand and CPD condition. Its publication is January 2025; the conduct/decision date is not supplied. [Indexed official article][S11].

**Analysis:** The TCSP count shows a regulated population, not the number willing to buy software. The enforcement examples suggest testable problems around incomplete files, review timing and small-team assurance; pricing, incumbent systems and budgets need customer evidence.

**Scope reminder:** The [baseline's EAA scope discussion](2026-10-13-baseline.md#market-trends) remains essential before applying property CDD. A property sale, lease and sale of shares in a property-holding company must not be treated as interchangeable activities under that guideline.

## AML Technology Landscape

| Existing approach / proposed aid | Strength | Weakness or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name/sanctions screening | Finds candidates for further checks. | Prism illustrates that matching people is insufficient if entity checks or documented assessment are missing. A hit alone does not settle risk. [S02][S02]. |
| KYC and professional-firm workflow products in [updates 1](2026-10-13-update-01.md) and [4](2026-10-13-update-04.md) | Existing offers cover ownership, screening and records; reuse/integrate where fit is established. | Their existence does not prove that a particular firm's controls were effective or that software caused these breaches. |
| Evidence/timing assurance | Could show who checked each party, which source was available before acceptance, and which review remains overdue. | Proposed design, not a mandated product. Late-added evidence must not rewrite the historical record. |
| Remittance field validation/change tracking | Could retain instruction time, required party fields and notification deadlines. | Validation should preserve genuine exceptions and recorded resolution rather than silently invent missing values. |
| DPMS registration self-assessment | Customs already offers iPASS to indicate registration category/readiness. [S18][S18]. | A duplicate questionnaire offers limited differentiation. Assessment output does not grant registration or prove compliance. |

## Business Opportunities

**Hypothesis:** Smaller professional firms, TCSPs and MSOs may benefit from a focused file-readiness review and training service: correct activity scope, right parties, evidence dates, open questions, ongoing changes, and reasons for decisions. A reviewer should be able to see a genuine missing control without being encouraged to infer criminality from every incomplete file.

**Recommended next steps:** Compare existing case-management tools, then interview compliance owners about file completion and inspection preparation. Test the proposal against synthetic cases with both a genuine gap and a complete, low-concern explanation. Measure omissions caught, false alarms, time saved and whether a reviewer can reproduce the reasoning. Verify licensed data access and record retention; do not use fine totals as a software-market estimate.

**TwinAML proposals:** Use a document dated after acceptance to test whether the learner understands historical evidence availability; present a domestic PEP match requiring an assessment rather than an automatic accusation; introduce a changed controller/account and a notification deadline; separate a dealer-registration case from a laundering suspicion. These are prospective synthetic exercises. This update changes research notes only.
