# Hong Kong RegTech Adoption: Cohorts, Barriers and Bank-Owned Evidence

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update adds denominator-specific adoption evidence and named bank disclosures to the [baseline](2026-10-13-baseline.md) and [update 4](2026-10-13-update-04.md). Reported use, a trial, an implementation plan and measured effectiveness remain different claims.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** HKMA's broad fintech stock-take analysed 56 submissions from 58 institutions; its 97% RegTech figure is not a measure of AI-in-AML penetration. [Stock-take][S01].
- **Fact:** The AML-specific exercise involved 48 AIs. More than 30% had adopted AI monitoring; above 80% was anticipated from plans over the following 12–24 months. [November circular][S02].
- **Fact:** September 2026 HKMA remarks still describe the supplier cybersecurity baseline as being formulated. Do not treat it as an issued standard from that source. [Speech][S04].
- **Fact:** ICBC Asia distinguishes deployed fraud automation from trial BRAINS AML monitoring; BOCHK reports AML/negative-media applications without measured AML performance. [ICBC Asia][S05], [BOCHK][S06].
- **Analysis:** An adoption opportunity must demonstrate a specific improvement over an incumbent workflow; adoption percentages alone do not establish an unserved buyer.

## Market Trends

**Broad survey facts:** The stock-take was conducted in January and issued 16 July 2025. Significant-operation licensed banks/major foreign branches were invited, some submitting jointly. Numerical analysis normally uses submissions. Reported RegTech adoption rose from 83% in 2022 to 97% in 2025; AI from 59% to 75%. [S01][S01].

| Broad fintech barrier | Reported proportion |
| --- | ---: |
| Implementation cost | 75% |
| New-technology risk | 73% |
| Integration | 71% |
| Privacy/cybersecurity | 61% |
| Talent / regulatory landscape | 59% each |
| High-quality data | 41% |

The table is from the stock-take, not AML-specific purchasing data. It distinguishes planning, pilot, basic, advanced and pioneer maturity. These self-reported responses are not a census of independent buyers, audited effectiveness or vendor revenue. [S01][S01].

**AML-specific facts:** Existing AI use concentrated on risk detection and alert prioritisation. Some institutions took a broader data/analytics approach; others used phased components. The source gives more than 30%, not an exact 37% or a public list of adopters. [S02][S02].

**Analysis:** Buyers already using monitoring tools may still need better data, review, calibration or integration. That is different from needing another detector. Interviewing the people responsible for those tasks is necessary before interpreting survey barriers as demand for TwinAML.

## AML Technology Landscape

| Bank-owned disclosure | What the bank reports | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICBC Asia Annual Report 2025, page 104 | A 24/7 Police-instruction email robot was implemented; group anti-fraud monitoring was fully deployed; Customer Financial Crime Risk View/BRAINS AML monitoring was in trial operation. [S05][S05]. | Do not transfer the fraud deployment status to AML. No supplier contract, false-positive rate, loss reduction or review-time metric is supplied. |
| BOCHK annual-results transcript, pages 17–18 | Describes AI governance and applications in AML/negative-news monitoring; separate sandbox validation concerns other avatars/fraud solutions. [S06][S06]. | Does not establish an AML go-live date, named supplier or quantified AML outcome. |
| BOCHK Sustainability Report 2025, filed March 2026 | Describes screening, monitoring and customer risk ratings; reports over 50 AML sessions and over 90,000 cumulative participations. [S07][S07]. | Participations are not unique learners or measured learning gains. Anti-fraud model statements are not AML detection metrics. |

**Change in the deployment map:** These customer-owned disclosures provide more precise status than a vendor logo or a broad AI strategy. They show existing bank controls and training while leaving effectiveness, cost and a new tool's comparative value unresolved.

**Analysis:** An evaluation should explain the task, data lineage and access, how uncertain outputs are reviewed, reliability under exceptions and the support needed in the existing workflow. The September speech supports asking those questions; it does not certify a product or prescribe one implementation stack. [S04][S04].

## Business Opportunities

**Hypothesis:** Bank training/QA teams could use a narrow evidence-review exercise complementary to current monitoring. Possible tasks include interpreting a prioritised alert, detecting missing inputs, explaining an override, evaluating a changed risk profile and passing an exception to the right reviewer. Buyer demand and operational impact remain unmeasured.

**Recommended next steps:** Compare incumbent case/training workflows and identify the budget owner, integration constraints and actual assessment gap. Evaluate a synthetic pilot on missed risks, unnecessary escalation, rationale completeness and time. Keep model accuracy, review efficiency and learning gain as separate measures. A successful demo does not establish production readiness or an AI adoption mandate.
