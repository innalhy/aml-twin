# Hong Kong AML Controls Software and Market Opportunities

Research date: 13 October 2026. This first Hong Kong baseline covers the operating process, selected regulatory developments, documented software deployments, enforcement lessons, and opportunities relevant to TwinAML. Monetary amounts are Hong Kong dollars unless stated otherwise. Historical deployments and case outcomes retain their publication dates; they are not a live inventory or current court docket.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** Hong Kong combines preventive controls at financial institutions and specified professions with suspicious transaction reporting to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU), criminal investigation, and court proceedings. A monitoring alert, an STR, an account restriction, an arrest, and a conviction are separate events. [Legislative overview][R01], [JFIU reporting guidance][R03], [DOJ judgment summary][R05].
- **Fact:** Discovery increasingly combines customer information, transaction behaviour, connected accounts, device information, and shared intelligence. The HKMA documents both dynamic risk monitoring and tools that detect suspected mules during onboarding. [HKMA AI circular][R08], [bank network-analysis presentation][R17].
- **Fact:** Public Hong Kong deployment evidence exists for HSBC with Google Cloud AML AI and Quantexa, Bangkok Bank's Hong Kong operation with SAS AML, and HashKey Exchange with Elliptic. Each claim has a defined customer, function, and date. [HSBC AI account][R18], [Quantexa announcement][R19], [SAS case study][R20], [HashKey announcement][R23].
- **Fact:** Recent control findings concern missing transaction data, inadequate alert follow-up, undocumented customer enquiries, and weak identification of relevant parties. These failures can persist even when a firm owns a monitoring system. [HKMA bank decisions][R30], [SFC Zheng Da decision][R33], [IA broker findings][R35].
- **Analysis:** The most defensible product opportunities are evidence review, monitoring-data assurance, ownership verification, and analyst training. These address documented problems; public sources do not establish market size, budgets, or willingness to buy. For TwinAML, the strongest near-term application is training learners to investigate and document decisions with incomplete evidence.

### How the legal framework fits together

**Facts.** AMLO, Cap. 615, provides preventive CDD and recordkeeping requirements for specified financial institutions and designated businesses and professions. OSCO, Cap. 455, concerns criminal proceeds; DTROP, Cap. 405, concerns drug proceeds; UNATMO, Cap. 575, concerns terrorist property and financing. Their STR duties extend beyond the AMLO-regulated population. UNSO, Cap. 537, implements the Hong Kong UN sanctions framework. [Companies Registry overview][R01].

HKMA supervises banks' AML systems; SFC covers its securities and VA perimeter; IA covers its insurance perimeter; Customs supervises money service operators and precious-metals/stones dealers; Companies Registry regulates TCSPs. Estate agents, lawyers, and accountants have their own competent authorities and applicable activity boundaries. FSTB coordinates policy. JFIU receives and analyses intelligence; investigators and prosecutors pursue criminal cases. [Government risk assessment, chapter 3][R42].

CDD establishes who the customer and authorised representatives are, who ultimately owns or controls the customer, and the intended relationship. Ongoing scrutiny compares activity with that profile. Higher risk calls for proportionate additional checks; a screening hit or unusual payment does not automatically establish criminality. [CR guideline][R43], [JFIU indicators][R44].

The bank guideline says suspicion can involve attempted transactions and any amount; proof of a predicate crime is unnecessary before reporting. Internal reports reach the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO), who evaluates and documents the decision. CDD records generally remain through the relationship and at least five years afterward; transaction records remain at least five years after completion. Tipping off that prejudices an investigation is an offence. [HKMA bank guideline, chapters 7 and 8][R02].

Ownership verification should go beyond an account signatory or director. Hong Kong's Significant Controllers Register is normally kept by the company and is available to law enforcement; it is not a public central UBO search engine. For trusts, older ownership-threshold descriptions can be misleading after the 2023 AMLO changes. [CR SCR FAQ][R45], [SFC 2023 amendment circular][R46].

**International comparison.** Hong Kong implements the FATF risk-based approach: identify relevant risks, verify customers and ownership, monitor, report suspicions, and support investigation. FATF's country page records 11 compliant, 25 largely compliant, and four partially compliant Recommendations from the **2023 technical follow-up**. Effectiveness findings derive from the **2019 mutual evaluation**. These are different assessments and are not a 2026 effectiveness audit. [FATF country page][R47].

### What happens after suspicious activity is discovered

JFIU's SAFE approach is to screen indicators, ask appropriate questions, find existing records, and evaluate the combined information. Reporting should explain the people or entities, activity, reasons for suspicion, and customer explanation. Regulated entities submit through STREAMS 2. [JFIU reporting guidance][R03].

The following is an **operational synthesis** of the cited guidance, not a prescribed universal sequence:

1. A monitoring rule, screening match, staff observation, customer complaint, or intelligence lead opens a review.
2. An analyst checks the customer profile, historical activity, payment purpose, counterparties, and prior concerns. Missing explanations prompt proportionate further enquiries.
3. Linked-account analysis and independently checked records help corroborate or contradict the explanation.
4. The reviewer closes a resolved concern with reasons, continues enquiries, or escalates to the MLRO. An STR is filed when the reporting trigger is met; investigators need not wait for criminal proof.
5. Relevant bank controls and law-enforcement processes proceed on their own legal and operational bases. JFIU may disseminate intelligence; criminal investigators develop evidence; court outcomes follow separately.

HKMA requires documented reasons for alert closure, appropriate escalation, and assurance over review quality. Buying a vendor system does not transfer responsibility from the institution. [HKMA monitoring guidance, sections 2.4 and 2.15–2.22][R48]. JFIU states that an STR is not itself a formal police crime report; a victim needing crime investigation must report the crime separately. [JFIU FAQ][R49].

In **Tam Sze Leung**, decided 10 April 2024, the Court of Final Appeal upheld the challenged no-consent regime. A letter of no consent withholds statutory immunity for dealing with suspect property; it is not itself a court restraint order. Banks make their own account-restriction decisions when suspicion remains. A simulator should not implement STR filing as an automatic police freeze. [DOJ judgment summary][R05].

## Market Trends

### Detection increasingly examines relationships and behaviour

**Facts.** A bank presentation hosted by HKMA describes joining email addresses, phones, addresses, employment, company links, IP/device tokens, and payment counterparties. Graphs and timelines help connect accounts that look unrelated individually. A separate presentation combines devices, login patterns, test payments, fast flows, and police intelligence. Neither discloses a commercial vendor. [Network-analysis presentation][R17], [digital-footprint presentation][R29].

Banks still need reliable inputs. HKMA's April 2024 review addresses data lineage, missing data during system changes, customer segmentation, threshold testing, and outcome-based performance review. A reduction in alerts is useful only if relevant detection remains effective. [HKMA monitoring review][R52].

**Analysis.** AML increasingly overlaps fraud prevention because scam proceeds move quickly through mule accounts. Fraud teams focus on stopping the loss; AML teams assess account misuse, wider networks, and reporting. The two functions benefit from shared context, while retaining their own responsibilities. An IP match, shared address, unemployment, or cryptocurrency usage is a clue requiring context; ordinary households, shared offices, and legitimate businesses can exhibit similar features.

### What the technology actually detects

**Facts.** HKMA's June 2026 report describes four anonymised Hong Kong banks: external-partner dynamic risk assessment, an internally built risk model, a digital-bank face watchlist, and corporate-mule monitoring across the customer life cycle. The face-watchlist example combines repeated faces/backgrounds with account and transaction context; the corporate example joins onboarding indicators with later behaviour. Bank and vendor identities must not be inferred from these anonymous examples. [HKMA case studies, pages 11–21][R63].

**Analysis of capabilities and limits:**

| Capability | Useful output | Practical weakness or constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| KYC and entity resolution | Verified identity, ownership chain, and links between differently represented entities. | Stale records, name transliteration, nominees, and incomplete ownership evidence can create false or missed links. |
| Rules/scenario monitoring | Explainable alerts about unusual flows and behaviour relative to the customer. | Poor segmentation and absent data can produce excessive noise or leave risks unobserved. |
| Behavioural and network models | Risk priorities, connected-account views, and changes across the customer life cycle. | Links need contextual review; training labels and historical review outcomes are imperfect. |
| Sanctions/PEP screening | Potential matches for investigation using multiple identifiers. | A name resemblance is not a confirmed identity. Relevant lists must be current and matched to legal obligations. |
| Adverse-media review | Source documents and allegations relevant to a customer or owner. | Reports may refer to another person, repeat the same allegation, or omit later acquittals. Review must preserve source dates and attribution. |
| Blockchain analytics | Wallet/exposure leads and fund-flow traces. | Wallet attribution needs corroboration; on-chain visibility does not reveal every off-chain owner or transaction. |
| Generative AI for review work | Draft summaries, document extraction, and proposed questions tied to sources. | Unsupported statements or omitted contradictions can contaminate a case record. Human review and traceable evidence are required design choices. |

The analytical table is a synthesis, not a benchmark of a particular vendor. HKMA guidance links technology to institution-specific risk, explainability, validation, and human expertise. Its September 2024 AI annex describes combining internal customer/transaction/review data with external intelligence, and prioritising higher-risk cases. [HKMA AI annex][R64].

**Prioritisation analysis.** For the existing TwinAML project, training has the clearest fit and the smallest dependency on private banking data. A general monitoring engine would require proprietary data, integration, operational validation, and substantial competition research. A training product can begin with synthetic evidence and test whether learners make better decisions.

### What this means for TwinAML

The existing [course policy](../../data/evidence/analyst-session-001/learner/course-policy.md) already distinguishes poor video quality from proven manipulation, checks independent evidence, and keeps internal escalation separate from a provisional outbound hold. Those distinctions are consistent with the research. They remain **authored classroom rules**, not Hong Kong legal thresholds.

Recommended research-to-training mappings:

- Keep separate simulated events for alert generation, review, internal escalation, STR drafting, filing, bank restriction, law-enforcement action, and legal outcome. A report should not automatically freeze an account.
- Pair suspicious cases with legitimate lookalikes. Reward the evidence available at decision time rather than hindsight about hidden facts.
- Add an exercise in which a core-system change omits a transaction type. This tests monitoring coverage and management response, beyond analyst alert-clearing skill.
- Add a securities/VA case joining funding, small trading activity, account/wallet changes, and withdrawals. Use the SFC circular as the source of the problem, while keeping scenario thresholds synthetic.
- Include wealth/insurance party identification: customer, controller, authorised representative, policyholder, insured person, and payer can be different people.
- Score evidence quality, unresolved contradictions, proportionate controls, timing, and explanation. A high STR count or low alert count should not automatically earn a high score.

These are proposed content additions. This research update changes no simulation code, case rules, or FlexSim runtime.
