# Hong Kong Screening Workloads, Named Implementations and Assurance

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update sharpens the [baseline screening evidence](2026-10-13-baseline.md) and [update 4's AI/vendor analysis](2026-10-13-update-04.md). It distinguishes survey responses, sandbox results, implemented onboarding and customer-owned processing disclosures.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** LSEG's survey reports substantial screening delays and review burdens among Hong Kong respondents. It does not measure the proportion of alerts that are false positives or disclose a representative Hong Kong operational dataset. [Report][S01].
- **Fact:** HKMA's March 2026 review found examined screening systems generally met expectations; configuration, testing and closure assurance remain material. [HKMA][S04].
- **Fact:** Aereve already supplies multilingual/adverse-media functions. Its Citi sandbox metrics are vendor-reported; Cyberport corroborates the collaboration rather than the performance figures or production launch. [Aereve][S05], [Cyberport][S06].
- **Fact:** Wonder/Jumio documents an implemented identity workflow, while BOCHK's own declaration names identity suppliers and processing exceptions. These do not establish AML detection effectiveness. [Jumio][S08], [BOCHK][S10].
- **Analysis:** A plausible TwinAML gap is practice in evidence assessment and accountable decisions across existing tools. Uniqueness, buyer demand and learning effectiveness remain untested.

## Latest Regulatory Developments

| Guidance and date | Impact | Who is affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HKMA screening guidance, February 2023, referenced by the 2026 review | Effective manual or automated handling should cover non-Latin names/codes. Guidance includes Chinese characters and romanisation variants, controlled suppression, reviewer responsibilities, disposition rationale and independent assurance. This is not a rule requiring a particular native-script AI vendor. [S03][S03]. | Banks operating screening controls. |
| HKMA sanctions-screening review, 16 March 2026 | Testing is risk based, with additional tests following relevant changes. [S04][S04]. | Banks; observations also described as useful to SVFs. |
| Bank AML guideline's current-status record | Sanctions screening extends beyond a customer's name and distinguishes Hong Kong requirements from foreign unilateral regimes. State the relevant jurisdiction rather than importing a universal foreign ownership rule. [S11][S11], [guideline][S12]. | Banks mapping party and regime coverage. |
| September identity-control guidance | Builds on the [baseline's remote-onboarding evidence](2026-10-13-baseline.md#latest-regulatory-developments). | Banks and SVFs; source already registered in the baseline. |

**Analysis:** A test should cover candidates missed, candidates over-matched, parties omitted, stale input data and unjustified closures. Passing a name-string benchmark would not demonstrate every operational control.

## Market Trends

**Vendor research facts:** LSEG's mid-2025 survey covered 850 decision makers globally, including 250 across five APAC markets. Reported Hong Kong responses include 90% experiencing screening delays at least occasionally, 80% identifying high false positives as a challenge and 86% identifying manual review/remediation. Precision and risk-appetite customisation each influenced 54% of respondents' tool choices; real-time data influenced 48%. [S01][S01]. Its related article is dated 10 February 2026. [S02][S02].

**Evidence boundary:** The report does not disclose the Hong Kong sample count, weighting, institution names or audited alert outcomes. In particular, 80% describes respondents identifying a problem, not an 80% alert false-positive rate. Treat this as interview direction rather than market-size or achievable-performance evidence. [S01][S01].

**Analysis:** Buyers can have competing needs: broader coverage may raise review volume, while aggressive suppression may hide relevant candidates. Customer validation should therefore ask about missed candidates, uncertainty and review quality alongside speed and headline alert reduction.

| BOCHK's ID Verification Declaration, October 2023 filename | Customer-owned document names Jumio/subproviders and livi/eIDLINK-related handling, with data-flow/retention distinctions, manual review and assistance when automated document reading blocks a request. [S10][S10]. | No verified 2026 rollout, supplier price or measured identity/AML performance. |

**Change in the vendor map:** There is now stronger evidence for a named Citi/Aereve sandbox and a named Wonder identity integration, plus direct bank-owned supplier disclosures. Generic claims that Chinese-name screening, adverse-media summaries or identity onboarding are unserved Hong Kong functions are therefore unsupported.

**Sanctions boundary:** The bank guideline addresses relevant ownership/control and specified connected-party screening. Its treatment of overseas sanctions differs from Hong Kong's ordinary obligations. A candidate name is the beginning of an identity/coverage assessment; it does not by itself establish the applicable restriction. [Chapter 6][S12].

**Analysis:** Keep original-script names, identifiers, aliases, source dates, article relevance, ownership links and reviewer reasoning available. A summary should not silently convert an allegation into a conviction, a similarly named person into the customer, or an inaccessible source into a negative finding.

## Business Opportunities

**Hypothesis:** A teaching and review-assurance layer could help bank, SVF and fintech teams practise screening decisions across existing supplier systems. It should expose evidence quality and missing information, distinguish identity relevance from risk, and preserve the reason for closure or escalation. The opportunity would need to complement capabilities already present in named tools.

**Recommended next steps:** Interview screening leads about tuning, QA, multilingual review and exception handling. Compare the proposed exercise with current vendor workflows and existing training offerings. Use synthetic cases containing near matches, genuine matches, irrelevant articles, conflicting identifiers, uncertain ownership and legitimate explanations. Evaluate missed risks, unnecessary escalation, evidence completeness and reasoning consistency as well as time.

**TwinAML design implication:** An analyst could receive a screening candidate, seek identifiers and original material, identify contradictory evidence, then make a reviewable disposition. A separate identity case should distinguish forged documents, genuine documents used by an impostor and a genuine customer whose later activity changes. These remain research proposals; no model was trained, source data licensed or learning benefit demonstrated.
