# Hong Kong Ownership Records and SCR Evidence Limits

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update adds Significant Controllers Register (SCR) access, tracing and inspection evidence to the beneficial-ownership comparison in [update 9](2026-10-13-update-09.md). It distinguishes keeping a register from demonstrating the accuracy of its contents or completing a bank's CDD.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** The SCR is kept by the company in Hong Kong and is not delivered to CR for public registration. [FAQ][S02].
- **Fact:** Statutory access includes bounded rights for entered significant controllers as well as relevant official inspection; it is not an unrestricted public database. [Statute][S04], [guide][S03].
- **Fact:** CR's 2024–25 report says over 96% of inspected companies kept SCRs; that is not a population ownership-accuracy rate. [Report][S06].
- **Analysis:** Corporate KYC needs dated sources, underlying ownership/control tracing and explicit unresolved gaps. Register availability alone cannot settle those questions.

## Latest Regulatory Developments

| Current source | Impact | Who is affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CR overview / FAQ | Includes Hong Kong incorporated and re-domiciled companies; distinguishes listed companies and registered non-Hong Kong companies. Registers stay locally rather than being filed publicly. [S01][S01], [S02][S02]. | Companies identifying their SCR obligations and parties assessing supplied records. |
| SCR guide, updated 23 May 2025 | Covers controller access and ownership/control tracing, including nominees and trusts. [S03][S03]. | Companies maintaining records and reviewers testing ownership explanations. |
| Companies Ordinance sections 653W / 653X | Distinguishes entered-controller inspection/copy rights from CR and specified law-enforcement access. [S04][S04]. | Parties establishing the legal basis for access. |

**Analysis:** A KYC vendor or bank employee cannot infer the same statutory access simply because the information would be useful. Company-supplied records, ordinary public filings and official inspection rights are different evidence routes. Overseas ownership above a Hong Kong company does not turn its SCR into a public foreign beneficial-ownership database.

## Market Trends

**Facts:** The annual-report index still listed 2024–25 as latest when checked. The report describes over 96% register-keeping in site inspections, 5,483 broader company-name/register inspection events and 3,353 Companies Ordinance summonses. It provides no separate raw SCR sample, unique-company denominator or contents-accuracy result in the reviewed passages. Multiplying the broader inspection total by 96% would invent a count. [S05][S05], [S06][S06].

**Facts:** CR's current prosecution table gives 2,801 summonses and HK$9,066,284 total fines for January–August 2026, with later months blank, and 3,457 summonses for calendar 2025. There is no SCR-specific breakdown. These outputs are not unique firms or directly interchangeable with financial-year totals. [S07][S07].

**Historical case:** A 2 August 2023 CR release names 15 companies, including HT Machining Limited and Hong Kong Shunda Business Secretary Limited. Each was convicted of four register-location offences concerning members, directors, company secretaries and significant controllers, with HK$16,000 total fines each. The amount cannot be assigned entirely to the SCR offence. The release establishes required-record availability failures, not false ownership, laundering or software causation. [S08][S08].

No 2025–2026 SCR contents-accuracy audit or newer named ownership-fraud conviction was verified in this slice. Search results do not prove that none exists.

## AML Technology Landscape

**Facts:** The guide attributes nominee-held shares to the underlying person and requires further tracing where that person is a legal entity. Its trust/firm provisions address relevant trustees/members and significant influence/control. Share percentages alone can miss qualifying control. [S03][S03].

**Facts:** The FAQ's overseas corporate-shareholder example includes both the corporate shareholder and its ultimate individual owner. It also requires an appropriate recorded statement where no significant controller is known; that is different from leaving a register blank. [S02][S02].

| Proposed review task | Supporting evidence | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establish the source and access route | Public filing, supplied register, agreement or official record. | Availability does not establish completeness or permission for every use. |
| Trace a nominee arrangement | Underlying holder, relevant agreements and further ownership evidence. | A named legal shareholder may not settle ultimate control. |
| Test non-share control | Supported governance/trust rights and actual explanation. | A percentage-only chart can omit material control. |
| Compare dates and contradictions | Versions, notices, documents and corroboration. | Automatically merging conflicting records can hide uncertainty. |

These are analytical tasks, not proof that a named software product completes them accurately.

## Business Opportunities

**Hypothesis:** Corporate onboarding and professional-service educators may value exercises that require source comparison rather than copying an ownership chart. A possible case could present a public extract, company-supplied SCR, nominee agreement and conflicting explanation. Demand and purchase authority remain unverified.

**Recommended next steps:** Observe how reviewers establish access and request corroboration; compare current materials and company-record tools. Assess the learner's supported tracing, proportionate enquiries and recorded uncertainty. Keep a missing record, inaccurate entry, unresolved CDD and formed suspicion as distinct outcomes.

**TwinAML scope:** Reuse existing evidence cards and retained decisions if a synthetic case is later authorised. It should not claim access to private registers or automatic verification of real ownership. This update adds research only.
