# Hong Kong Insurance AML Party and Payment Controls

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update adds an insurance workflow to the [baseline](2026-10-13-baseline.md), clarifies guideline versioning, and records a recent corporate KYC partnership. Facts, operational analysis, and proposed products are separated below.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** Revised GL3 took effect on 30 May 2025 and superseded the prior version. Its amendment reflects company re-domiciliation and consequential AMLO changes. [Gazette][S01], [IA amendment circular][S02].
- **Fact:** GL3 distinguishes representatives, beneficiaries, and payout screening; these are separate control steps. [Current guideline and marked counterpart][S03], [indexed paragraph evidence][S04].
- **Fact:** IA's 9 April 2024 cashier-order circular examines unidentified third-party premium payments and sets a benchmark for purchaser evidence and aggregate checks. [Circular][S05].
- **Fact:** A 23 September 2026 vendor release names Bowtie's corporate KYC partnership and describes integration with its policy system. It does not independently verify production go-live or measured outcomes. [Vendor case study][S06].
- **Analysis:** A single customer record or screening result can obscure the different roles in a policy. Useful review tools must retain who acted, who paid, who receives money, and the evidence connecting them.

## Latest Regulatory Developments

| Update | Impact | Who is affected |

**Selected GL3 requirements:** Verify a representative's identity and documentary authority (4.5.3–4.5.4). Record a designated beneficiary; verify identity when rights are first exercised or at first payout, whichever is earlier (4.6.1–4.6.3). Policy changes and unusual premiums/surrenders can trigger review (5.3); high-risk customers receive at least annual review (5.4). Resolve screening matches and retain results (6.18); overseas-office screening leaves institutional responsibility intact (6.19). Screen payees, including beneficiaries, at payout against relevant terrorist/sanctions concerns (6.20). [May 2025 marked text][S04].

**Cashier-order benchmark, pages 4–5:** Under a threshold approach, conclusive purchaser evidence is expected for an order exceeding HK$400,000/US$50,000, or when orders for the same policy in the same year exceed that amount. Below that level, orders exceeding HK$120,000/US$15,000 receive random sampling. Keep an all-channel order log, detect splitting/common payors across unrelated policyholders, and report where suspicion exists. The circular also addresses third-party identity, relationship, and payment purpose; unrelated third-party payments should generally be rejected. These thresholds do not remove the broader risk-based duties. [Circular][S05].

**What changed:** The baseline discussed insurance control findings. This update adds the applicable policy/payment workflow and the newer guideline reference. The 24 September 2026 FWD penalty remains a separate, secondary-confirmed lead: the IA decision could not be retrieved, so its detailed findings are not promoted to primary-verified status.

## Market Trends

**Fact:** Know Your Customer's 23 September 2026 case study says its Bowtie partnership brings official company-record retrieval and ownership checks into the insurer's existing policy-administration workflow through APIs. It uses both implementation and prospective wording. No measured review-time saving, customer volume, fee, or independently confirmed go-live date is disclosed. A customer-issued corroborating release was not found in the research pass. [Vendor case study][S06].

**Analysis:** This extends the [bank corporate KYC evidence](2026-10-13-update-01.md) into insurance. It supports a specific workflow problem—corporate evidence collection inside existing operations—rather than a claim that all insurers need a new standalone platform. Integration and review ownership are part of the problem alongside access to data.

Insurance review also extends across time. A relationship can begin with one set of parties and later involve a new beneficiary, changed premium pattern, surrender, or payout. A proposed tool should be evaluated across that lifecycle rather than solely by onboarding speed.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Know Your Customer with Bowtie | Named Hong Kong insurer relationship; registry/ownership checks integrated into an existing policy workflow are described. | Vendor-issued evidence; deployment maturity and effectiveness are unmeasured. Corporate KYB is not the insurer's entire monitoring or payout-control stack. [S06][S06]. |
| Party records, screening, payment logs, and case management | **Analysis:** These functions can connect policy events to available identity and payment evidence. | A screening status recorded for one party or date cannot settle every later recipient/payment question. |
| Evidence extraction or AI review assistance | **Analysis:** Can prepare a comparison of records and highlight contradictions for review. | A confident statement that the purchaser is the policyholder needs supporting evidence, not merely a completed declaration or extracted name. |

**Recommended evaluation:** Test whether a reviewer can reconstruct the parties and evidence from the case record. Include a newly appointed representative, a changed beneficiary, an unclear purchaser, and a later correction. Assess false identity links and premature closure as well as review speed.

**Recommendation for TwinAML:** A future exercise could reveal a policyholder declaration first, then purchaser evidence that either corroborates or contradicts it. Keep the simulated decision and its supporting documents visible in the debrief. Treat the exercise as training in evidence assessment; no live insurer integration or additional application behaviour was implemented here.
