# Hong Kong AML AI Governance Procurement and Competition

Research date: 13 October 2026. This update refines the [baseline's business hypotheses](2026-10-13-baseline.md#business-opportunities) with privacy/model guidance, customer implementation evidence, a public tender, and existing providers. An advertised capability, sandbox result, or procurement specification does not establish production effectiveness.

## Executive Summary

- **Fact:** PCPD published agentic-AI privacy guidance on 25 August 2026, covering data access, retention, accountability, testing, and human oversight. It supplements its existing framework. [Official release][S01].
- **Fact:** SFC's 12 November 2024 language-model circular calls for proportionate oversight, validation, monitoring, and third-party assessment within its defined licensed-corporation scope. [Circular][S03].
- **Fact:** CLPS announced an AML review/risk-rating sandbox project on 22 September 2026. Its accuracy claims are vendor-reported and do not establish live detection or financial return. [Issuer release][S06].
- **Fact:** Training, professional-firm case management, and AML consulting already have providers. [IQ-EQ][S08], [KYC Management][S09], [Protiviti][S10].
- **Analysis:** A credible opportunity needs a specific workflow improvement and comparative evidence. A generic chatbot, screening interface, or gamified course is insufficient differentiation by itself.

## Latest Regulatory Developments

| Update | Impact | Who is affected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PCPD agentic-AI privacy guidance, 25 August 2026. | Address purpose-bound/minimised access, least privilege, traceability, deletion of personal data in chats/caches/memory, testing, significant-decision oversight, and clear responsibility. [S01][S01]. | Organisations using agentic AI to process personal data. This is privacy guidance, not a new AML licensing rule. |
| PCPD employee GenAI checklist, 31 March 2025. | Define permitted tools/purposes, input-data rules, retention, accuracy checks, staff access, incident handling and training. [S02][S02]. | Employers introducing staff-facing GenAI tools. |
| SFC language-model circular, 12 November 2024; relevant established guidance. | Validate intended uses before deployment/material changes; monitor operation; assess third-party/supply-chain components. [S03][S03]. | Licensed corporations using models in services/functions relating to regulated activities; apply proportionality and use-case risk. |

**Facts:** The SFC circular addresses leakage risks across model-related components and calls for documented evaluation. Existing enterprise risk frameworks may suffice rather than creating duplicate structures; benchmark scores alone may be inadequate. [Paragraphs 6–8, 13, 26–29 and footnotes 13–14][S03]. PCPD's guidance does not create a blanket local-hosting requirement. [S01][S01].

**Analysis:** A live analyst assistant and a synthetic training exercise involve different information and decision consequences. The relevant scope and controls need to be assessed for the actual use. In particular, a proposal to draft a review note should identify which sources the model sees, where data persists, who checks the result, and how a correction reaches the retained case record.

## Market Trends

### Customer evidence concerns implementation as well as models

**Facts:** Futu Securities International (Hong Kong)'s 3 November 2025 announcement records an MOU with FCC Analytics and participation in the AWS/FCC Regtech Innovation Lab. It describes PoC/testing, secure hosting, and reducing local-deployment complexity. This establishes an exploratory collaboration, without a measured production benefit. [Customer release][S04].

BEA's 2025 annual report, published 25 March 2026, records an AI governance framework/steering committee, a three-year AI strategy, model-risk appetite, tighter third-party production controls, and continuing AML/fraud technology review. It names no AML vendor or spend and provides no measured AML benefit. [Issuer report, printed page 46/PDF page 48][S05].

**Analysis:** These sources suggest that integration, oversight, security, and operation are material adoption questions. They do not disclose a market-wide procurement budget. A product demonstration should include evidence of how it fits an existing review workflow, rather than relying solely on an impressive model output.

### A public tender gives a concrete purchasing example

**Fact:** SFC's 11 February 2026 VA Accelerator tender specified proposal-review workflows, templates, reasoned assessments/supporting documents, and training. Technical review includes AML monitoring readiness and third-party responsibilities. It required separate software, consultancy, operation and training fees, with milestone-based payments and per-proposal review pricing. The deadline was 11 March 2026; the tender envisaged a Q2 start and 18 months of operational support. No award, contract amount, or delivered operation was verified. [Tender, printed pages 3–8, 11–12 and 18][S11].

**Analysis:** This is evidence of a requested regulatory assessment service and a possible consulting-tool channel. It is not evidence that commercial banks will buy the same product, nor an open tender as of the research date.

**Change compared with earlier research:** Evidence review, institutional risk work, and training were previously framed as candidate opportunities. The additions show existing competition in each category. The opportunity question is now whether a narrower method improves a customer's actual task enough to justify switching, integration, or an additional supplier.

**Analysis of the CLPS claim:** A high overall rating accuracy may be compatible with poor performance on a scarce high-risk class. Without the evaluation population and independent test details, the reported number cannot support a procurement comparison. Expert review and evidence reproducibility should be assessed separately from agreement with model-generated ratings.

## Business Opportunities

These remain **hypotheses**, with demand, buying authority, budgets and switching costs unresolved.

| Potential opportunity | Target customers | Recommended next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence-quality evaluation and validation materials | Banks/brokers evaluating review assistants; established vendors and consultants. | Compare current reviewer practice and proposed assistance using expert-scored synthetic cases. Measure unsupported claims, missed contradictions, high-risk misses, and review effort. |
| Review documentation integrated with existing consulting delivery | AML remediation/assurance consultancies and compliance teams. | Observe one assessment end to end; test whether source links, issue ownership, and repeatable checks reduce rework beyond existing case-management tools. |
| Investigation practice with defensible learner scoring | Training providers and institutions already buying AML learning. | Compare the paired-evidence exercise with their existing course. Assess enquiry quality and decisions on unseen cases, rather than only completion or quiz scores. |

**Recommendation for TwinAML:** First test the existing educational method with practitioners: can learners distinguish a suspicious case from a legitimate lookalike, explain their uncertainty, and revise decisions when evidence changes? A later AI assistant should be evaluated as assistance to that task. The research does not establish enterprise readiness, certification, training-credit eligibility, or customer demand for TwinAML.
