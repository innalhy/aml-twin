# Global AML Catches Paperwork Better Than Criminals

Anti-money laundering (AML) worldwide runs on one standard: the Financial Action Task Force's 40 Recommendations. Peer review and public listing enforce it. National laws then turn it into private duties: banks, crypto firms and "gatekeeper" professions must know their customers, screen against sanctions lists, monitor transactions and report suspicion to a national financial intelligence unit (FIU). The machine is enormous. The United States alone received about **4.8 million suspicious activity reports (SARs) and 21.5 million currency transaction reports in FY2025**. Industry-sponsored studies put global compliance spending at **about US$206 billion a year**. Yet independent estimates find only **about 0.1–1% of criminal proceeds is ever confiscated**. The headline enforcement cases show banks failing at the basics: TD Bank left **92% of its transaction volume unmonitored**, and Starling screened customers against **39 of 3,088** sanctioned names. Missed exotic laundering schemes are rarer causes.

2025–2026 is a year of divergence:
- **The EU** is centralising under a single rulebook and a new supervisor (AMLA).
- **The UK** is consolidating supervisors.
- **Hong Kong and Singapore** have built statutory bank-to-bank data sharing.
- **The US** has cut program burden (corporate-registry rollback, SAR FAQs, removal of "reputation risk") while sharpening national-security tools aimed at cartels, scam centres and sanctions evasion.

For TwinAML, the evidence says a trainee simulation should model process integrity. That means coverage gaps, backlogs, the SAR clock, narrative quality, recorded overrides and the cost of exiting customers, not only typology spotting. All FATF list data below reflect the **June 2026 plenary**. The October 2026 plenary results had not been published as of 13 October 2026.

## FATF sets one global standard and enforces it through peer pressure and lists

### Forty Recommendations, eleven outcomes and a fast-moving rulebook

FATF is an intergovernmental standard-setter of roughly 38 member jurisdictions plus the European Commission and the Gulf Cooperation Council. In February 2023 it suspended Russia, the first such step in its history. Russia must still implement the standards and remains in the Eurasian Group ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-february-2023.html)). Indonesia joined in October 2023 ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-october-2023.html)). Together with nine FATF-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs), the "Global Network" covers **more than 200 jurisdictions** ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/fight-illicit-finance.html); [CFATF](https://cfatf-gafic.org/global-network/)). The FSRBs include APG, MONEYVAL, GAFILAT and ESAAMLG. The Plenary decides by consensus three times a year. Mexico's presidency ended at the June 2026 Plenary. That Plenary approved the priorities of an incoming **UK presidency** and named **India's Vivek Aggarwal Vice-President** for 2026–27 ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html); [US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0547)).

The standard has changed more in 2022–2026 than in the previous decade:

| Date | Recommendation | Change |
|---|---|---|
| March 2022 | R.24 (companies) | Multi-pronged beneficial ownership (BO) collection, usually a registry, plus a ban on new bearer shares ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/R24-statement-march-2022.html)) |
| February 2023 | R.25 | Extended this to trusts and other legal arrangements |
| October 2023 | R.4 and R.38 | Countries must prioritise asset recovery, create **non-conviction-based confiscation**, and cooperate with foreign confiscation orders ([Basel Institute](https://baselgovernance.org/blog/fatf-seeks-change-landscape-international-asset-recovery-what-means-latin-america)) |
| February 2025 | R.1 | "Commensurate" became "**proportionate**", to push simplified measures for about 1.4 billion unbanked adults ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-standards-promote-financial-conclusion-feb-2025.html)) |
| June 2025 | R.16 ("payment transparency") | Widened to fraud; virtual IBANs may no longer disguise where an account sits; **implementation due by end-2030** ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfrecommendations/update-Recommendation-16-payment-transparency-june-2025.html); [A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/fatf-updates-standards-on-payment-transparency)) |
| June 2026 | R.6 | Strengthened to protect humanitarian aid from sanctions side-effects ([ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/)) |

Read together, these revisions move FATF from "paper rules" toward knowing who owns companies, getting money back, carrying fraud-relevant payment data and not excluding the poor.

### Mutual evaluations grade countries twice, and the fifth round is harder

Every country receives two parallel ratings:
- **Technical compliance** on each of the 40 Recommendations: Compliant, Largely Compliant, Partially Compliant or Non-Compliant.
- **Effectiveness** on 11 Immediate Outcomes, which cover areas such as supervision, financial intelligence, prosecution and confiscation: High, Substantial, Moderate or Low ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Assessment-ratings.html)).

The fifth round began in 2024 under the 2022 Methodology (amended June 2026). It runs a roughly six-year, risk-based cycle ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Fatf-methodology.html)). Early results suggest "High" is harder to earn. **Singapore received no High rating on any outcome, while Latvia got five**. RUSI concludes the round is "significantly increasing the onus" on countries to prove effectiveness ([RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/opening-door-uks-2027-financial-action-task-force-evaluation)). The June 2026 Plenary adopted Canada's and Türkiye's reports. The UK's on-site visit is due in June 2027 ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html); [RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/opening-door-uks-2027-financial-action-task-force-evaluation)).

Poor results trigger review by FATF's International Co-operation Review Group (ICRG). Under the 2022 procedures, a country is referred if **nine or more outcomes are Low or Moderate (with at least two Low), or six or more are Low** ([FATF procedures](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/methodology/Assessment-Follow-Up-ICRG-Procedures-2022.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)).

### Twenty-two grey-listed jurisdictions and three on the black list

After the 19 June 2026 Plenary, **22 jurisdictions** are under increased monitoring (the "grey list"): Angola, Bolivia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Bulgaria, Cameroon, Côte d'Ivoire, DR Congo, Haiti, Iraq, Kenya, Kuwait, Lao PDR, Lebanon, Monaco, Nepal, Papua New Guinea, South Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Vietnam, the British Virgin Islands and Yemen ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/High-risk-and-other-monitored-jurisdictions/increased-monitoring-june-2026.html)). The "call for action" list is unchanged: **Iran (countermeasures), DPRK and Myanmar** ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Fatfgeneral/outcomes-fatf-plenary-june-2026.html)). The list churns quickly:

| Plenary | Added | Removed |
|---|---|---|
| June 2025 | Bolivia, British Virgin Islands | Croatia, Mali, Tanzania ([VinciWorks](https://vinciworks.com/blog/june-2025-fatf-grey-list-updates-guide-to-high-risk-jurisdictions/)) |
| October 2025 | none | Burkina Faso, Mozambique, Nigeria, South Africa ([Plenitude](https://www.plenitudeconsulting.com/news-insights/fatf-updates-grey-list-after-october-plenary)) |
| February 2026 | Kuwait, Papua New Guinea | none ([NZ DIA](https://www.dia.govt.nz/AML-CFT-Jurisdictions-under-Increased-Monitoring---February-2026)) |
| June 2026 | Bosnia and Herzegovina, Iraq | Algeria, Namibia ([ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/)) |

Bulgaria shows the standard exit path. In June 2026 FATF found it had substantially completed its action plan. An on-site visit comes next, and removal can follow at a later Plenary ([ComplyAdvantage](https://complyadvantage.com/insights/fatf-plenary-june-2026-outcomes/)).

Grey-listing is not a legal sanction. It bites because correspondent banks apply enhanced due diligence (EDD) to listed countries. IMF research finds a "large and statistically significant reduction in capital inflows" ([IMF WP/21/153](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2021/05/27/The-Impact-of-Gray-Listing-on-Capital-Flows-An-Analysis-Using-Machine-Learning-50289)). Secondary reports put the drop at about **7.6% of GDP** ([BusinessNow Malta](https://businessnow.mt/50-darker-shades-of-listing/)); that figure was not checked against the IMF text.

### FIUs, standard-setters and indices complete the architecture

FIUs receive the reports. The **Egmont Group links 182 FIUs** ([Egmont](https://egmontgroup.org/faqs/)). They exchange intelligence over the FinCEN-managed Egmont Secure Web under binding Principles revised in July 2025 ([Egmont](https://egmontgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/EG-Principles-for-Information-Exchange-Revised-July-2025.pdf)). FIUs follow four models: administrative (attached to a regulator or ministry), law-enforcement, judicial, or hybrid ([Egmont](https://egmontgroup.org/about/financial-intelligence-unit)). The Basel Committee turns AML into bank-supervisory expectations in chapter AFS10 of its framework ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-consolidated-guidelines/module/afs/10)). The private Wolfsberg Group sets bank-to-bank norms through its Correspondent Banking Due Diligence Questionnaire, now at v1.4 ([Wolfsberg CBDDQ](https://s203.q4cdn.com/215756951/files/doc_downloads/corporate-responsability/2026/01/Wolfsberg-Correspondent-Banking-Due-Diligence-Questionnaire.pdf)).

Two indices show that "risky" and "secretive" are different things:
- The **Basel AML Index 2025** measures vulnerability. It ranks Myanmar highest risk (8.18) and Finland lowest ([Basel Institute](https://baselgovernance.org/publications/basel-aml-index-2025)).
- The **Financial Secrecy Index 2025** measures secrecy weighted by how many financial services a country sells to non-residents. It ranks the **United States first**, ahead of Switzerland, Singapore and Hong Kong ([Tax Justice Network](https://taxjustice.net/indexes-tools/)).

The often-quoted "2–5% of global GDP" laundered is not a measurement. It is an IMF "consensus range" from 1998. UNODC's own 2011 estimate put all criminal proceeds at about 3.6% of GDP and warned it must be "treated with caution" ([UNODC](https://unodc.org/lpomex/es/publicaciones/documentos/crimen/Illicit_financial_flows_2011_web.html)).

## Nine jurisdictions implement one standard in strikingly different ways

The FATF standard tells countries what to achieve, not how. The table summarises how nine major jurisdictions answer that question. Sources for each row appear in the paragraphs below.

| Jurisdiction | FIU and reporting channel | Distinctive feature | Key 2024–2026 change | FATF standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EU | National FIUs; no EU-wide STR count | Moving from directives to one directly applicable rulebook | AMLR applies 10 July 2027; AMLA direct supervision from 1 Jan 2028 | Members assessed individually |
| UK | NCA UKFIU, SAR Portal | Consent regime (DAML) gives a defence to proceed | MLR amendments in force 30 June 2026; FCA becomes single professional-services supervisor 2028–2030 | 5th-round on-site June 2027 |
| Singapore | STRO (police) | Statutory, MAS-run bank-to-bank platform (COSMIC) | Post-S$3bn-case laws on company service providers and casinos | 5th round: no High ratings |
| Hong Kong | JFIU (police and customs), STREAMS 2 | Staged police-to-bank-to-bank fraud sharing | Statutory sharing for individual accounts from 3 Nov 2025 | 2019 "compliant and effective overall" |
| Mainland China | CAMLMAC (central bank) | RMB 50,000 large-cash reporting | Revised AML Law from 1 Jan 2025 with extraterritorial reach | 2019 MER critical of BO and DNFBP coverage |
| UAE | UAE FIU via goAML | Rapid grey-list exit | Decree-Law 10/2025 from 14 Oct 2025 | Delisted Feb 2024; 5th round 2026 |
| Australia | AUSTRAC | Large civil-penalty enforcement | "Tranche 2" professions from 1 July 2026 | Not covered in sources reviewed |
| India | FIU-IND, FINnet 2.0 | Digital-payments shift counted as a mitigant | MER adopted June 2024 | Regular follow-up |
| Japan | JAFIC (National Police Agency) | Crypto travel rule with no threshold | All PC ratings upgraded by Oct 2024 | 4 C, 35 LC |

### The EU replaces 27 transpositions with a single rulebook and a new supervisor

The 2024 package has three parts:
- the **AML Regulation (2024/1624)**, directly applicable from **10 July 2027**;
- **AMLD6 (2024/1640)**, a directive on supervision and registers;
- **AMLA**, a new authority in Frankfurt.

([Matheson](https://publications.matheson.com/anti-money-laundering-timeline/))

AMLA's Executive Board is now complete under chair Bruna Szego ([eucrim](https://eucrim.eu/news/amla-executive-board-now-fully-operational-with-fifth-member-appointed/)). AMLA will select **up to 40 cross-border, high-risk institutions** active in at least six Member States in 2027 and supervise them directly from **1 January 2028** ([AMLA](https://www.amla.europa.eu/about-amla_en)). National supervisors fed eligibility data by 15 August 2026 ([FIAU Malta](https://fiaumalta.org/news/amla-direct-supervision-key-preparatory-steps-ahead-of-2027-selection-exercise/)).

The Regulation also changes three things for institutions:
- It sets an EU-wide **€10,000 cash-payment limit**. Sources disagree on whether the limit covers only traders in goods ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/amlr-explained-eu-aml-regulation-2027/)).
- It harmonises the beneficial ownership test at "25% or more" ([Zavia](https://zavia.ai/eu-beneficial-ownership-in-2026-amlr-amld6-amla-explained/)).
- Through **Article 75**, it creates the first EU legal basis for information-sharing partnerships ([Lucinity](https://lucinity.com/blog/a-compliance-professionals-guide-to-amlr-article-75)).

The EU's list of high-risk third countries has diverged from FATF's. In January 2026 it added **Russia**, under a new category for FATF-suspended members ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32026R0046)). Until 2027, EU supervision and reporting remain national, so no single EU SAR count or fine regime exists.

### The UK lets reporters ask permission

The UK pairs risk-based Money Laundering Regulations, amended by **SI 2026/621** (mostly in force 30 June 2026), with the criminal framework of the Proceeds of Crime Act ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348281743)). The amendment narrows mandatory country-based EDD to FATF "call for action" countries ([STEP](https://www.step.org/industry-news/updated-uk-money-laundering-regulations-come-effect-30-june)).

Its distinctive feature is the **consent regime**. A reporter files a "defence against money laundering" (DAML) request. It gets a defence to proceed if the National Crime Agency does not refuse within **7 working days**, or if a refusal's **31-day moratorium** expires ([POCA s.335](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/29/section/335)). The UK FIU's 2024–25 figures ([NCA](https://www.nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk/who-we-are/publications/786-sars-annual-report-2025/file)):
- **866,616 SARs** received;
- **57,666 DAML requests**;
- a record **£382.6 million** denied to suspects.

The structure is also changing:
- The **FCA will replace 22 professional-body supervisors**, with a phased transfer from late 2028 to 2030 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/aml-supervisory-reform)).
- A corporate **failure-to-prevent-fraud** offence took effect on 1 September 2025 ([Slaughter and May](https://www.slaughterandmay.com/insights/corporate-update/corporate-update-bulletin-11-september-2025/)).
- Mandatory **authorised push payment (APP) scam reimbursement**, from October 2024, splits costs **50/50 between sending and receiving banks**. This makes mule detection a direct cost line for the receiving bank ([PSR](https://www.psr.org.uk/media/rhelv4op/ps25-5-app-scams-reimbursement-consolidated-policy-statement-may-2025.pdf)).

### Singapore and Hong Kong build legal pipes between banks

Singapore's regime was reshaped by the August 2023 Fujian-gang case. Police seized or froze more than **S$3 billion** in assets. An inter-ministerial committee reported on 4 October 2024 ([MOF](https://www.mof.gov.sg/docs/default-source/default-document-library/news-and-publications/press-releases/2024/imc-aml-report.pdf)). Laws passed in 2024:
- brought nominee directors under registered corporate service providers ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/singapore-passes-corporate-service-providers-bill/));
- cut the casino customer due diligence threshold to **S$4,000** ([Rahmat Lim](https://www.rahmatlim.com/publication/articles/28899/anti-money-laundering-and-other-matters-bill-enhancing-amlcft-regime-passed-key-takeaways-from-second-reading)).

In July 2025, MAS fined **nine institutions S$27.45 million** for "poor or inconsistent implementation" of controls ([AsiaOne](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/mas-issues-2745m-penalties-9-financial-institutions-over-3b-money-laundering-case)). Since 1 April 2024, **COSMIC** has let six major banks share customer information. Sharing is allowed only when objectively defined red flags appear, and it covers legal-person misuse, trade finance and proliferation financing ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2024/04/223331-monetary-authority-of-singapore-launches-cosmic-platform-to-combat-money-laundering/)).

Hong Kong was the first Asia-Pacific jurisdiction rated "compliant and effective overall" by FATF, in 2019 ([HSF](https://www.hsf.com/insights/2019-09/fatf-and-apg-publish-long-awaited-mutual-evaluation-report-on-hong-kong)). It built its sharing in stages:
1. In 2023 the **FINEST** pilot let banks share data on corporate accounts ([HKMA](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2023/06/20230620-5/)).
2. Police began feeding data from the **Scameter** fraud database into bank network analytics ([HKMA](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/news-and-media/press-releases/2025/04/20250410-7/)).
3. From **3 November 2025**, a statutory gateway covers **individual accounts**. Officials noted that about **90% of fraud mule accounts are individual** ([news.gov.hk](https://news.gov.hk/eng/2025/06/20250604/20250604_162550_541.html); [HK Gov](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/05/P2025090500211.htm)).

Since 2 February 2026, suspicious transaction reports go only through **STREAMS 2** ([IA/JFIU](https://www.ia.org.hk/en/supervision/antimoney_laundering/files/20251027_AML_Seminar_Eng_JFIU_MLEC.pdf)). The Stablecoins Ordinance took effect on 1 August 2025, and the first issuer licences went to HSBC and Anchorpoint on 10 April 2026 ([Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hkma-issues-first-stablecoin-issuer-licences/)).

### China, the UAE, Australia, India and Japan close different gaps

**Mainland China.** The revised **AML Law**, effective 1 January 2025, adds extraterritorial reach and explicit beneficial-ownership checks ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/chn/legal-updates/china-amends-its-anti-money-laundering-law)). A separate central-bank and market-regulator (PBOC/SAMR) BO filing regime started on 1 November 2024 ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/0bcfa5a3/pboc-releases-consultation-draft-on-beneficial)). Institutions report cash of **RMB 50,000** or more to CAMLMAC, the central bank's FIU ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/278d4460/china-financial-services-regulation-increased-supervision-of-large-sum-and-suspicious-transactions)). These moves answer a 2019 FATF report that faulted BO transparency and DNFBP coverage ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/Mer-china-2019.html)).

**UAE.** It went from grey-listing in March 2022 to removal in February 2024. FATF credited a new Executive Office for AML/CTF and a specialised court ([Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/insights/pc-fatf-takes-uae-greylist)). **Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025** took effect on 14 October 2025. It makes tax evasion an explicit predicate offence and sets corporate fines up to AED 100 million ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/10/uae-issues-new-aml-law)). It is the one jurisdiction in this set confirmed to file through UNODC's **goAML** platform ([Waystone](https://compliance.waystone.com/uaes-success-exiting-the-fatf-grey-list/)).

**Australia.** "Tranche 2" brought lawyers, accountants, real estate agents and dealers in precious metals and stones into the regime on **1 July 2026**, an estimated **80,000–90,000 new reporting entities** ([LexisNexis AU](https://www.lexisnexis.com/blogs/en-au/insights/austraia-aml-ctf-regime-whats-changed-and-whats-coming-in-2026)). The regulator, AUSTRAC, holds Australia's enforcement records:
- **Westpac, A$1.3 billion** for more than 23 million contraventions ([AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/our-recent-work/westpac-penalty-ordered));
- **CBA, A$700 million** ([AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-and-cba-agree-700m-penalty)).

**India.** FATF adopted India's report in June 2024 and placed it in **regular follow-up**, the better of the two follow-up tracks. FATF praised the shift from cash to digital payments but flagged slow prosecutions under India's money-laundering statute (PMLA) between 2014 and 2022 ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!international/india-now-in-regular-follow-up-category-of-countries-under-fatf-what-it-means-enn24062806665)).

**Japan.** After its 2021 report, Japan upgraded every remaining Partially Compliant rating by October 2024 and now holds **4 Compliant and 35 Largely Compliant** ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Mutualevaluations/japan-fur-2024.html)).

### Where the regimes converge and diverge

Three themes run across these jurisdictions:
- **Gatekeepers are being pulled in.** Australia's Tranche 2, Singapore's service-provider law and the UK's FCA consolidation all extend AML duties to professionals beyond banks.
- **Sharing gateways are triggered by suspicion.** Every durable bank-to-bank scheme requires a red flag or suspicion before data flows.
- **Beneficial ownership is moving in opposite directions.** The US is retreating while the UK and EU tighten.

Reporting technology also splits by size. goAML runs in roughly **60 to 75 FIUs** ([UNODC](https://unitego.un.org/goportfolio/node/746); [FIU-Nederland](https://www.fiu-nederland.nl/en/?p=4829)). High-volume FIUs such as the US, UK, Hong Kong and India run their own systems.

## The United States runs the largest and most rule-bound AML machine

The US matters most for AML for two reasons. Its dollar-clearing system makes US law reach banks worldwide, and its enforcement sets the global benchmark for penalties. It is also the jurisdiction where 2025–2026 changed the most.

### The Bank Secrecy Act still runs on fixed dollar triggers

The Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) is built on bright lines:
- A bank files a **currency transaction report (CTR)** for cash over **$10,000** in a business day.
- It files a **SAR** for suspicious activity of at least **$5,000** where a suspect is known, or **$25,000** where none is. The $25,000 figure comes from the banking agencies' own rules.
- The SAR is due **30 days** after initial detection, extendable to 60 if no suspect is identified.
- Records are kept for five years.

([31 CFR 1020.320](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.320); [FinCEN SAR FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-10/SAR-FAQs-October-2025.pdf))

The $5,000 SAR threshold has not changed since 1992 ([America's Credit Unions](https://www.americascreditunions.org/news-media/news/updated-ctrsar-thresholds-would-be-targeted-commonsense-regulatory-reform)). **Structuring** cash to dodge the CTR is a crime in its own right under 31 USC 5324. It carries up to five years, or ten when aggravated ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5324)).

Unlike the UK, the US has **no consent mechanism**. A SAR is a pure report with a safe harbour from civil liability. Filing it neither blocks the transaction nor authorises it.

The Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020 promised a modernised regime. By October 2026 its rulemaking had mostly been delayed, narrowed or reversed:

| Corporate Transparency Act beneficial ownership reporting | **Ended for US companies.** Final rule of 11 August 2026; only foreign companies report ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/08/us-fincen-issues-final-rule-ending-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirement)) |

### FinCEN writes the rules while other agencies run the exams

FinCEN administers the BSA and holds overall enforcement authority, but it delegates examinations:
- the OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC and NCUA examine banks and credit unions;
- the SEC (and in practice FINRA) examine broker-dealers;
- the CFTC examines futures firms;
- the IRS examines money services businesses (MSBs) and casinos.

([31 CFR 1010.810](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.810)) State regulators form a parallel layer. In January 2025, 48 states jointly fined Block (Cash App) $80 million ([CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/newsroom/state-regulators-issue-80-million-penalty-block-inc-cash-app-bsaaml-violations)).

A bank's program must have five "pillars":
1. internal controls;
2. independent testing;
3. a designated BSA officer;
4. training;
5. risk-based customer due diligence, added in 2016.

([31 CFR 1020.210](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1020/subpart-B/section-1020.210))

Examiners follow the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual. They scope from the bank's own risk assessment and its independent test, check each pillar, then test transactions in higher-risk areas. Findings escalate in this order: a matter requiring attention (MRA), then a formal agreement, then a consent or cease-and-desist (C&D) order, then a civil money penalty.

Three changes in 2025–2026 narrowed exam scope without relaxing the underlying duties:
- The OCC let community-bank examiners **rely on independent testing and carry forward conclusions** on two pillars, for exams from 1 February 2026 ([OCC Bulletin 2025-37](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-37.html)).
- The OCC and FDIC codified a **ban on supervising for "reputation risk"**, effective 9 June 2026. The rule states that BSA/AML and OFAC obligations stay intact ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-06947/prohibition-on-the-use-of-reputation-risk-by-regulators)).
- The agencies reissued 15 guidance documents with reputation-risk references removed ([Cooley](https://finsights.cooley.com/agencies-strip-reputation-risk-from-interagency-supervisory-guidance/)).

The April 2026 program proposal would add two more limits. Banking agencies would have to give FinCEN **30 days' notice before significant supervisory actions**, and enforcement for "maintenance" failures would be limited to significant or systemic ones ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)).

### New York makes a named executive certify the monitoring engine

New York's **3 NYCRR Part 504** is the only US rule that requires a firm to run two named systems:
- a **transaction monitoring program**, which reviews transactions after execution;
- a **filtering program**, which screens payments against watch lists.

The board or a senior officer must file a compliance finding each **15 April** and keep supporting records for five years ([3 NYCRR 504.4](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-3/chapter-iii/subchapter-c/part-504/section-504-4); [504.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-3/chapter-iii/subchapter-c/part-504/section-504-1)). The New York regulator, NYDFS, enforces it.

The clearest case is the April 2025 **Block** order: a **$40 million** penalty plus an independent monitor. It recorded these failures ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/enforcement-discipline/ea20250410-block)):
- an alert backlog that grew **from about 18,000 in 2018 to over 169,000 by 2020**;
- SARs filed on average **129 days after the alert**, some more than a year later;
- a vendor system that for a period did not alert on Bitcoin transactions;
- customers not rescreened against updated OFAC lists.

Other Part 504-era cases include Coinbase (reported at $100 million, 2023), Robinhood Crypto ($30 million, 2022, partly for an improper certification) and Paxos ($48.5 million, 2025) ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/new-york-state-department-financial-services-and-coinbase-reach-100-million); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2022/08/NYDFS-Fines-Trading-Platform-for-BSA-AML-Transaction-Monitoring-and-Cybersecurity-Lapses); [Willkie](https://complianceconcourse.willkie.com/articles/nydfs-reaches-48-5-million-settlement-with-paxos-trust-company-to-resolve-aml-deficiencies/)). The pattern is consistent: backlog, slow alert-to-SAR cycle, and thresholds with no documented rationale.

### OFAC liability is strict, so screening and monitoring are different machines

The Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) runs sanctions programmes under emergency-powers statutes. Its civil liability needs no knowledge or intent. Under its Enforcement Guidelines, the base penalty depends on whether a case is "egregious" and whether the firm voluntarily self-disclosed ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/-/media/organize-archive/cgsh/files/publication-pdfs/ofac-publishes-new-economic-sanctions-enforcement-guidelines.pdf)):
- egregious and undisclosed: the statutory maximum;
- egregious and self-disclosed: half the maximum.

Entities **owned 50% or more by listed persons are blocked** even if not named ([Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/files/34423_OFACs_50_Percent_Rule_Penalty.pdf)).

2025 penalties totalled about **$262–266 million**, of which **GVA Capital's $216 million** made up roughly 81%. Interactive Brokers paid **$11.8 million** for 12,367 violations, partly from gaps in screening customer addresses and IP addresses ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/five-key-takeaways-from-2025-us-sanctions-enforcement); [GRC Report](https://www.grcreport.com/post/interactive-brokers-to-pay-11-8-million-for-sanctions-violations-across-multiple-jurisdictions-programs)).

The February 2025 designation of **eight cartels and gangs as foreign terrorist organisations** moved cartel-linked parties onto the sanctions (SDN) list. Serving them now risks **material-support liability** under 18 USC 2339B, which turns some formerly report-only typologies into strict-liability exposure ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/trump-administration-announces-designation-of-international-cartels); [Bracewell](https://bracewell.com/resources/trump-administration-efforts-to-eliminate-cartels-pose-heightened-risk-for-financial-institutions)).

The table below is a synthesis from the sources above. It shows why a training simulation should keep the two controls in separate queues.

| | Sanctions screening (OFAC) | AML monitoring (BSA) |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Before the transaction, real-time | After the transaction, retrospective |
| Logic | Name, entity and 50%-ownership matching; geography and IP | Behavioural patterns and scenarios |
| Outcome | Block or reject, then report to OFAC | Investigate; SAR within 30 or 60 days |
| Liability | Strict: one miss is a violation | Judged on whether the program is reasonably designed |

### Enforcement escalates from exam findings to guilty pleas

US enforcement comes in four layers:
- **Supervisory:** MRAs, formal agreements, cease-and-desist orders, civil money penalties, asset caps and growth restrictions.
- **FinCEN civil:** penalties, including against individuals.
- **DOJ criminal:** guilty pleas, deferred and non-prosecution agreements (DPAs and NPAs), monitorships and forfeiture.
- **State:** NYDFS and multistate actions.

The benchmark is **TD Bank** (October 2024). It was the first US bank to plead guilty to money-laundering conspiracy and paid about **$3 billion**, including a record **$1.3 billion** to FinCEN ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-assesses-record-13-billion-penalty-against-td-bank); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/td-bank-pleads-guilty-money-laundering-case-will-pay-3-billion-penalti-rcna174912)). What went wrong:
- It deliberately did not monitor domestic ACH transfers and most cheques, leaving **92% of volume, about $18.3 trillion, unmonitored**.
- Executives held to a "**flat cost paradigm**" for the compliance budget.
- Launderers bribed staff with about $57,000 in gift cards.

([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/10/10/td-bank-money-laundering-charges-doj); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/10/feds-slap-td-bank-with-3-1-billion-in-fines-for-money-laundering))

Remedies include a **$434 billion asset cap** and an independent monitor. Sources say three or four years.

Smaller cases since then add other failure patterns:

Total US AML and sanctions penalties fell from **$3.55 billion in 2024 to about $940 million in 2025** ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/economic-sanctions-and-anti-money-laundering-developments-2025-year-in-review)). Individuals can be liable too:
- **Thomas Haider**, MoneyGram's former chief compliance officer, settled for $250,000 and a three-year industry bar ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/former-chief-compliance-officer-can-be-held-personally-liable-for-aml-violations)).
- **Michael LaFontaine**, a former U.S. Bank executive, paid $450,000 for understaffing compliance and capping SAR alerts ([Post & Schell](https://postschell.com/insights/fincen-imposes-civil-money-penalty-former-bank-officer-failing-adequately)).

DOJ's **May 2025 Galeotti memo** promised a near-automatic **declination** for companies that self-report, cooperate and remediate. It also capped resolution terms at three years and narrowed monitor use ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/05/us-doj-unveils-white-collar-enforcement-priorities)).

### FinCEN's statistics show heavy filing and thin measurable feedback

| Report type | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SARs | 4.3m | 4.6m | 4.7m | **4.8m** |
| CTRs | 20.6m | 20.9m | 20.5m | **21.5m** |

([FinCEN FY2025 Year in Review](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-05/FinCEN-Year-in-Review-2025.pdf))

About **335,000 filers** file these reports, and the **top ten filers submit about 45% of SARs** ([FinCEN FY2024](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-08/FinCEN-Infographic-Public-2025-508.pdf)).

FinCEN reports how often law enforcement uses the data, not how often a SAR leads to a case. In FY2025, **88.6% of IRS Criminal Investigation cases had a BSA filing on the primary subject**, and 11.7% of its investigations started from one ([FinCEN FY2025](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-05/FinCEN-Year-in-Review-2025.pdf)). FBI programmes linked between 16% and 40% of their investigations to BSA reports in FY2024. **432 agencies** ran 2.3 million searches of the data ([FinCEN FY2024](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-08/FinCEN-Infographic-Public-2025-508.pdf)).

These numbers support two readings. BSA data is clearly useful as background intelligence. But most of the 4.8 million SARs a year are never individually acted on. Because filing is so concentrated, a handful of large banks' defensive-filing policies largely set the national SAR picture.

### A US bank turns an alert into a SAR through a tiered funnel

**Onboarding.** The customer identification program (CIP) rule requires four data points before account opening: name, date of birth, address and an identification number. These are verified by documents or by non-documentary checks, and the customer is checked against government lists ([31 CFR 1020.220](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.220)). For a company, the CDD Rule requires identifying **up to four individuals who own 25% or more, plus one control person** ([31 CFR 1010.230](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1010.230)).

With the corporate registry gone for domestic companies, this bank-collected self-certification is now the **main US beneficial-ownership control**. FinCEN's February 2026 relief means it is collected once, at first account opening, and refreshed only when something triggers a review ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/fincen-issues-exceptive-relief-from-cdd-rule-requirements)). Customer risk ratings, EDD and periodic-review cycles are left to the bank's risk-based judgement.

Onboarding data feeds detection directly. The first red flags in FinCEN's 2025 advisory on Chinese money-laundering networks are onboarding facts, such as a "student" or "housewife" with unexplained wealth ([FinCEN FIN-2025-A003](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-08/FinCEN-Advisory-CMLN-508.pdf)).

**Alert handling.** Vendor descriptions of the funnel are consistent ([Sanction Scanner](https://www.sanctionscanner.com/blog/aml-alert-investigation-workflow-from-alert-to-sar-decision-1376)):
- **L1 analysts** clear or escalate an alert in "five to ten minutes".
- **L2 investigators** spend **2–8 hours** building a case.
- A SAR decision-maker or committee chaired by the BSA officer decides whether to file.

L1 and L2 work is widely offshored. Deutsche Bank, for example, advertises L2 investigation roles in its "India Offshore Hub" ([ACAMS careers](https://careers.acams.org/job/ipb-aml-investigations-asia-pacific-vp/81014434)). Managed services usually stop at a recommendation, because the bank and its BSA officer cannot outsource accountability.

LexisNexis/Forrester put US and Canadian financial-crime compliance at **$61 billion a year**, with labour the largest cost. This is an industry-sponsored figure with only 160 US/Canada respondents ([LexisNexis](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/about-us/press-room/press-release/20240221-true-cost-of-compliance-us-ca)).

**The SAR.** The October 2025 interagency FAQs dropped three entrenched habits ([FinCEN SAR FAQs](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-10/SAR-FAQs-October-2025.pdf)):
1. **Near-$10,000 cash is not by itself a reason to file.** A SAR is needed only where evasion is suspected.
2. **The 90-day continuing-activity review is optional.** It had been a "rule of thumb" that hardened into a perceived requirement.
3. Banks face **no expectation to document decisions not to file**.

For banks that keep the continuing-activity cycle, the clock runs as follows:

| Day | Suspect identified | No suspect |
|---|---|---|
| Initial detection | 0 | 0 |
| Initial SAR due | 30 | 60 |
| Continuing SAR due | 150 | 180 |

The narrative is the only free-text field. FinCEN expects it to cover **who, what, when, where, why and how** ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/suggestions-addressing-common-errors-noted-suspicious)). SARs are filed as Form 111 through BSA E-Filing ([FinCEN](https://bsaefiling.fincen.treas.gov/docs/FinCENSARElectronicFilingRequirements.pdf)). Filers tag typologies with **advisory key terms** in field 2 and the narrative, for example `FIN-2023-PIGBUTCHERING` or `CMLN-2025-A003`. FinCEN uses these tags to route SARs to its analysts.

SARs are confidential. Revealing that one exists is prohibited, which is the US version of a "tipping-off" ban, and filers have a civil safe harbour ([31 CFR 1020.320](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/1020.320)).

**CTRs** aggregate cash in and cash out separately across branches per business day. Two classes of customer can be exempted ([FinCEN FIN-2012-G003](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/shared/FIN-2012-G003.pdf)):
- Phase I: banks, government bodies and listed companies.
- Phase II: non-listed businesses and payroll customers, after two months and five reportable transactions a year.

### Sponsor banks own the BSA risk of customers they never see

US technology splits by institution size. The large-bank tier below is industry knowledge rather than a sourced finding:
- **Large banks** run enterprise suites.
- **Community banks and credit unions** cluster on cloud platforms. Nasdaq paid **$2.75 billion for Verafin** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/11/19/nasdaq-to-buy-financial-fraud-detection-firm-verafin-for-2point75-billion.html)), and Abrigo serves over 2,400 institutions ([Abrigo](https://www.abrigo.com/success-stories/credit-union-centralizes-aml-risk-management-with-abrigo-bam/)).
- **Fintechs** assemble API stacks: Alloy, Persona or Socure for identity checks, Sardine for device signals, Unit21 or Hummingbird for monitoring and case management ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/f7d5e122-5bb4-4c21-ae0a-d90194d3202e/)).

Banking-as-a-service (BaaS) splits AML duties in two. The fintech owns the customer relationship and data. Its **sponsor bank** holds the legal BSA obligation. Regulators punished that split heavily:
- In **2023, BaaS banks accounted for 13.5% of severe federal bank enforcement actions** ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2024/1/small-group-of-banking-as-a-service-banks-logs-big-number-of-enforcement-actions-80067110)).
- Consent orders against Cross River, Blue Ridge, Lineage, Sutton, Piermont and Evolve required **regulator non-objection before taking on new fintech partners** ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/federal-reserve-synapse-partner-evolve-enforcement-action-aml-risk-fintech-baas-compliance/719027/); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2023/05/cross-river-bank-enters-consent-order-with-fdic-over-fair-lending-compliance-practices)).
- The 2024 collapse of middleware firm **Synapse** left an estimated **$65–95 million gap** between its ledgers and the funds actually held at partner banks ([American Banker](https://americanbanker.com/news/evolve-bank-says-it-will-start-returning-money-to-synapse-end-users)). The ledger itself turned out to be a control.

Vendors now sell "sponsor oversight" as a product. In March 2026 Unit21 announced a partnership giving sponsor banks shared visibility of their fintech partners' alerts ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260323835434/en/Unit21-Announces-Partnership-to-Power-AI-Driven-AML-Workflows-and-Sponsor-Bank-Oversight-for-BaaS-Programs)).

On AI, US regulators sound permissive but have issued no binding rules. The April 2026 program proposal says responsible experimentation adds no supervisory risk "solely from use of the technology" ([Napier](https://www.napier.ai/post/fincen-aml-ai-effectiveness)). The same month, the revised model-risk guidance (SR 26-2) placed **generative and agentic AI outside its scope** ([Baker Tilly](https://www.bakertilly.com/insights/updated-interagency-guidance-on-model-risk-management)).

### US typologies now read like a national-security briefing

FinCEN's recent products are dominated by cartels, fraud and sanctions evasion. Each follows the same pattern: a typology narrative, red flags grouped as behavioural, financial and technical, and a SAR key term. That structure maps directly onto a rules-first case script.

### 2025–2026 policy: lighter program burden, sharper targeted weapons

The US direction has two halves.

**Program burden went down:**
- The corporate beneficial-ownership registry was ended for US companies.
- The SAR FAQs retired defensive filing habits.
- Supervising for reputation risk was banned.
- The April 2026 program proposal promises not to second-guess reasonable resource decisions ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)).
- **Executive Order 14331** (August 2025) told regulators to end "politicized or unlawful debanking" and to require "individualized, objective, and risk-based" decisions on customer exits ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/president-trump-signs-debanking-executive-order)).
- In December 2025 the OCC reported preliminary findings that the **nine largest banks** had restricted lawful sectors such as firearms and oil and gas ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2025/nr-occ-2025-123.html)).

**Targeted tools got sharper:**
- **Cartel FTO designations.**
- The first **FEND Off Fentanyl Act orders** (June 2025), cutting off three Mexican institutions: CIBanco, Intercam and Vector ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/30/2025-11993/imposition-of-special-measure-prohibiting-certain-transmittals-of-funds-involving-cibanco-sa)).
- A **section 311 cutoff of Cambodia's Huione Group** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/16/2025-19571/imposition-of-special-measure-regarding-huione-group-as-a-foreign-financial-institution-of-primary)).
- Fraud task forces, and 314(b) guidance that treats fraud as a basis for bank-to-bank sharing ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)).

FinCEN's FY2026 budget justification frames its mission around the "total elimination of international cartels" and "maximum pressure" on Iran ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/266/11.-FinCEN-FY-2026-CJ.pdf)). FinCEN committed in 2021 to update its National AML/CFT Priorities at least every four years. No update has been found, so the 2021 list remains formally in force ([ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2021/06/fincen-issues-national-anti-money-laundering-priorities)).

This leaves banks caught between two pressures. They are pushed not to exit customers on reputational grounds, yet remain strictly liable for sanctions breaches and exposed to material-support liability. The safe path is a documented, individual, financial-crime rationale for every exit.

## AML software fails at the data plumbing, and supervisors now test there

### The stack is a pipeline, and the leaks happen between stages

A typical bank AML stack runs in this order:
1. Customer data feeds a **customer risk rating**.
2. **Name screening** checks sanctions, politically exposed persons (PEP) and adverse-media lists, while **payment screening** checks transactions before they execute.
3. **Transaction monitoring** reviews activity after it executes.
4. Alerts go to **case management** for L1 triage and L2 investigation.
5. A SAR or STR is filed with the FIU, and the outcome feeds back into the risk rating.

This pipeline is our synthesis; the Part 504 and Wolfsberg definitions anchor it ([3 NYCRR 504.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-3/chapter-iii/subchapter-c/part-504/section-504-1); [Wolfsberg](https://wolfsberg-group.org/resources/168)).

The biggest recent failures all happened at the joints between stages, not in the detection logic:

| Case | Where the pipe leaked | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| TD Bank (US, 2024) | ACH and most cheque feeds deliberately excluded | 92% of volume, ~$18.3tn ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/10/10/td-bank-money-laundering-charges-doj)) |
| Metro Bank (UK, 2024) | Feed error: transactions on account-opening day never reached monitoring | 60m+ transactions, £51bn+; junior staff warnings in 2017–18 unheeded ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-metro-bank-16m-financial-crime-failings)) |
| Starling (UK, 2024) | Screening configured against only UK-linked designations | 39 of 3,088 names ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-starling-bank-failings-financial-crime-systems-and-controls)) |
| CBA (Australia, 2018) | 2012 software update stopped deposit machines generating threshold reports | ~53,000 contraventions ([AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/news-and-media/media-release/austrac-and-cba-agree-700m-penalty)) |
| Block (US, 2025) | Vendor system not alerting on Bitcoin; no OFAC rescreening | 169,000-alert backlog ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/enforcement-discipline/ea20250410-block)) |

The widely quoted claim that **90–95% of rules-based alerts are false positives** is an industry rule of thumb traced to an unlocated PwC analysis. Alert-to-SAR conversion rates of 1–5% come from vendor content that labels itself "illustrative" ([FluxForce](https://www.fluxforce.ai/statistics/false-positive-rates-transaction-monitoring)). We found no peer-reviewed measurement of either.

### A few incumbents, a layer of data providers and a wave of ML specialists

The vendor market has three layers:
- **Enterprise suites** win on breadth. Chartis's 2025 RiskTech100 placed Moody's, SAS, Oracle, FIS and Nasdaq in its overall top five. Oracle won the AML category, Moody's the data category and Feedzai enterprise fraud ([Chartis](https://www.chartis-research.com/risk-tech100/winners)).
- **Data providers**, such as Moody's, LSEG and Dow Jones, supply the sanctions, PEP and adverse-media reference data.
- **Specialists** win on machine learning and network analytics. Quantexa reports a Chartis category lead in AML transaction monitoring for 2026 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/24/3368811/0/de/quantexa-erh%C3%A4lt-von-chartis-research-h%C3%B6chste-auszeichnungen-in-den-bereichen-aml-transaktions%C3%BCberwachung-und-im-rahmen-der-risktech100.html)). In 2025 Chartis created a "Financial Crime Co-pilot" category, which Lucinity won, making GenAI investigator assistance a product segment of its own.

Visa's acquisition of Featurespace (December 2024) shows payment networks absorbing fraud and AML analytics ([Visa](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2024/Visa-Completes-Acquisition-of-Featurespace/default.aspx)). Market-size estimates vary about threefold with definition, from roughly $2.8 billion to $4.1 billion for 2024–2025 ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/anti-money-laundering-software-market); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250723311835/en)).

### Data sharing works only when a trigger opens the gate

| Gateway | Jurisdiction | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 314(b) | US | Registered institutions; suspicion. 7,200+ registered in FY2025 ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-05/FinCEN-Year-in-Review-2025.pdf)) |

The counter-example is the Netherlands. **TMNL**, the five Dutch banks' pooled transaction-monitoring venture, was wound down in 2024. EU law did not allow jointly monitoring customers without a specific cause, and that was the venture's whole premise ([Accountant.nl](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2024/7/banken-herzien-gezamenlijk-antiwitwasproject-om-nieuwe-wetgeving/)).

Privacy-enhancing technology pilots report large gains, but only on **synthetic data**:
- BIS **Project Aurora** concluded that collaborative monitoring beats siloed monitoring ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/othp66.htm)).
- **Swift's** federated-learning trial with 13 banks reported models "twice as effective" as single-bank ones ([Swift](https://swift.com/news-events/press-releases/swift-launch-ai-powered-fraud-defence-enhance-cross-border-payments)).

### Generative AI assists, humans decide, and supervision lags

The most visible deployment is the **FIS–Anthropic Financial Crimes AI Agent**, announced on 4 May 2026 and in development with BMO and Amalgamated Bank. FIS claims it will compress investigations "from hours to minutes", while "investigators remain in control of every decision" ([FIS](https://www.fisglobal.com/about-us/media-room/press-release/2026/fis-brings-agentic-ai-to-banking-with-anthropic-starting-with-financial-crimes)). That is a vendor claim. We found no independent evaluation of GenAI triage or narrative drafting.

Regulators are moving at different speeds:
- **Hong Kong** is the most forward. In September 2024 the HKMA told banks to study using AI for monitoring, with feasibility studies due by March 2025, and runs a GenAI sandbox that includes an STR use case ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/hong-kong-monetary-authority-guidance-on-gen-ai-use-in-customer-facing-applications-and-use-of-ai-in-ml-tf/); [Global Government Finance](https://www.globalgovernmentfinance.com/hkma-generative-ai-sandbox-first-projects/)).
- **Singapore**'s MAS consulted on AI risk-management guidelines that leave rule-only tools outside the AI definition ([Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/knowledge/publications/alerts-newsletters-and-guides/2025/november/27/mas-consults-on-new-ai-risk-management-guidelines-for-financial-institutions)).
- **Industry**: the Wolfsberg Group's 2025 guidance on moving from rules to machine learning rests on three pillars: validation during the transition, balancing model risk against financial-crime risk, and explainability ([Wolfsberg](https://wolfsberg-group.org/resources/202)).

### What supervisors actually test in AML software

US supervision of AML software rests on model-risk guidance. **SR 21-8** (2021) applied that guidance to BSA systems and allowed lighter, change-focused validation for routine threshold updates ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/SR2108.htm)). **SR 26-2** (April 2026) replaced it. It scales oversight to materiality and **excludes deterministic rule-based processes and GenAI/agentic AI** from the definition of a "model" ([Abrigo](https://www.abrigo.com/blog/model-risk-management-what-sr-26-2-means-for-financial-institutions/)). Read with the secondary summaries, the result is that classic threshold scenarios may now be governed as program controls rather than models, while ML scores stay in model-risk scope. We have not verified this against the Fed's primary text.

Enforcement shows supervisors testing four things:
- **completeness and lineage**: does every product and feed reach the engine? (TD, Metro)
- **configuration and list integrity** (Starling)
- **scenario coverage and tuning against the risk assessment** (NYDFS orders)
- **truthful attestation** (Part 504)

## Crypto applies the same standard to a faster, more traceable system

### R.15 is on the books almost everywhere and fully met almost nowhere

FATF Recommendation 15 applies the full standard to virtual assets and virtual asset service providers (VASPs): licensing or registration, supervision, CDD, suspicious-transaction reporting, and the R.16 **travel rule**. The travel rule requires originator and beneficiary data to accompany transfers between VASPs.

FATF's seventh targeted update (July 2026) found only **one of about 149 jurisdictions fully compliant**. 34% were largely compliant, up from 29%. **83% now have travel-rule legislation**, but enforcement lags ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/news/targeted-updated-va-vasps-2026.html); [21 Analytics](https://www.21analytics.co/blog/2026-fatf-targeted-update-part1/)). The risks FATF flags are:
- industrialised fraud;
- stablecoin misuse;
- peer-to-peer transfers through unhosted (self-custody) wallets;
- offshore VASPs;
- decentralised finance (DeFi).

Thresholds diverge sharply:

| Jurisdiction | Travel-rule threshold |
|---|---|
| EU | None, since 30 Dec 2024 ([Joinble](https://joinble.io/en/blog/mica-travel-rule-july-2026-casp-compliance)) |
| US | $3,000 ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/blockchain-basics/what-is-the-travel-rule)) |
| Singapore | All transfers; fuller data above S$1,500 ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide)) |
| Hong Kong | Names and accounts below HK$8,000; address or ID above ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-travel-rule-compliance-guide)) |
| Dubai (VARA) | Above AED 3,500 ([VARA](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/node/146)) |
| Japan | None, since June 2023 ([Sygna](https://www.sygna.io/?p=7815)) |

IVMS101 is the shared data model. The transport layer remains split between closed networks such as Coinbase's TRUST and open protocols such as TRP ([21 Analytics](https://www.21analytics.ch/blog/trust-and-trp-compared)). The "**sunrise problem**" is the result: VASPs in live jurisdictions must still deal with counterparties where the rule is not yet in force ([21 Analytics](https://www.21analytics.ch/blog/2025-fatf-targeted-update-summarised)).

### Blockchain analytics produce probabilistic opinions, not facts

Analytics firms such as Chainalysis, Elliptic and TRM group addresses into clusters believed to belong to one entity. The main heuristic is **common-input ownership**: addresses spent together in one transaction are presumed to share an owner. Firms then attach off-chain tags from exchanges, seizures and open sources ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/glossary/common-input-heuristic); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09979)). Clusters carry confidence levels and can wrongly merge unrelated actors ([Sardine](https://www.sardine.ai/learn/wallet-clustering)). CoinJoin breaks the core heuristic, and privacy coins and cross-chain bridges degrade tracing further. Compliance therefore concentrates at the fiat on- and off-ramps.

The February 2025 **Bybit** hack, about **$1.5 billion** attributed by the FBI to North Korea, shows how fast laundering moves. Hundreds of millions passed through the THORChain bridge within days ([The Record](https://therecord.media/fbi-bybit-laundering-crypto-warning); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/343855/bybit-hackers-half-the-stolen-eth-bitcoin-fbi-confirms-lazarus)). Analytics also delivered the **Prince Group** case: a civil forfeiture complaint against **127,271 BTC (about $15 billion)** held in 25 unhosted wallets, the largest forfeiture action in DOJ history ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/operation-prince-inside-the-global-effort-that-led-to-the-largest-forfeiture-in-us-history)).

### Enforcement swung from platform liability to bad-actor targeting

The 2023–2025 resolutions set records:
- **Binance**: about $4.3 billion, including FinCEN's $3.4 billion and OFAC's $968 million ([Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102ita1/binance-and-founder-changpeng-zhao-plead-guilty-and-pay-more-than-4-billion-in-p/)).
- **OKX**: more than $504 million ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/regulations-compliance/okx-resolves-us-doj-investigation-agrees-to-pay-around-504m-in-penalties)).
- **KuCoin**: about $297 million ([BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/cryptocurrency/kucoin-to-pay-nearly-300-million-in-penalties-after-guilty-plea)).

Then US policy turned:
- DOJ's April 2025 "**Ending Regulation by Prosecution**" memo disbanded the crypto enforcement team. It told prosecutors to stop charging platforms for their users' acts and to bring licensing or BSA charges only for wilful violations ([Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/doj-announces-shift-in-crypto-enforcement-and-end-of-regulation-by-prosecution)).
- OFAC **delisted Tornado Cash** in March 2025, after the Fifth Circuit held that immutable smart contracts are not "property" ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/international-compliance-blog/treasury-department-delists-tornado-cash-following-the-fifth-circuits-decision.html)). Developer Roman Storm's retrial is set for April 2027 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/376577/roman-storms-tornado-cash-retrial-pushed-to-april-2027)).
- Binance founder Changpeng Zhao was **pardoned** on 23 October 2025 ([Reuters via Citizen Digital](https://www.citizen.digital/news/trump-pardons-convicted-binance-founder-zhao-white-house-says-n371838)).

At the same time, the **GENIUS Act** made payment-stablecoin issuers BSA financial institutions. They must have the technical ability to freeze, burn or block tokens on lawful order ([Senate Banking](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/majority/fact-sheet-the-genius-act-bolsters-national-security)). Implementing rules were proposed in April 2026 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260420-treasury-announces-proposed-rule-to-implement-the-genius-acts-requirements-to-counter-illicit-finance)).

Elsewhere, Asia-Pacific and Gulf hubs converge on "license or leave":
- **EU**: MiCA's transition period ended on 1 July 2026 ([AGP Law](https://www.agplaw.com/?p=32785)).
- **Singapore**: from 30 June 2025, firms serving only overseas customers need a licence that MAS "will generally not issue" ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/sgp/legal-updates/singapore-s-june-30-hard-deadline-for-crypto-firms-serving-overseas-customers)).
- **Hong Kong and Dubai**: run their own licensing regimes.

### Illicit crypto volume hit a record, driven by sanctions evasion on stablecoins

Vendor estimates put 2025 illicit inflows at a record **$154–158 billion**, still around 1% of on-chain activity:
- Chainalysis found that **stablecoins carried 84%** of illicit volume ([GN Crypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/chainalysis-crypto-crime-2026-record-illicit-volume/)).
- TRM found **sanctions-related flows made up 86%** of illicit volume, nearly half of it through the Russian rouble stablecoin A7A5 ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/01/258852-cryptocurrency-ecosystem-saw-major-spike-in-illicit-activities-totaling-158b-report-reveals/)).

These are industry estimates, sensitive to methodology changes and to which entities the vendor labels as sanctioned. They suggest the 2025 jump reflects sanctions evasion more than a proportional rise in fraud. That explains the policy pivot toward issuers: their freeze powers and AML programmes are now central levers.

## Effectiveness is low, costly and hard to measure, and de-risking is its side effect

### The same five failures recur in every major case

Across two decades of landmark cases, the same root causes repeat:

| Failure pattern | Cases |
|---|---|
| **Coverage gaps** | TD, Metro, Starling, CBA |
| **Backlogs** | ABN AMRO's alert backlog ([OM](https://www.prosecutionservice.nl/latest/news/2021/04/19/abn-amro-pays-eur-480-million-on-account-of-serious-shortcomings-in-money-laundering-prevention)); Commerzbank's **2,226 overdue KYC refreshes** ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-commerzbank-london-37805400-over-anti-money-laundering-failures)); Block |
| **Growth outrunning controls** | Monzo, whose customers grew from about 600,000 to 5.8 million ([Linklaters](https://financialregulation.linklaters.com/post/102ksp6/fca-fines-monzo-21m-for-failing-to-ensure-its-financial-crime-systems-and-contro)); Starling's 54,000 accounts opened in breach of a voluntary restriction; TD's flat budget |
| **Overridden or corrupted front-line decisions** | TD gift cards; EagleBank executives; Commerzbank's misused exceptions process |
| **High-risk flows treated as low-risk** | **Danske Estonia**'s non-resident portfolio, about $160 billion through US banks ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/danske-bank-pleads-guilty-fraud-us-banks-multi-billion-dollar-scheme-access-us)); HSBC's $881 million in cartel proceeds ([CNN](https://money.cnn.com/2012/12/10/news/companies/hsbc-money-laundering/index.html)) |

Westpac adds a sixth lesson, about **partial review**. It flagged one account of a known child-exploitation offender but did not review his other accounts ([ACFCS](https://www.acfcs.org/after-months-of-court-battles-westpac-settles-with-austrac-agrees-to-pay-1-3-billion-for-millions-of-aml-failings-ties-to-child-exploitation-network)).

Penalties are now explicitly calibrated against under-spending. ABN AMRO's €480 million included **€180 million disgorgement of compliance costs it had saved**. Regulators priced under-investment as a gain to be confiscated.

### Somewhere between 0.1% and 1% of criminal money is recovered

Independent researchers converge on low numbers but measure different things:
- Ronald Pol estimates AML's impact on criminal finances at **about 0.1% or less** and calls AML perhaps "the world's least effective policy experiment" ([Policy Design and Practice](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/25741292.2020.1725366)).
- Europol found **2.2% of EU criminal proceeds provisionally seized and 1.1% finally confiscated** ([Europol](https://www.europol.europa.eu/newsroom/news/does-crime-still-pay)).
- FATF's own 2022 stocktake found only **19% of about 120 assessed countries** reached high or substantial effectiveness on confiscation, and criticised "tick-box" adoption of laws ([FATF](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/reports/Report-on-the-State-of-Effectiveness-Compliance-with-FATF-Standards.pdf.coredownload.pdf)).
- Halliday, Levi and Reuter argue that FATF's objectives are too vague to evaluate, and that no credible evidence links formal compliance to less crime ([Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/88168/1/Report_Global%20Surveillance%20of%20Dirty%20Money%201.30.2014.pdf)).
- Older RUSI-cited UK data found only **about 6.3% of SARs** prompted further action ([RUSI](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/commentary/suspicious-activity-reports-regime-information-sharing-heart-tackling-financial-crime)).

The costs are large but come from industry-sponsored studies: **$206.1 billion** globally (2023) and $61 billion in the US and Canada ([LexisNexis](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/global/en/about-us/press-room/press-release/20230926-global-financial-crime-compliance-costs)).

The fair counter-argument is that confiscation ratios understate the regime's value. Financial intelligence supports investigations whose results show up elsewhere: 88.6% of IRS-CI cases had a BSA filing on the primary subject, the UK denied suspects £382.6 million through DAML refusals, and the Prince Group forfeiture traced back to financial data. Both the cost and the benefit numbers are soft. The defensible position is that AML works better as an intelligence and deterrence system than as a recovery system, and that it measures its own outputs (filings, ratings) far better than its outcomes.

### De-risking is the regime's most visible side effect, and now a political issue

When gathering evidence costs more than a customer is worth, banks exit the customer rather than investigate ([IEA](https://iea.org.uk/publications/debanked-the-economic-and-social-consequences-of-anti-money-laundering-regulation/)). FATF now says plainly that "**wholesale de-risking is contrary to the RBA**" (the risk-based approach), and revised R.1 in 2025 for that reason ([FATF guidance](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/content/dam/fatf-gafi/guidance/Guidance-Financial-Inclusion%20-Anti-Money-Laundering-Terrorist-Financing-Measures.pdf.coredownload.pdf)).

Politics has turned the issue around:
- **UK**: Coutts' 2023 closure of Nigel Farage's accounts led to the resignation of both the Coutts and NatWest chief executives. An FCA review found that financial-crime concerns, not political views, were the most common closure reason ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-09-19/fca-finds-no-evidence-banks-rejected-politicians-over-views)).
- **US**: EO 14331, the OCC's December 2025 findings and reported 2026 DOJ subpoenas have made unjustified exits a supervisory and possibly legal risk ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/mid-year-developments-in-anti-money-laundering-in-2026/)).

An exit is no longer a free, safe choice. It needs an individual rationale.

## What a TwinAML trainee simulation should model

TwinAML's contract already matches the regulatory consensus: rules decide, humans own the decision, overrides are stored as events, and any model explains only after rules fire. FIS describes its AI agent with the same split: the agent assists and "investigators remain in control of every decision". The SR 26-2 carve-out shows that no regulator yet blesses AI as the system of record.

A note on terms. The repository's `docs/comp3424-mapping.md` uses **Chan Level 1** for the script mirror and **Chan Level 2** for the training shadow. This brief frames them as an **L1 analyst** and **L2 investigator**, matching US industry tiers. The two readings fit together:
- The Level 1 mirror holds the case world: customer, signals, accounts, rules.
- The Level 2 shadow records what an L1 analyst and an L2 investigator decide, as replayable events.
- The event log is the "threading" record.

The design recommendations below follow from the findings. Each row links an evidence base to a state variable or event the twin can carry.

| What to model | Why (evidence) | Twin mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| **Two separate queues: screening and monitoring** | OFAC is strict-liability, pre-transaction, and ends in a block. AML is retrospective and ends in a SAR (Starling, Block, Interactive Brokers) | `screening_hit` blocks the transaction at once; a missed true match fires a rule violation regardless of intent. Monitoring alerts enter a separate queue with a SAR clock |
| **The SAR clock** | 30 days from "initial detection", 60 with no suspect; Block averaged 129 days | State `sar.detected_at`, `sar.due_at`; a rule fires `SAR_LATE` if exceeded. The trainee must decide when detection happened |
| **Coverage-gap scenarios** | TD (92% unmonitored), Metro (account-opening-day feed), Starling (39 of 3,088), CBA (coding error) | A case where the alert queue is clean but a feed is missing. Reward the L2 investigator for asking for data lineage or noticing absent same-day activity |
| **Backlog pressure** | ABN AMRO, Commerzbank, Block's 169,000 alerts; the 90–95% false-positive heuristic | A queue mostly of false positives with a service-level timer. Score both handling time and true positives missed, so speed alone loses |
| **Link analysis discipline** | Westpac reviewed one account of a known offender; TD's nominee networks | When one account is flagged, a rule expects review of linked parties before closure, recorded as `LINKED_REVIEW_DONE` |
| **Narrative quality, not filing volume** | FinCEN's who/what/when/where/why/how; ~6% UK SAR actionability; defensive filing | Score the SAR draft on the 5 Ws plus how, linked parties and correct key term (`sar.key_terms[]`, e.g. `FIN-2023-PIGBUTCHERING`) |
| **Overrides and escalations as immutable events** | EagleBank executives, Commerzbank exceptions, Metro's unheeded junior staff | Every escalation, exception and senior override is an append-only event with rationale and approver. The existing `R1_OVERRIDDEN` pattern generalises here |
| **Exit decisions carry a cost** | FATF: wholesale de-risking contradicts the RBA; EO 14331 requires individualised rationale | "Exit customer" requires a rationale field and records an exclusion side-effect, not a free safe outcome |
| **Restrictions the business pushes against** | Starling and Monzo onboarding restrictions; TD asset cap; Wells Fargo non-objection | Scenario flag `under_restriction`. Onboarding a high-risk customer fires a breach rule even if the customer seems fine |
| **Policy versions** | Oct 2025 SAR FAQs retired near-$10k SARs and 90-day reviews; UK DAML vs US no-consent | Case scripts carry `policy_version` and `jurisdiction`. The same facts replayed under 2024 vs 2026 US rules, or US vs UK consent, give different required actions. This is threading made visible |
| **Partner-bank blindness** | BaaS consent orders; Synapse's $65–95m ledger gap; CFSB 2026 | A sponsor-bank case where end-customer data arrives late or incomplete, so the investigator must request it |
| **US typology library** | FinCEN advisories: behavioural, financial and technical red flags plus key terms | Script cases mirror advisory structure. Candidates: elder pig-butchering via home-equity loan to a crypto kiosk; CMLN "student" mule; border business with armoured-car cash; mobile-deposit washed cheque |
| **Crypto inputs as evidence, not verdicts** | Clustering is probabilistic; travel-rule thresholds diverge; IVMS101 fields | A vendor risk score enters as one input to a deterministic rule. Travel-rule fields are checked against the jurisdiction threshold (EU zero, US $3,000) |
| **AI after rules** | FIS–Anthropic "investigators remain in control"; SR 26-2 excludes GenAI | A language model may draft the narrative only after rules fire. Changing `disposition` requires a human override event |
| **STR as a structured object** | goAML is used by ~60–75 FIUs; FinCEN Form 111 fields | "File STR" emits a report object (subjects, activity type, key terms, narrative) shaped like goAML/FinCEN fields, so it can be validated |

In the funnel, the **L1 analyst** role is fast triage: clear or escalate, with a short documented reason. The **L2 investigator** role covers case building, link analysis, the SAR draft and the clock. A rule gate, or the instructor acting as BSA officer, takes the file/no-file decision. That keeps final accountability with a human, as in real banks.

Scoring should combine coverage, timeliness, narrative quality and the completeness of the audit trail. Enforcement shows that is what regulators examine. The `deepfake-001` case already fits this model:
- **R1**: liveness failures block onboarding unless overridden.
- **R2**: a deepfake signal plus third-party inflow requires reporting or enhanced review.

Adding the SAR clock, a coverage-gap variant and a jurisdiction toggle would bring it close to the real failure modes described here.

## Conclusion

The AML regime is not short of rules or reports. It is short of evidence that the reports reach the right criminals, and the institutions it relies on keep failing in mundane, observable ways. The most expensive failures of the past decade came from unmonitored data feeds, under-configured screening lists, budgets frozen against rising risk, and executives overruling compliance. Clever launderers beating good detection are a minor cause by comparison. So the frontier of "better AML" is mostly about process integrity: data lineage, backlog control, documented judgement and trigger-based sharing. Smarter models matter less. The 2025–2026 split reinforces this. The US is cutting program paperwork while making targeted national-security tools sharper. Europe and Asia are centralising supervision and legalising data sharing. In both places, the record that supervisors examine is the decision trail.

For TwinAML, the implication is sharper than "teach typologies". A training twin is valuable because it makes the regime's invisible failure modes visible: a missing feed, a clock running out, an override nobody challenged, an exit with no rationale. These are exactly the things an append-only event log captures. A rules-before-AI twin with human overrides stored as events is a credible miniature of how supervisors already judge real banks. The open uncertainties are the October 2026 FATF list changes, the final US program rule and the threshold bills. These belong in the twin as versioned policy parameters, not hard-coded truths.
