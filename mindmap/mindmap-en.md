# AML AI Simulation Training Platform

Mind map converted to Markdown (English).

## 1. Case Input

### 1.1 Deepfake executive video-conference fraud
Based on the Hong Kong case involving about HK$250 million. Fraudsters use AI to impersonate multiple senior executives in a video meeting and trick finance staff into transferring funds. Trainees practise that “seeing is not always believing” and verify payment instructions before acting.

### 1.2 Cryptocurrency mixers and mule-account laundering
Trainees follow peel chains through DeFi protocols and high-volume transfers. They learn to recognise mixer patterns (e.g. Tornado Cash–style mixing) and mule-account networks.

### 1.3 Synthetic identity fraud
Platform advantage: cases are grounded in public court documents, not invented fiction. A real legal basis makes training more credible to regulators and avoids content that feels detached from practice.

### 1.4 Core pain points and solutions
**Pain point:** Traditional AML training is often PDF multiple-choice. Institutions struggle to show regulators that staff can handle modern threats such as deepfakes, synthetic identities, and crypto laundering.

**Solution:** A closed loop of *real judgments → AI generation → interactive decisions → audit trail*, turning static training into immersive simulation.

## 2. AI Generation

### 2.1 AI script generation
Large language models read court judgments and generate training scripts, dialogue, decision trees, and trap points. When new cases or typologies appear, content can be refreshed without a full production team.

### 2.2 AI video synthesis
Integrates digital-human video (e.g. HeyGen) and voice synthesis (e.g. ElevenLabs) APIs to turn scripts into realistic fraud scenario videos. Cost is far lower than live filming, with many variants for different trainees.

## 3. Immersive Training

### 3.1 Branching decision simulation
Built with the Unity engine as a decision-based training room. While watching the AI fraud scenario, trainees decide in real time—e.g. approve the transfer or file a suspicious transaction report (STR). Each choice changes consequences and the next beat. Passive video becomes active decision practice, with every choice logged.

## 4. Output / Backend Platform

### 4.1 Learning behaviour data capture
The system records response time (how quickly a risk was spotted), trap / fraud recognition rate, vulnerability signals (where learners fail), and full decision paths—behavioural evidence traditional tests rarely capture.

### 4.2 Audit-ready compliance dashboard
Learning data is organised in formats useful to regulators (e.g. HKMA) and auditors, to evidence that the organisation ran innovative, effective AML training. Training shifts from a pure cost line to audit evidence.

## 5. Business Model and Monetisation

### 5.1 B2B / Enterprise / SaaS subscription
Annual fee by headcount. Targets banks, virtual banks, brokerages, and corporates. Indicative range about HK$10,000–50,000 per institution per year, for recurring revenue.

### 5.2 Custom regulatory modules for multi-market expansion
Higher-margin local case simulations (e.g. Hong Kong vs Singapore vs USA) aligned to local guidance. Complements SaaS and supports multi-year contracts.

### 5.3 Target customers
Corporates, large institutions, banks, virtual banks, securities firms, payment platforms, and crypto exchanges.

**Decision makers:** Compliance Officers, Anti-Financial Crime (AFC) teams, and HR.
